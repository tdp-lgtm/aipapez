## Sketch A (rank 1) — *Backed Coinage*: proliferation as investment in a scarce commons

**Thesis (P/Q/R).** I argue that the proliferation of named injustice concepts is good for philosophy and society when and because the coinages are *backed* — and that the flagship coinages are backed, so the practice as actually conducted is on balance good — against both the inflation critique (multiplying injustice-talk devalues it) and the abundance view (concepts are free, so coining needs no license), on the grounds that what injustice claims compete for is not deontic validity, which is not scarce, but uptake, which is; and a backed coinage *economizes* the scarce thing rather than spending it.

**The Backing Condition (the criterion the essay delivers).** A coinage "K-injustice" is backed iff it specifies (i) who is wronged and in what capacity, (ii) the distinctive wrong-making mechanism, and (iii) the shape of redress — what would count as responding to the claim (which may be structural/remedial, not a culprit).

**Premises / key steps.**
1. Injustice-talk is claim-making: to call something an injustice is not merely to classify but to assert a claim that demands response. (Speech-act premise; this is what distinguishes the injustice-suffix from neutral taxonomy.)
2. Deontic validity is not scarce: coining "ontic injustice" weakens no one's duty not to commit testimonial injustice. So the naive currency model — and the rights-inflation transposition — fails as stated. (Concession to the boosters.)
3. But uptake — recognition, sympathy, institutional response — is scarce, and injustice claims compete for it. Crucially, this scarcity is internal to the practice, not external friction: because claims demand response, a claim that predictably cannot get uptake is not merely useless — an ignored claim is a further wronging of the claimant. A practice that mints claims faster than uptake can follow manufactures a new tier of ignored claims. (The truth in the inflation worry, stated in the practice's own terms.)
4. Coinages differ in their uptake economics. A backed coinage works like a good diagnostic category: it bundles detection (you can now see the wrong), assignment (you know whose claim against whom or what), and remedy-shape (you know what responding looks like) — lowering the attention cost of every future instance. An unbacked coinage raises claims without enabling response: pure expenditure.
5. So the good/bad question resolves into the Backing Condition; judged by it, the practice's exemplars (testimonial, hermeneutical, ontic injustice) pass, and the condition both disciplines the practice going forward and explains the residual unease that fuels the critics.

**Corollary (from old C3).** The "-injustice" label borrows verdictive force; the Backing Condition says what earns it. Analysis without backing is fine philosophy — it just isn't entitled to the suffix yet.

**Distinction the essay mints.** Proliferation (coining new kinds) vs. creep (stretching an existing kind, Haslam's topic): critics run them together; their economics differ — creep degrades an existing concept's backing, proliferation adds concepts whose backing is assessable one by one.

**Anticipated main objection + reply direction.** Structural injustice: backing seems to demand assignable duty-bearers, which structural wrongs lack — so the condition would absurdly unlicense the most important coinages. Reply: clause (iii) demands a shape of redress, not a culprit; remedial/political responsibility satisfies it. Second objection: vacuity — if every coinage passes, the criterion is toothless; the essay must exhibit a genuine fail (constructed, plus a charitable borderline real case).

**The crux (solve first).** Premise 3: that uptake scarcity is normatively real and internal to justice-practice, against the abundance view. If 3 falls, the essay collapses into Sketch B; if it stands, the Backing Condition follows almost immediately.

**Key engagement.** Kitsik 2025 (attention as the stake — the closest neighbor; this essay goes beyond by making the stake normative and deriving a criterion); Haslam (creep, distinguished); rights-inflation literature (Alston, Griffin — the model corrected); Fricker (the practice's flagship); Jenkins (ontic injustice as test case); Young (remedial responsibility for the objection).

**Fit to venue.** One spine (the uptake-scarcity argument), a named criterion, a concession-rich dialectic against two rivals at once, current literature. Feasible in 6k. Contribution type: new argument + criterion.

## Sketch B (rank 2) — *Genus First*: the case against the practice as conducted

**Thesis (P/Q/R).** I argue that the proliferation is bad for philosophy in its current form, against the proliferation-as-progress view, on the grounds that "injustice" is a verdictive, not merely an explanatory, category: a new species-of-injustice claim borrows a verdict whose grounds must be supplied now, not deferred — yet the coinage genre standardly multiplies species while treating the genus (what makes any of them *injustice*) as an IOU.

**Premises / key steps.**
1. Species-multiplication is epistemically progressive only relative to a working genus (nosology progressed when diseases were individuated by pathology, not symptom clusters).
2. For explanatory kinds, genus-theory can be deferred (biology thrived before a settled species concept). For verdictive kinds it cannot: a verdict without grounds is an accusation on credit.
3. "K-injustice" coinages are verdictive: the suffix does normative work in every application.
4. The genre's actual practice defers the genus question (evidence: the standing debates over whether the flagship cases are *injustices* rather than harms, bad luck, or ordinary discrimination — the debate exists because the coinage papers left the genus open).
5. So the practice is structurally premature: not wrong to coin, but wrong to coin *in the verdictive register* ahead of the genus work. Verdict: bad for philosophy as conducted; the fix (a genus-first norm) is stated, making the stance constructive.

**Anticipated main objection + reply direction.** Over-demandingness: no genus theory of justice commands consensus, so the norm would silence the whole field. Reply: the norm demands a *stated* genus commitment per coinage (a "this is unjust because…" clause), not a consensus theory — cheap to meet, and the good coinages already meet it, which is exactly why they are good. But then the objector presses: if flagship coinages meet it, the practice isn't bad after all — and the essay must hold the line that the *genre's default* is still deferral (riskier empirical-ish claim about the literature).

**The crux.** Premise 2 — the explanatory/verdictive asymmetry in when theory can be deferred. Novel if defensible; the essay lives or dies on it.

**Fit to venue.** Sharp, contrarian, one crux. Risks: the genus territory is partially occupied for the epistemic case (Byskov 2021; Coady); premise 4 requires careful, fair literature handling to avoid strawman findings from referees. Contribution type: diagnosis + norm.

## Sketch C (rank 3) — *The Filter, Not the Flood*: a selection argument for proliferation

**Thesis (P/Q/R).** I argue that proliferation is good for philosophy and society, against creep-and-inflation critics, on the grounds that coinages are cheap, non-rival conjectures about the structure of wrongs, and the practice is a generate-and-filter search of normative space whose expected value is positive because payoffs are asymmetric: failed coinages die in the seminar room at near-zero social cost, while a successful one compounds indefinitely; every cost the critics cite is a cost of filter failure, not of proliferation.

**Premises / key steps.** (1) Concepts are non-rival; coining is cheap. (2) The value of the practice = expected value of the portfolio, not of the median coinage. (3) Historical base rate: the practice's wins (what the epistemic-injustice family did for medicine, law, AI governance) dwarf the losses of its failures. (4) The critics' evidence (dilution, misapplication) localizes to filter failures — uptake of concepts that should have died. (5) So the reformable target is the filter (review, replication of application, sunset norms), not the generative practice.

**Anticipated main objection + reply direction.** The filter doesn't exist: citation and career incentives preserve bad coinages, and public uptake is stickier than academic retraction — so filter failure *is* the practice's normal condition. Reply concedes partially and goes conditional: proliferation is good where filtering is live, and the essay specifies what keeps it live. Risk: the conclusion weakens to institution-dependence, and premise 3 is an empirical track-record claim philosophy referees will press.

**The crux.** Whether filter-failure costs can be cleanly separated from proliferation costs when the coiners can foresee the filter's weakness.

**Fit to venue.** Fresh mechanism, but the essay's center of gravity drifts toward social epistemology-cum-institutional design; hardest of the four to keep strictly philosophical. Contribution type: reframing + argument.

## Sketch D (rank 4) — *Claims Are Not Currency*: the anti-scarcity defense

**Thesis (P/Q/R).** I argue that the proliferation is straightforwardly good and the inflation frame is confused, against dilution critics, on the grounds that deontic force does not behave like currency — duties and claims do not weaken as they multiply — and the mechanism from "more names" to "less concern per name" is an unsupported psychological conjecture doing all the critics' work; what they call inflation is the sound of previously unclaimable wrongs entering the space of claims.

**Premises / key steps.** (1) The dilution thesis needs a scarce medium; candidates: validity (not scarce — duties don't dilute), meaning (concepts individuate finely without loss), attention (scarce, but allocation tracks salience and power, not concept-count). (2) Each candidate fails, so the inflation critique has no mechanism. (3) Meanwhile the recognition gains are concrete and documented. Verdict: good, without conditions.

**Anticipated main objection + reply direction.** Sketch A's premise 3: attention scarcity is internal to claim-making because ignored claims are further wrongings. The reply must show that the ignored-claim cost falls on failures of *institutions*, not of *coining* — which drifts toward Sketch C's conditional. That drift is the sketch's weakness: under pressure it converges on A or C.

**The crux.** Whether any scarce medium survives scrutiny. (Same crux as A, opposite verdict.)

**Fit to venue.** Cleanest prose-line of the four, but dominated: A contains D's insight (validity non-scarcity) as a concession and still delivers a criterion. Contribution type: rebuttal.

## Ranking rationale (S, O, feasibility)

1. **A** — richest dialectic for 6k (concedes something real to each rival and beats both); criterion + minted distinction = clearly statable contribution; engages the newest close literature (Kitsik 2025) and goes beyond it; crux solvable in-house.
2. **B** — most contrarian and second-most original; lives on one bold asymmetry claim; partially occupied territory (epistemic-case genus debates) raises Originality risk.
3. **C** — freshest mechanism, weakest philosophical center; conclusion goes conditional under its own best objection.
4. **D** — clean but dominated by A; converges on A/C under pressure.

Recommendation to the custodian: **A**, with B preserved as the strongest internal opposition (its verdictive-register point becomes an objection A must answer).
