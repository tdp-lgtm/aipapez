# Thesis Candidates v0.1 — injustice-concept-proliferation

Changelog: v0.1 — initial generation of 12 candidates (AI-originated, 2026-09-22), pre-screen pending.

Task (custodian's framing): is the proliferation of named injustice concepts ("epistemic injustice", "zetetic injustice", "ontic injustice", …) good or bad for philosophy and society? Take a stance; novel argument; objections and replies.

Format per candidate: one-sentence test ("I argue that P, against the view that Q, on the grounds that R"), contribution type, originality hypothesis, first-pass notes. Verdicts added after the pre-screen (§ at end).

---

## C1 — The Uptake-Scarcity (Currency) Argument [conditional contra]

- **One-sentence:** I argue that the proliferation of injustice coinages is justified only when a coinage is *backed* — when it specifies who is wronged, in what capacity, and what would count as redress — against both the booster view that coining is hermeneutical progress per se and the blanket concept-creep critique, on the grounds that injustice-talk competes for a scarce social resource (uptake: attention, sympathy, enforcement), so unbacked coinages devalue the claim-making power of all injustice claims.
- **Type:** new argument + a criterion (the Backing Condition).
- **Originality hypothesis:** dilution worries exist informally ("concept creep", "harm inflation"), but the literature lacks (a) the distinction between deontic validity (non-scarce) and uptake (scarce) as the thing coinages compete for, and (b) a stated adequacy condition on *injustice* coinages specifically.
- **Notes:** The strongest objection is internal: deontic force is not a currency — my duty not to commit testimonial injustice is not weakened when ontic injustice is named (see C12). The refined version concedes that validity is non-scarce and relocates the scarcity in uptake. Must handle structural injustice (no neat duty-bearers) without ruling it out absurdly — backing may be satisfiable by remedial rather than causal responsibility.

## C2 — The Selection Argument [pro]

- **One-sentence:** I argue that proliferation is good for philosophy and society, against concept-creep critics who take multiplication itself to dilute, on the grounds that coinages are cheap, non-rival hypotheses about the structure of wrongs, and the practice is a generate-and-filter search of normative space with asymmetric payoffs: failed coinages die cheaply while successful ones (as "epistemic injustice" in medicine and law) compound.
- **Type:** new argument (population-thinking / mechanism-design reframing of the practice).
- **Originality hypothesis:** evolutionary framings exist for conceptual engineering generally; the asymmetric-payoff argument applied to injustice concepts, with the critics' evidence recast as filter-failure rather than proliferation-cost, appears fresh.
- **Notes:** Empirical soft spot: do bad coinages actually die? Citation incentives may preserve them. A defensible reply concedes this and turns the conclusion conditional on filter health, with the filter's institutional design as the payoff. Risk: conclusion drifts from "good" to "good if we fix peer review", which is weaker.

## C3 — Concepts vs Labels [mixed, resolvable to "good, with a burden"]

- **One-sentence:** I argue that the proliferation of fine-grained *analyses* of wrongs is good while the proliferation of *injustice-labels* is justified only where a specific normative burden is discharged, against wholesale evaluations of the practice, on the grounds that the "-injustice" suffix is a persuasive classification that borrows the verdictive force of "injustice" before it has been earned by argument.
- **Type:** distinction (analysis/label) + diagnosis.
- **Originality hypothesis:** persuasive-definition machinery (Stevenson) has not, to my knowledge, been turned on the injustice-suffix convention.
- **Notes:** Overlaps C1 (both impose a burden at the point of coining). C3's burden is discursive (earn the verdict); C1's is structural (specify the claim). Mergeable; C3 alone may be too thin a spine for 6k.

## C4 — The Hermeneutical Bootstrap [pro, burden-shifting]

- **One-sentence:** I argue that the presumption favors coining, against critics who demand advance criteria, on the grounds that by our best theory of hermeneutical injustice the absence of a concept for a genuine wrong is itself an injustice, so the critic, not the coiner, bears the burden.
- **Type:** burden-of-proof argument.
- **Originality hypothesis:** weak — this is close to the field's self-understanding since Fricker; likely already implicit or explicit in the boosters' literature.
- **Notes:** Also structurally awkward: it defends the practice using the practice's own flagship concept (circularity objection would eat much of the essay). Likely kill after pre-screen unless searches surprise.

## C5 — Genus Erosion [contra]

- **One-sentence:** I argue that the proliferation is bad for philosophy in its current form, against the proliferation-as-progress view, on the grounds that species-multiplication is epistemically progressive only against a theorized genus, and the practice multiplies species of injustice while leaving the genus — what makes any of them *injustice* — untheorized, eroding the category that gives each coinage its point.
- **Type:** diagnosis.
- **Originality hypothesis:** "is X-injustice really injustice?" challenges exist for particular coinages; the general genus-first adequacy claim about the whole practice may be open.
- **Notes:** Needs a positive account of the genus to avoid being merely demanding; that account (equal standing in a specific capacity?) risks being the essay's real contribution and its most attackable part.

## C6 — Moral-Perception Public Goods [pro]

- **One-sentence:** I argue that proliferation is good for society, against grievance-culture critics, on the grounds that injustice concepts are non-rival public goods that train moral perception and enable victims' claim-making, and such goods are systematically under-produced outside academia.
- **Type:** reframing (economics of hermeneutical resources).
- **Originality hypothesis:** "hermeneutical resources as public goods" may already exist in the Fricker literature; must check.
- **Notes:** Empirical leg (uptake benefits) is grounded in documented applications (healthcare, law). Overlaps C2; C2 is the sharper mechanism.

## C7 — Taxonomy Displaces Argument [contra]

- **One-sentence:** I argue that the proliferation is bad for philosophy, against the assumption that conceptual fineness is progress, on the grounds that classificatory disputes ("is X a K-injustice?") are frequently merely verbal once the underlying normative facts are fixed, and their proliferation crowds out the first-order question of what is owed to whom.
- **Type:** application of verbal-dispute diagnostics to injustice taxonomy.
- **Originality hypothesis:** verbal-dispute machinery is well developed; its application here may be new; but the "crowding out" premise is empirical and hard to ground.
- **Notes:** Partially false as stated: classification sometimes fixes remedy, so not idle. The salvageable core (classification matters only via remedy) belongs inside C1.

## C8 — Mechanisms, Not Sui Generis Wrongs [reframing]

- **One-sentence:** I argue that proliferation is good exactly insofar as new concepts are read as mechanism-specifications of one genus of injustice and bad insofar as they are read as discoveries of sui generis wrongs, against both boosters and critics who evaluate the practice without distinguishing these readings, on the grounds that the explanatory work is done by mechanisms while the normative work is done by the genus.
- **Type:** distinction/reframing.
- **Originality hypothesis:** plausible open ground; adjacent to C5.
- **Notes:** More conciliatory than C5; risk of "both-sides" blandness unless the two readings make a concrete difference somewhere — remedy and blame practices could supply that.

## C9 — Concepts Travel Without Their Theories [split verdict]

- **One-sentence:** I argue that the proliferation is good for philosophy but carries a distinctive social cost, against uniform verdicts, on the grounds that concepts travel into public use without the theory that disciplines them (scope conditions, defeaters), so the very features that make a coinage academically successful predict its misapplication outside.
- **Type:** diagnosis with an empirical leg.
- **Originality hypothesis:** decontextualized-uptake worries exist in scattered applied literatures; a general argument may be open.
- **Notes:** Requires empirical claims about uptake this essay can gesture at but not establish; risks sociology. The travel mechanism could instead serve as an objection-section topic in C1 or C2.

## C10 — The Zetetic Opportunity Cost [contra]

- **One-sentence:** I argue that the proliferation is zetetically bad for philosophy, against neutrality about research allocation, on the grounds that coinage is professionally over-rewarded relative to remedy-research, so the field over-invests in taxonomy at the margin.
- **Type:** incentive argument.
- **Originality hypothesis:** likely fresh in print, but the load-bearing premises are sociology-of-academia claims I cannot properly ground within this essay's evidential means.
- **Notes:** Probable kill on feasibility (Accuracy criterion), not on interest.

## C11 — A Redundancy Test via One Coinage [counterexample]

- **One-sentence:** I argue that at least one prominent coinage is redundant (its cases are fully covered by existing concepts), against its proponents, on the grounds that redundancy is demonstrable by paraphrase without loss.
- **Type:** counterexample generalized into a test.
- **Originality hypothesis:** depends entirely on the chosen target; several coinages have standing critiques already.
- **Notes:** Too narrow for the custodian's question (which asks for a stance on the practice); but the paraphrase-without-loss test is a usable component inside C1/C5.

## C12 — Claims Are Not Currency [pro, direct rebuttal of scarcity]

- **One-sentence:** I argue that proliferation is good and the inflation metaphor is confused, against dilution critics, on the grounds that deontic force does not behave like a currency — duties and claims do not weaken as they multiply — so what critics call inflation is the sound of previously unclaimable wrongs entering the space of claims.
- **Type:** new argument (rebuttal of the scarcity model).
- **Originality hypothesis:** the explicit validity/scarcity disentangling seems fresh on the pro side too.
- **Notes:** C12 is the strongest objection to C1; whichever is the essay, the other supplies the dedicated objection section. The crux between them: whether the relevant quantity is deontic validity (non-scarce) or uptake (scarce). Whoever wins the crux wins the pair.

---

## The crux across the strongest candidates

C1 vs C12 identify the live crux: **what, if anything, do injustice claims compete for?** If nothing (validity view), proliferation is presumptively good (C12, C2). If uptake — finite attention, sympathy, institutional enforcement — then coinages impose externalities on existing claims and a backing/adequacy condition follows (C1, with C3's label-burden as a corollary). Any selected essay should be built so that this crux is solved first.

## Pre-screen verdicts (searches run 2026-09-22; queries logged in Process Log)

Key discoveries shaping the verdicts:

- **Kitsik 2025** (*Philosophical Studies*, "Conceptual inflation, communication and understanding, and collective attention"): the closest neighbor to C1. Argues conceptual inflation of problem-concepts neither significantly limits nor enhances communication/understanding, and identifies **collective attention allocation** as the real stake — but (per abstract) offers no normative verdict and no adequacy condition on coining, and her topic is *inflation* (stretching existing concepts), not *proliferation* (coining new kinds). C1 must engage her and go beyond.
- **Rights-inflation literature** (Alston 1984; Griffin 2008; recent deconstruction in *Leiden JIL* 2021): the currency/devaluation model is established *for rights*. A bare transposition is not novel; the validity/uptake disentangling and a backing condition for injustice-coinages specifically remain open.
- **Nikolaidis 2020** (*Educational Theory*): "Concept Proliferation as an Educational Good" — a direct pro-side interlocutor.
- **Haslam 2016/2020** ("concept creep", "harm inflation"): the standard critical frame; conflates creep and proliferation — a distinction this essay can mint.
- **Byskov 2021** (*J. Social Philosophy*, "What Makes Epistemic Injustice an 'Injustice'?") and **Coady** (distributive reframing): the genus question (C5) is partially occupied for the epistemic case; the general genus-first claim about the whole practice appears open but adjacent.
- **"Zetetic injustice"** is real and recent (*PQ* 2025, "Zetetic rights and wrong(ing)s") — usable as the practice's newest exhibit.
- No philosophy paper found applying selection/population thinking to injustice-concept proliferation (C2 open).
- No application of persuasive-definition machinery to the "-injustice" suffix found (C3 open).
