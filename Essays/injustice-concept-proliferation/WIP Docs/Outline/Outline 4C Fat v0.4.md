## §1 The Coining Boom (800w, 6 paras)

- **1.1** Cold open, "Compare:". *Dismissed*: a patient reports disabling post-operative pain; her chart calls her anxious; her testimony is discounted along lines that track her group membership; the pattern recurs across thousands of charts. The pattern has a name — testimonial injustice — and where the name went, recognition followed: training, guidelines, a way for the patient herself to say what happened (Fricker chs. 1, 7 on disk for the concept's content; case constructed). Verdict sentence: the coinage worked.
- **1.2** *Ambient Injustice*: imagine a paper coining this term for the diffuse worseness of life under modern social arrangements — real, socially produced, unevenly borne, and deliberately unlocalized: no particular capacity wronged, no mechanism, no shape a response could take; diffuseness is the concept's selling point. Verdict sentence: something has misfired — and not because the badness is unreal. The difference between these two coinages is this essay's subject.
- **1.3** The phenomenon named: since the flagship coinage (Fricker 2007), the family has grown fast — contributory (Dotson 2012), discursive (Kukla 2014, attested in Jenkins's genre-framing intro), exploitative (Berenstain 2016, attested in Smith & Archer's survey), affective (Srinivasan 2018; Archer & Mills 2019), ontic (Jenkins 2020), attention-based (Smith & Archer 2020), zetetic (Friedman 2025) — all records on disk or attested in on-disk sources. Not stray neologisms: a genre with shared form — identify a recurrent bad, name it "X-injustice", argue the name fills a gap (Jenkins's own intro frames the genre exactly so). The question, put plainly: is this practice good or bad for philosophy and for the societies philosophy addresses?
- **1.4** Two camps, fast. The Inflation View: injustice-talk is a currency, and minting new denominations debases it — the transposition of a worry with a distinguished history in the theory of rights (Alston on disk; Griffin #10 awaited). The Abundance View: concepts are non-rival; a new concept costs no one anything, and where wrongs go unnamed, naming is itself justice (Fricker ch. 7 and Nikolaidis, both on disk). One-word stances; both get their best form in §2.
- **1.5** Thesis, twice. Compressed: both camps are wrong about what a coinage spends, and seeing what it does spend yields a test that vindicates the practice — conditionally. Scoped: the Investment View — coining draws on the scarce capacity of others to take claims up; a coinage is justified when *backed* (capacity, mechanism, redress-shape fixed); backed coinages enlarge the very capacity they draw on; the flagships are backed; so the practice as conducted has been good — because of the condition, not the momentum.
- **1.6** Scope fences + roadmap. Not a licensing regime; unbacked ≠ unreal; verdict comparative and defeasible. §2 states the rivals at strength and mints a needed distinction; §3 argues both misidentify what coinage spends; §4 builds the Backing Condition; §5 tries the practice's exemplars; §6 answers the three strongest objections; §7 concludes.

## §2 Two Views About Minting (850w, 7 paras)

- **2.1** Distinction first: *proliferation* — adding new kind-concepts — is not *creep* — stretching an existing concept's extension outward or downward — Haslam's horizontal (new phenomena) and vertical (milder phenomena) creep (on disk). "Trauma" reaching milder cases is creep; "ontic injustice" is proliferation. The debate runs these together; the two have different economics.
- **2.2** Why the difference matters: creep degrades an existing concept's precision from inside — its mechanism blurs as unlike cases accumulate under it; the concept's own users lose the ability to say what it picks out. Proliferation leaves existing concepts untouched and adds a new one whose credentials can be assessed at birth. The best-documented costs in the critical literature are creep costs — the trivialization findings concern *broadened* concepts appearing less serious (Haslam et al. 2020, on disk); and Haslam's own ledger concedes the benefits sit exactly where broadening amounts to naming ("emotional abuse": a label that let a problem without a name be identified and protested) — that is, where creep approximates coining. If proliferation is bad, it must be bad in a different way. The Inflation View, restated to target proliferation properly, is next.
- **2.3** Inflation View steelmanned (i): in the theory of rights the worry is old and precise — Alston: rights "conjured up as if by magic," proliferation "much more likely to contribute to a serious devaluation of the human rights currency than to enrich significantly the overall coverage"; note his exact target — "not so much... the proliferation of new rights but rather the haphazard, almost anarchic manner" of their proclamation, and his observation that vagueness ("chameleon-like quality") is what eased their adoption (all on disk). The philosophical version: inflationary pressure debases the term's determinateness of sense (Griffin, verify — #10, still awaited; steelman stands on Alston alone if needed). The picture: normative language as common currency, each coinage an issue of new denominations, unbacked issues devaluing every note.
- **2.4** Inflation View steelmanned (ii) + concession: transposed to injustice-concepts, the worry is not silly. Something in the neighborhood *is* finite; and some coinage behavior is plainly extractive — a career is made by a minting, whether or not the mint was needed. Grant both. Note also where Alston himself ended: substantive quality-criteria are unworkable *for a political assembly* (their application "quintessentially political"), so he retreated to procedure — an appellation contrôlée. Philosophy's forum is different: a coinage paper can be assessed substantively, clause by clause, which is exactly the possibility this essay takes up. What the Inflation View owes us — and never supplies — is the identity of the scarce medium. Its own metaphor fails exactly there (§3.3).
- **2.5** Abundance View steelmanned (i): concepts are non-rival; my using "ontic injustice" leaves you no less of it. And on the practice's founding theory, conceptual gaps are themselves sites of injustice — those whose experiences lack names are disadvantaged in making sense of and claiming against what happens to them (Fricker ch. 7, on disk). Coining, on this view, is not spending but repair.
- **2.6** Abundance View steelmanned (ii) + concession: the strongest published defense of proliferation adds a rider — proliferation serves flourishing when paired with critical tools for sorting good coinages from bad (Nikolaidis, on disk — note his anti-paternalism: the sorting is done by concept-users, not gatekeepers, which 6.4 will echo). Grant the non-rivalry of concepts; grant that licensing regimes overshoot. Note the rider concedes precisely what the view officially denies: that sorting is needed. What the view owes us: an account of why sorting matters if nothing is spent.
- **2.7** Impasse diagnosis, pivot paragraph (one line): the camps disagree about the object of expenditure while neither names one. So ask directly: when a philosopher coins an injustice concept, what — if anything — is drawn on?

## §3 What a Coinage Spends (1,200w, 9 paras)

- **3.1** Claim-making analysis (i): calling something an injustice is not sorting; it is claiming. The genre's own papers end in calls — recognize, reform, redress (checked fair against Jenkins §6 and Friedman §4, on disk). A classic account of rights locates their value in the activity of claiming — what it is to stand and demand (Feinberg, on disk: claiming as the activity that gives rights their value; Nowheresville). Injustice-ascription is that activity's kind-level analogue, and not by metaphor: the coinage paper itself performs a standing, general claim on behalf of the kind's members — to coin "K-injustice" *is* to claim at the level of kinds, minting a standing form under which anyone in the kind's extension can file.
- **3.2** Claim-making analysis (ii): the minimal contrast. "Epistemic bad luck" and "epistemic injustice" can sort the same events; only the second demands anything of anyone. The suffix carries verdictive force: it asserts that response is owed. Two terms, defined here and used with discipline throughout: **uptake** — the recognition and response a claim calls for (owed to claimants); **uptake capacity** — the finite social ability to supply it (the scarce thing). Scarcity claims attach to capacity; owed-ness to uptake. Micro-concession: some tokens of the suffix are thin; the analysis targets the practice's central, thick use — the use that makes the coinage worth wanting.
- **3.3** First result, against the Inflation View's metaphor: validity does not dilute. *Two Wrongs*: a regime tortures A; it emerges that it also tortures B; no one thinks the wrong done to A has shrunk. Recognizing more wrongs does not thin the wrongness of any. Grant the conditional — if coinages spent validity, dilution would follow — and deny the antecedent: bindingness is not a currency (in the rights domain, the deconstruction of the inflation idiom makes the same point (Theilen, on disk)). Even the tempting counterexample confirms the line: with imperfect duties of aid, more recognized claimants do thin what each receives — but what thins is fulfillment, not bindingness; the case points straight at the real scarcity, which is next.
- **3.4** Diagnose the rival's appeal: the inflation idiom survives because something nearby *is* scarce, and the critics reached for the wrong thing. What is finite is not the validity of claims but the world's capacity to take them up: attention (Simon anchor, #13 still awaited; James's line quoted in Kitsik, on disk, suffices), and, more tractably, institutional processing — dockets, curricula, complaint routes, news cycles, training hours.
- **3.5** *The Docket*: an ombuds office files complaints under k headings; each heading carries an intake route, a caseload, a triage weight. Add heading k+1: every pending claim's expected uptake shifts — not because any claim lost validity, but because processing is rationed. Verdict: uptake competition is structural fact, not psychological conjecture — the institutional face of Kitsik's commitment mechanism (public commitments attach to categories; categories are the units institutions run on). To mint a kind is to enter the rationing.
- **3.6** Uptake capacity is not mere throughput — its allocation can be *owed*: within the practice's own recent literature, attention itself is argued to be something one can be unjustly denied (Smith & Archer, on disk). [Drafting discipline: that work establishes person-directed attention-justice; the claim-directed normativity this essay needs rides on 3.8's internal turn — cite them as corroboration, never as the load-bearer.] And the nearest prior work locates the stake of conceptual change exactly here: Kitsik argues the communication and understanding worries fail (linguistic resources are flexible) and that what changed usage really moves is the community's allocation of collective attentional resources. One honest relation sentence: Kitsik locates the stake and hands the verdict to first-order politics — whether the newly indicated problems deserve priority; this essay adds a form-level, content-neutral condition *prior* to that negotiation — an unbacked coinage cannot even enter it as an answerable claim-kind.
- **3.7** Micro-objection: "attention allocation doesn't track concept-count." Reply via Kitsik's own mechanisms (on disk): the *emotion* mechanism (the terms' mobilizing charge — with her honest caveats about backfire) and, more robustly, the *commitment* mechanism — institutions publicly committed to addressing category K must prioritize whatever falls under K, which is why category-change moves funding, procedure, and research agendas; add the essay's own extensions: categories make claims countable in reports, filable in procedures, teachable in curricula. Built-in fallback: the argument needs only that coinages are a non-trivial channel of uptake-capacity allocation, not the sole determinant.
- **3.8** The ignored-claim point, internal turn: the practice's founding concept already treats uptake failure as wronging — testimonial injustice *is* wrongful recognition-failure (Fricker ch. 1, on disk). By the practice's own lights, then, a valid claim met with predictable non-uptake wrongs its bearer a second time. This is the premise the essay stakes most on, and it is argued from inside the practice, not imported.
- **3.9** Consequence, carefully drawn — and the distinction that guards it: the wrong is minting claims that could not be answered *even by a willing audience* — non-uptake rooted in the coinage's own structure (*Ambient Injustice*: nothing to look for, no one to believe, no shape a response could take). It is emphatically not the coiner's wrong when an answerable claim meets an audience's refusal — that refusal is the audience's wrong, and the practice has already named it: Dotson's contributory injustice is precisely the case where the marginalized *can* articulate their claims in their own resources and dominant audiences willfully refuse uptake (on disk). Prophetic coinages, minted into predictable refusal, stand vindicated. Answerability, not actual uptake, is the standard. Placed objection: "this proves too much; all discourse competes for attention" — no: only verdictive coinages mint claim-kinds; analysis and description demand no uptake. So coinages divide by their relation to the commons. What property makes the difference — §4.

## §4 The Backing Condition (1,050w, 8 paras)

- **4.1** Method sentence + B0: start crude and repair in public. B0: a coinage is justified iff it names a real bad. *Ambient Injustice* breaks it: the bad is real; the coinage is idle. Realness is not the active ingredient.
- **4.2** B1: add capacity — the coinage must fix whom it wrongs, *as what* (as knower, as inquirer, as member of a constructed kind). *Ambient Injustice\**: the coiner amends — "wronged as residents of modernity." Capacity named, yet claims remain unfilable: against what route? Still idle. Capacity without mechanism is address without street.
- **4.3** B2: add mechanism — the route by which the arrangement wrongs the capacity (prejudicial credibility deficit; refusal of standing concepts; wrongful construction of the kind itself). Now a harder break, from the practice's most important cases: structural wrongs have no culprit to whom the mechanism points. If the third clause demands a respondent, the condition disqualifies the very coinages that matter most. The repair must aim lower than a perpetrator.
- **4.4** B3, final, displayed (the essay's one labeled display): "K-injustice" is **backed** iff it fixes (i) capacity — who is wronged, as what; (ii) mechanism — the route of the wronging; (iii) redress-shape — what responding to the claim would be, where the shape may be shared and forward-looking rather than culprit-directed. Gloss in plain words: a backed coinage tells you what to look for, whom to believe, and what helping would mean. In two words: backing is answerability — clause (iii) demands an *articulable* response-shape, not institutions currently willing to respond.
- **4.4a** Anchoring and a lurking objection (footnote or short paragraph): Feinberg's manifesto rights (on disk) already license claims-*to* without valid claims-*against* — "needs and demands in movement," "permanent possibilities of rights" — which vindicates clause (iii)'s shape-not-respondent form: claims may validly outrun assignable addressees. But doesn't the manifesto license then legitimate unbacked coinages as rhetoric? No: Feinberg's license is earned by the determinacy of the *need* and the direction of the claim's transformation into rights — a manifesto claim fixes capacity and mechanism and points its movement; *Ambient Injustice* fixes nothing and moves nowhere. Backing is the manifesto license made explicit.
- **4.5** The economizing step (i), the governing image earned: a good diagnostic category bundles detection, address, and treatment-shape; once it exists, each future case costs less to recognize and answer. *Dismissed* revisited: what was invisible in a thousand charts becomes a teachable pattern with a corrective practice attached. The coinage did not consume the clinic's attention; it organized it.
- **4.6** The economizing step (ii), the reversal: therefore a backed coinage *adds* uptake capacity — it is investment in the commons it draws on; only unbacked minting spends. Grant the critics their scarcity premise in full and their conclusion flips under the condition. In a slogan: minting is not spending; unbacked minting is.
- **4.7** Vacuity micro-objection: a test everything passes is no test. Exhibit failure: *Ambient Injustice* fails (i) (no capacity fixed), fails (ii) (no route), fails (iii) (no response-shape — by design). And clause-satisfaction is checkable in a coinage paper's own text, which is why the §5 trials can be run at all.
- **4.7b** Congestion micro-objection (the economist's reply): scarcity is aggregate, the condition is marginal — even individually backed coinages could jointly congest triage, as forty thousand diagnostic codes would swamp a clinic that runs well on forty. Three-part answer: backed coinages *nest* — the mechanism clause locates each new kind relative to existing kinds, which is why the actual family is a tree (species under a genus), not a flat list, and why unbacked coinages cannot nest (no mechanism, no location); backed coinages historically create capacity (4.5); and, conceding the in-principle absorption limit, the actual practice's scale sits nowhere near it — the Investment View prices the marginal coinage, which is the live question.
- **4.8** Central argument, displayed once in premise form, defense locations named: P1 coining K-injustice mints a claim-kind (§3.1–3.2); P2 claim-kinds spend no validity (§3.3); P3 claim-kinds draw on uptake capacity, which is scarce, and mint claims whose uptake is owed (§3.4–3.8); P4 a backed coinage economizes uptake capacity, an unbacked one spends it (§4.5–4.7b); C so backed coining is pro tanto justified and unbacked coining pro tanto not — proliferation is vindicated when and because its coinages are backed. Whether the actual practice is vindicated is now an assessable question — §5.

## §5 The Practice on Trial (900w, 7 paras)

- **5.1** Method sentence: run B3 over exemplars oldest to newest, filling each clause from the coinage paper's own text; the essay's verdict on the practice is the sum of these trials.
- **5.2** Testimonial injustice: capacity — the subject as knower; mechanism — identity-prejudicial credibility deficit (Fricker ch. 1, on disk); redress-shape — the corrective virtue of testimonial justice in hearers plus its institutional training (chs. 1, 7). PASS, clause-fills quoted from the source at drafting. One sentence: the trial shows why this coinage traveled — clinics and courts could run on it.
- **5.3** Ontic injustice, worked fully as the hard case (Jenkins on disk): prima facie clause (iii) fails — no response un-constructs a kind. Work her machinery: capacity — the subject as bearer of moral entitlements, owed recognition respect; mechanism — the mismatch between moral entitlements and the constraints/enablements that partly constitute kind membership (moral injury, via Hampton's diminishment); redress-shape — explicitly two-track in her §5: reform the kind where the wrongful constraints are contingent (the social kind *wife* after the 1991 end of the marital rape exemption — the kind changed, the label survived) or abolish it where they are necessary (*slave*). Her §6 makes the demand-for-intervention role of the concept explicit. Verdict: PASS — and the working exhibits backing achievable off the easy path; she even devotes a section to what the concept gains us — the backing question asked in her own voice. (Two paragraphs.)
- **5.4** Zetetic injustice, the newest exhibit (Friedman on disk): capacity — the subject qua participant in group inquiry; mechanism — violation (not mere infringement) of interest-grounded zetetic rights (aid, non-impediment, influence), the interest being exercise of Epistemic Contribution within cooperative inquiry; redress-shape — accountability standing, inquiry-overlaying norms (the lab meeting as the influence-right institutionalized), exit from the joint venture, plus his own §4 conjecture that distinctive treatment is needed. Verdict: PASS, with an honest degree-note — the ameliorative side is the most programmatic of the three trials, backing largely inherited from the rights-form; said plainly, this shows the condition discriminating by degree rather than rubber-stamping. Bonus for 5.6: his §4 does backing-work explicitly (extension-difference from testimonial injustice and epistemic oppression; remedies that do not transfer).
- **5.5** The label corollary: the suffix is a persuasive classification — it borrows a verdict's force at the moment of naming (Stevenson, on disk). Backing is what earns the borrowing. Unbacked analysis is respectable philosophy under a non-verdictive name ("epistemic bad luck", "ambient burden") until the clauses can be filled. A naming norm, not a censorship norm.
- **5.6** Verdict on the practice, with the practice's own self-discipline exhibited: the flagship coinages pass, and the genre already polices the suffix from inside — Fricker herself urges keeping discriminatory epistemic injustice "bounded and specific" while new phenomena "merit their own classifications" (2017, on disk: the founder's policy is bounded proliferation, not stretching); Dotson distinguishes her three forms precisely by the order of change each requires — remedy-shape as the principle of individuation (on disk); Byskov's stakeholder condition is introduced expressly "to avoid inflating the concept" (on disk). The boom has been investment, not inflation; the critics' unease tracks the failures the condition names — creep-degraded uses and the occasional unbacked mint — not minting as such.
- **5.7** What the verdict buys philosophy and society respectively (one paragraph, split): for philosophy, a discipline internal to the genre (coinage papers that fill the clauses are better papers); for society, concepts that arrive ready to run on — recognizable, countable, filable. The two benefits are one benefit seen from two sides.

## §6 Objections (900w, 7 paras)

- **6.1** Structural objection, steelmanned: the deepest structural wrongs resist assignment — that is the point of the social connection analysis (Young ch. 4, on disk, with page markers); if clause (iii) quietly demands a respondent, the condition disqualifies structural coinages and lands conservative, as inflation-rhetoric tends to (Theilen, on disk). State it as the opponent would.
- **6.2** Reply, with the concession held at the close: clause (iii) was built at B2→B3 precisely to demand shape, not respondent — and the social connection model *is* a redress-shape: shared, forward-looking, political responsibility (Young ch. 4, on disk). Structural coinages backed by such theory pass. Where even shape-level response is inarticulable, the coinage is not yet backed — and that is the right verdict, the same unease *Ambient Injustice* elicits; the condition asks of coiners only the work the best structural theorists already do.
- **6.3** Equity objection, steelmanned at full force with Dotson's own materials (on disk): dominant audiences already refuse uptake of concepts coined by marginalized speakers — contributory injustice; and Dotson's deeper lesson is that theorists' boundary-drawing itself perpetrates the wrong — Fricker's epistemic-bad-luck border was a *closed conceptual structure* that foreclosed further forms, and "a catchall theory of epistemic injustice is an unrealistic expectation." A backing norm looks like exactly such boundary-policing: a respectable dismissal ("insufficiently backed") whose burden falls on coiners working from inside the hermeneutical gaps the practice exists to close. The condition would institutionalize the wrong it prices. This is the objection the essay most owes an answer.
- **6.4** Reply + concession: the condition prices coinages, not coiners, and its clauses are claimant-serving — capacity, mechanism, and redress-shape are exactly what makes a marginalized claim *filable* rather than dismissible; a backed coinage is harder, not easier, to wave away, and meeting the condition costs a paragraph, not a gate. Concede: any norm can be weaponized in application; distinguish the norm from its abuse, and note what the norm gives the abused party that the status quo does not — a determinate reply (fill the clauses and the dismissal fails). And the answerability standard (3.9) blocks the worst abuse outright: refusing an answerable claim is the refuser's wrong — the condition cannot be cited against a coinage merely because its audience will not listen. Deeper reply to Dotson's boundary worry: B3 is an *open* structure in precisely her sense — it defines no boundary of injustice, forecloses no future form, conditions coinages one by one, and is satisfiable in indefinitely many ways; indeed it blocks the Fricker-style move she diagnoses, since no coinage is excluded by a prior taxonomy — each carries its own answerability. Her own practice models the condition: her three forms are individuated by the order of change each requires — remedy-shape as the principle of distinction.
- **6.5** Genus objection, steelmanned: a checklist is not a theory; verdictive labels need grounds — an account of what makes all these *injustice* — and backing seems to let coiners skip the genus question (the essay's own strongest internal rival, argued at strength).
- **6.6** Reply + concession: backing is the genus-commitment in operational form — each clause forces the coiner to say whom this wrongs, how, and what answering would be; that is what having grounds *is* at the point of coining, and the field's history confirms the order — the genus debate for the epistemic case proceeded productively after the coinages, on the material they provided — Byskov's five conditions systematize the injustices post hoc, and his stakeholder condition is introduced expressly "to avoid inflating the concept" (on disk): the practice polices its own suffix. Concede the deeper point: the misfortune/injustice boundary is historically mobile and rigid rules mislocate it (Shklar, #21 still awaited — claim held at the level her book's documented thesis supports, or softened to the essay's own observation if the source does not arrive) — which is why B3 conditions present *articulability*, not eternal metaphysics and not current institutional readiness (3.9's standard): what response-shapes can be articulated shifts historically, and the condition moves with it instead of fighting it.
- **6.7** Reflexivity objection, short: does the condition pass its own analogue? Capacity — claimants at large, as claimants; mechanism — unbacked minting's manufacture of unanswerable claims (§3.9); redress-shape — the condition itself, a naming norm any coiner can satisfy in a paragraph. It passes, and its cheapness is the point: the Investment View polices nothing; it prices.

## §7 Conclusion (250w, 2 paras)

- **7.1** Restatement + enumeration: the question was whether the coining boom is good or bad; the answer required finding what coinages spend. Three moves: validity does not dilute, so the Inflation View fails as stated; uptake is scarce and owed, so the Abundance View fails too; the Backing Condition separates investment from expenditure, and the practice's exemplars sit on the investment side. Scope re-marked: comparative, defeasible, no licensing.
- **7.2** Braided close: return to *Dismissed* and *Ambient Injustice*. What separated them was never sincerity, nor the reality of the bads they name. One coinage told us what to look for, whom to believe, and what helping would mean; the other told us only that things are bad. The difference is backing — and it is the difference between adding to the common store of justice and drawing it down.
