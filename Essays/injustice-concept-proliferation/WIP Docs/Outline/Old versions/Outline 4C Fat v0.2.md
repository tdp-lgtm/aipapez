## §1 The Coining Boom (800w, 6 paras)

- **1.1** Cold open, "Compare:". *Dismissed*: a patient reports disabling post-operative pain; her chart calls her anxious; her testimony is discounted along lines that track her group membership; the pattern recurs across thousands of charts. The pattern has a name — testimonial injustice — and where the name went, recognition followed: training, guidelines, a way for the patient herself to say what happened (verify — #3 for the concept's content; case constructed). Verdict sentence: the coinage worked.
- **1.2** *Ambient Injustice*: imagine a paper coining this term for the diffuse worseness of life under modern social arrangements — real, socially produced, unevenly borne, and deliberately unlocalized: no particular capacity wronged, no mechanism, no shape a response could take; diffuseness is the concept's selling point. Verdict sentence: something has misfired — and not because the badness is unreal. The difference between these two coinages is this essay's subject.
- **1.3** The phenomenon named: since the flagship coinage, the family has grown fast — contributory, discursive, exploitative, affective, ontic, attention-based, zetetic (verify — dates/records #3,#12,#5,#4,#7 + Lit Map footnote-tier). Not stray neologisms: a genre with shared form — identify a recurrent bad, name it "X-injustice", argue the name fills a gap. The question, put plainly: is this practice good or bad for philosophy and for the societies philosophy addresses?
- **1.4** Two camps, fast. The Inflation View: injustice-talk is a currency, and minting new denominations debases it — the transposition of a worry with a distinguished history in the theory of rights (verify — #2, #10). The Abundance View: concepts are non-rival; a new concept costs no one anything, and where wrongs go unnamed, naming is itself justice (verify — #3, #8). One-word stances; both get their best form in §2.
- **1.5** Thesis, twice. Compressed: both camps are wrong about what a coinage spends, and seeing what it does spend yields a test that vindicates the practice — conditionally. Scoped: the Investment View — coining draws on the scarce capacity of others to take claims up; a coinage is justified when *backed* (capacity, mechanism, redress-shape fixed); backed coinages enlarge the very capacity they draw on; the flagships are backed; so the practice as conducted has been good — because of the condition, not the momentum.
- **1.6** Scope fences + roadmap. Not a licensing regime; unbacked ≠ unreal; verdict comparative and defeasible. §2 states the rivals at strength and mints a needed distinction; §3 argues both misidentify what coinage spends; §4 builds the Backing Condition; §5 tries the practice's exemplars; §6 answers the three strongest objections; §7 concludes.

## §2 Two Views About Minting (850w, 7 paras)

- **2.1** Distinction first: *proliferation* — adding new kind-concepts — is not *creep* — stretching an existing concept's extension outward or downward (verify — #6 for the horizontal/vertical machinery). "Trauma" reaching milder cases is creep; "ontic injustice" is proliferation. The debate runs these together; the two have different economics.
- **2.2** Why the difference matters: creep degrades an existing concept's precision from inside — its mechanism blurs as unlike cases accumulate under it; the concept's own users lose the ability to say what it picks out. Proliferation leaves existing concepts untouched and adds a new one whose credentials can be assessed at birth. The best-documented costs in the critical literature are creep costs (verify — #6); if proliferation is bad, it must be bad in a different way. The Inflation View, restated to target proliferation properly, is next.
- **2.3** Inflation View steelmanned (i): in the theory of rights, the worry is old and serious — proclaim new rights without discipline and the currency's credibility erodes; a quality-control proposal followed (verify — #2). The philosophical version: inflationary pressure debases the term's determinateness of sense (verify — #10). The picture: normative language as common currency, each coinage an issue of new denominations, unbacked issues devaluing every note in circulation.
- **2.4** Inflation View steelmanned (ii) + concession: transposed to injustice-concepts, the worry is not silly. Something in the neighborhood *is* finite; and some coinage behavior is plainly extractive — a career is made by a minting, whether or not the mint was needed. Grant both. What the view owes us — and never supplies — is the identity of the scarce medium. Its own metaphor will fail exactly there (§3.2).
- **2.5** Abundance View steelmanned (i): concepts are non-rival; my using "ontic injustice" leaves you no less of it. And on the practice's founding theory, conceptual gaps are themselves sites of injustice — those whose experiences lack names are disadvantaged in making sense of and claiming against what happens to them (verify — #3). Coining, on this view, is not spending but repair.
- **2.6** Abundance View steelmanned (ii) + concession: the strongest published defense of proliferation adds a rider — proliferation serves flourishing when paired with critical tools for sorting good coinages from bad (verify — #8). Grant the non-rivalry of concepts; grant that licensing regimes overshoot. Note the rider concedes precisely what the view officially denies: that sorting is needed. What the view owes us: an account of why sorting matters if nothing is spent.
- **2.7** Impasse diagnosis, pivot paragraph (one line): the camps disagree about the object of expenditure while neither names one. So ask directly: when a philosopher coins an injustice concept, what — if anything — is drawn on?

## §3 What a Coinage Spends (1,200w, 9 paras)

- **3.1** Claim-making analysis (i): calling something an injustice is not sorting; it is claiming. The genre's own papers end in calls — recognize, reform, redress (verify — check fair characterization against #1,#4,#7). A classic account of rights locates their value in the activity of claiming — what it is to stand and demand (verify — #20). Injustice-ascription is that activity's kind-level analogue, and not by metaphor: the coinage paper itself performs a standing, general claim on behalf of the kind's members — to coin "K-injustice" *is* to claim at the level of kinds, minting a standing form under which anyone in the kind's extension can file.
- **3.2** Claim-making analysis (ii): the minimal contrast. "Epistemic bad luck" and "epistemic injustice" can sort the same events; only the second demands anything of anyone. The suffix carries verdictive force: it asserts that response is owed. Define **uptake**: the pair of recognition and response a claim calls for. Micro-concession: some tokens of the suffix are thin; the analysis targets the practice's central, thick use — the use that makes the coinage worth wanting.
- **3.3** First result, against the Inflation View's metaphor: validity does not dilute. *Two Wrongs*: a regime tortures A; it emerges that it also tortures B; no one thinks the wrong done to A has shrunk. Recognizing more wrongs does not thin the wrongness of any. Grant the conditional — if coinages spent validity, dilution would follow — and deny the antecedent: bindingness is not a currency (in the rights domain, the deconstruction of the inflation idiom makes the same point (verify — #15)).
- **3.4** Diagnose the rival's appeal: the inflation idiom survives because something nearby *is* scarce, and the critics reached for the wrong thing. What is finite is not the validity of claims but the world's capacity to take them up: attention (verify — #13), and, more tractably, institutional processing — dockets, curricula, complaint routes, news cycles, training hours.
- **3.5** *The Docket*: an ombuds office files complaints under k headings; each heading carries an intake route, a caseload, a triage weight. Add heading k+1: every pending claim's expected uptake shifts — not because any claim lost validity, but because processing is rationed. Verdict: uptake competition is structural fact, not psychological conjecture. Kinds are the units institutions run on; to mint a kind is to enter the rationing.
- **3.6** Uptake is not mere throughput — it can be *owed*: within the practice's own recent literature, attention itself is argued to be something one can be unjustly denied (verify — #5). And the stake of conceptual change has been located, in the nearest prior work, in collective attention allocation — with communication and understanding largely robust (verify — #1). One honest sentence on the relation: that work stops at locating the stake; this essay's next two steps — normativizing it and deriving a condition — are where the argument begins.
- **3.7** Micro-objection: "attention allocation doesn't track concept-count." Reply: concepts are the channel by which claims become perceptible at a glance, countable in reports, filable in procedures, teachable in curricula (align with the mechanism inventory where the text supports it (verify — #1)). Built-in fallback: the argument needs only that coinages are a non-trivial channel of uptake allocation, not the sole determinant.
- **3.8** The ignored-claim point, internal turn: the practice's founding concept already treats uptake failure as wronging — testimonial injustice *is* wrongful recognition-failure (verify — #3). By the practice's own lights, then, a valid claim met with predictable non-uptake wrongs its bearer a second time. This is the premise the essay stakes most on, and it is argued from inside the practice, not imported.
- **3.9** Consequence, and pivot: minting a claim-kind into predictable non-uptake manufactures second-order wronging — *Ambient Injustice* claimants are told they are wronged and handed nothing that makes response possible. Placed objection: "this proves too much; all discourse competes for attention" — no: only verdictive coinages mint claim-kinds; analysis and description demand no uptake. So coinages divide by their relation to the commons. What property makes the difference — §4.

## §4 The Backing Condition (1,050w, 8 paras)

- **4.1** Method sentence + B0: start crude and repair in public. B0: a coinage is justified iff it names a real bad. *Ambient Injustice* breaks it: the bad is real; the coinage is idle. Realness is not the active ingredient.
- **4.2** B1: add capacity — the coinage must fix whom it wrongs, *as what* (as knower, as inquirer, as member of a constructed kind). *Ambient Injustice\**: the coiner amends — "wronged as residents of modernity." Capacity named, yet claims remain unfilable: against what route? Still idle. Capacity without mechanism is address without street.
- **4.3** B2: add mechanism — the route by which the arrangement wrongs the capacity (prejudicial credibility deficit; refusal of standing concepts; wrongful construction of the kind itself). Now a harder break, from the practice's most important cases: structural wrongs have no culprit to whom the mechanism points. If the third clause demands a respondent, the condition disqualifies the very coinages that matter most. The repair must aim lower than a perpetrator.
- **4.4** B3, final, displayed (the essay's one labeled display): "K-injustice" is **backed** iff it fixes (i) capacity — who is wronged, as what; (ii) mechanism — the route of the wronging; (iii) redress-shape — what responding to the claim would be, where the shape may be shared and forward-looking rather than culprit-directed. Gloss in plain words: a backed coinage tells you what to look for, whom to believe, and what helping would mean.
- **4.5** The economizing step (i), the governing image earned: a good diagnostic category bundles detection, address, and treatment-shape; once it exists, each future case costs less to recognize and answer. *Dismissed* revisited: what was invisible in a thousand charts becomes a teachable pattern with a corrective practice attached. The coinage did not consume the clinic's attention; it organized it.
- **4.6** The economizing step (ii), the reversal: therefore a backed coinage *adds* uptake capacity — it is investment in the commons it draws on; only unbacked minting spends. Grant the critics their scarcity premise in full and their conclusion flips under the condition. In a slogan: minting is not spending; unbacked minting is.
- **4.7** Vacuity micro-objection: a test everything passes is no test. Exhibit failure: *Ambient Injustice* fails (i) (no capacity fixed), fails (ii) (no route), fails (iii) (no response-shape — by design). And clause-satisfaction is checkable in a coinage paper's own text, which is why the §5 trials can be run at all.
- **4.8** Central argument, displayed once in premise form, defense locations named: P1 coining K-injustice mints a claim-kind (§3.1–3.2); P2 claim-kinds spend no validity (§3.3); P3 claim-kinds draw on uptake, which is scarce and owed (§3.4–3.8); P4 a backed coinage economizes uptake, an unbacked one spends it (§4.5–4.7); C proliferation is justified when and because its coinages are backed. Whether the actual practice is vindicated is now an assessable question — §5.

- **5.4** [BLOCKED #7 — draft only after Friedman full text] Zetetic injustice, the newest exhibit: a rights-scaffold (aid, non-interference, influence) fixes capacity and response by construction; check the mechanism clause against the text. Verdict PASS or PARTIAL as the text merits — a PARTIAL would show the condition discriminating, which the essay says plainly.
- **5.5** The label corollary: the suffix is a persuasive classification — it borrows a verdict's force at the moment of naming (verify — #16). Backing is what earns the borrowing. Unbacked analysis is respectable philosophy under a non-verdictive name ("epistemic bad luck", "ambient burden") until the clauses can be filled. A naming norm, not a censorship norm.
- **5.6** Verdict on the practice: the flagship coinages pass; the boom has been investment, not inflation. The critics' unease is real but tracks the failures the condition names — creep-degraded uses and the occasional unbacked mint — not minting as such.
- **5.7** What the verdict buys philosophy and society respectively (one paragraph, split): for philosophy, a discipline internal to the genre (coinage papers that fill the clauses are better papers); for society, concepts that arrive ready to run on — recognizable, countable, filable. The two benefits are one benefit seen from two sides.

## §6 Objections (900w, 7 paras)

- **6.1** Structural objection, steelmanned: the deepest structural wrongs resist assignment — that is the point of the social connection analysis (verify — #9); if clause (iii) quietly demands a respondent, the condition disqualifies structural coinages and lands conservative, as inflation-rhetoric tends to (verify — #15). State it as the opponent would.
- **6.2** Reply, with the concession held at the close: clause (iii) was built at B2→B3 precisely to demand shape, not respondent — and the social connection model *is* a redress-shape: shared, forward-looking, political responsibility (verify — #9). Structural coinages backed by such theory pass. Where even shape-level response is inarticulable, the coinage is not yet backed — and that is the right verdict, the same unease *Ambient Injustice* elicits; the condition asks of coiners only the work the best structural theorists already do.
- **6.3** Equity objection, steelmanned: the practice's own literature shows dominant audiences already refusing uptake of concepts coined by marginalized speakers — that refusal has a name, contributory injustice (verify — #12). A backing norm hands the refusers a respectable dismissal ("insufficiently backed"), and its burden falls precisely on coiners working from inside the hermeneutical gaps the practice exists to close. The condition would institutionalize the wrong it prices. State it with full force — this is the objection the essay most owes an answer.
- **6.4** Reply + concession: the condition prices coinages, not coiners, and its clauses are claimant-serving — capacity, mechanism, and redress-shape are exactly what makes a marginalized claim *filable* rather than dismissible; a backed coinage is harder, not easier, to wave away, and meeting the condition costs a paragraph, not a gate. Concede: any norm can be weaponized in application; distinguish the norm from its abuse, and note what the norm gives the abused party that the status quo does not — a determinate reply (fill the clauses and the dismissal fails).
- **6.5** Genus objection, steelmanned: a checklist is not a theory; verdictive labels need grounds — an account of what makes all these *injustice* — and backing seems to let coiners skip the genus question (the essay's own strongest internal rival, argued at strength).
- **6.6** Reply + concession: backing is the genus-commitment in operational form — each clause forces the coiner to say whom this wrongs, how, and what answering would be; that is what having grounds *is* at the point of coining, and the field's history confirms the order — the genus debate for the epistemic case proceeded productively after the coinages, on the material they provided (verify — #11). Concede the deeper point: the misfortune/injustice boundary is historically mobile and rigid rules mislocate it (verify — #21) — which is why B3 conditions present serviceability, not eternal metaphysics; the condition inherits the lesson instead of fighting it.
- **6.7** Reflexivity objection, short: does the condition pass its own analogue? Capacity — claimants at large, as claimants; mechanism — unbacked minting's manufacture of unanswerable claims (§3.9); redress-shape — the condition itself, a naming norm any coiner can satisfy in a paragraph. It passes, and its cheapness is the point: the Investment View polices nothing; it prices.

## §7 Conclusion (250w, 2 paras)

- **7.1** Restatement + enumeration: the question was whether the coining boom is good or bad; the answer required finding what coinages spend. Three moves: validity does not dilute, so the Inflation View fails as stated; uptake is scarce and owed, so the Abundance View fails too; the Backing Condition separates investment from expenditure, and the practice's exemplars sit on the investment side. Scope re-marked: comparative, defeasible, no licensing.
- **7.2** Braided close: return to *Dismissed* and *Ambient Injustice*. What separated them was never sincerity, nor the reality of the bads they name. One coinage told us what to look for, whom to believe, and what helping would mean; the other told us only that things are bad. The difference is backing — and it is the difference between adding to the common store of justice and drawing it down.
