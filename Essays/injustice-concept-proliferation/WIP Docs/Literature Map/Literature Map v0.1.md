## D. The open niche (and the three nearest neighbors distinguished)

Nobody, as far as Stage 1–2 searching shows, gives a **normative verdict on the proliferation of named injustice concepts as a practice**, grounded in an account of what injustice-claims compete for:

1. **Kitsik 2025** is about inflation (stretching), not proliferation (coining); expressly stops short of a verdict; identifies attention as the stake but does not normativize it or derive a coining norm.
2. **Alston 1984 / Griffin 2008 / Wellman 1999** concern *rights* (mostly legal-institutional), not injustice-*concepts*; their quality-control proposals are procedural/legal or semantic, not grounded in the claim-making analysis; and the injustice-concept literature has not absorbed them.
3. **Smith & Archer 2020** make attention the *object* of one more injustice claim; they do not turn the point reflexively on the practice of coining itself.

**The essay's niche:** the intersection — a claim-theoretic account of why uptake scarcity (not validity, not meaning) is what injustice coinages draw on, yielding the Backing Condition as an adequacy norm, a creep/proliferation distinction, and a conditional-positive verdict on the actual practice.
