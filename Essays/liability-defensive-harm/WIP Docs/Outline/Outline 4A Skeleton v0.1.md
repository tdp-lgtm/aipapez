## §1 Introduction (600)

1.1 Open cold on three cases, each told in two or three sentences with its widely shared verdict:
- *Mistaken Attacker*: a resident reasonably takes a harmless twin for an escaped killer and
  attacks; the twin may fight back, and a police officer who knows the truth may stop the resident.
- *Conscientious Driver*: a careful driver's car veers toward a pedestrian; many deny that the
  driver is liable.
- *Bluff*: a prankster points an unloaded gun and shouts that he will kill; the victim breaks his
  leg; the prankster may not fight back and is owed nothing.
1.2 The puzzle. The first verdict makes liability follow the facts against a reasonable belief.
The second makes it follow the evidence against the facts. The third makes a harmless man bear the
cost of an appearance. Each major account keeps two verdicts and loses one.
1.3 Thesis, compressed and named: the Conferral Account. Liability follows the entitlement the
conduct needs, and who can confer it. The two nearest neighbours are distinguished in the same
paragraph: Quong's Moral Status Account (same verdicts on the first two cases, a different
condition and rationale) and Burri's view (same enforcement framing, but it clears the resident).
1.4 Scope, one sentence each: liability is not blame; the resident's evidence-relative permission
is untouched; the account settles whether one is liable, not how much.
1.5 Roadmap.

Exit state: the reader holds the three cases, the puzzle, the thesis, and its two neighbours.

## §2 The standpoint problem (800)

2.1 Accounts that fix liability by the facts: Thomson's violation view and McMahan's
responsibility view. They get Mistaken Attacker right, but make the careful driver liable (and,
for Thomson, the falling man). They need a second clause for Bluff.
2.2 Accounts that fix liability by the agent's evidence: Ferzan's culpability view and Burri's
subjective-duty view. They clear the driver, but also clear the resident, and so cannot explain
Police Intervention or Duped Soldiers; Burri accepts this cost.
2.3 The mixed test: Quong's Moral Status Account, quoted. It gets the resident and the driver
right. Clark shows its dependence test is too narrow (the grenade into the warehouse; the hiker)
and arbitrary (the two trolley cases). It also treats bluffers as liable, which runs into Clark's
necessity, proportionality, incentive and aggregation problems.
2.4 Diagnosis: all three families look for liability's standard in a standpoint (the world's or
the agent's). The enforcement premise is stated here, shared with Thomson, Quong and Burri: one is
liable when about to violate a claim the other may enforce. The live question becomes what an
agent's conduct must have to avoid violating a claim. Pivot.

Exit state: the reader sees why a principled mix of standpoints is needed and what would count as
one.

## §3 The Conferral Account (1,150) — CRUX SECTION

3.1 Display 1 (the view).
3.2 Acting upon versus imposing risk. Define "acting upon": aiming at a particular person's harm,
knowingly harming her, or singling her out to bear a risk. Imposing risk: exposing whoever may be
affected. Place the resident, the driver, the grenade-thrower and the hiker.
3.3 Who can confer an entitlement over a person. The consent parallel: no one can consent for
another, and a reasonable belief in consent is not consent. The forfeiture parallel: forfeiture
follows the forfeiter's own conduct. The balance of claims overrides a claim without altering it.
Legitimate authority, within its jurisdiction, is the one source that may act on evidence. So the
agent's evidence confers nothing.
3.4 The independence argument. A claim against being acted upon whose content was mere due care
would protect a person only as she appears. The twin's protection would depend on his brother's
crimes, a warning and a storm; that protects an appearance, not a person.
3.5 Why a licence to impose risk is conferred by due care. It must be satisfiable under
uncertainty, and a licence that lapsed whenever the risk materialised would be none. The driver's
single trip is justified by its own prospects with due care (Frowe 2022, token not type). The
pedestrian was never singled out.
3.6 The responsibility condition is derived, not stipulated: the capacity to forfeit is the
capacity to waive. So neither the falling man nor the drugged aggressor is liable.

Exit state: the view stated, its distinction drawn, the conferral claim defended from two
directions, the risk half explained, the responsibility condition derived.

## §4 The argument and the cases (1,100)

4.1 Display 2: the central argument in five steps, with defence locations.
4.2 Mistaken Attacker, Police Intervention and Duped Soldiers: liable. The twin's resistance
enforces an intact claim; the resident is blameless and owes repair.
4.3 The careful driver, the ambulance driver, the cell phone case, and the falling man: not liable.
Modularity: whether the pedestrian may kill the driver turns on the prerogative question (Quong
2009 against Tadros's priority of the status quo); on either view the driver is not liable.
4.4 Justification defeats liability, and the account explains why rather than stipulating it: the
bomber's entitlement (the balance of claims) exists.
4.5 Pre-emptive defence against "Quong restated". Three cases where the verdicts differ, with
Clark's verdicts where Clark is right: the grenade into the warehouse (liable), the trolley pair
(the same verdict in both), and the hiker (not liable, like the driver). Then the difference in
rationale.

Exit state: the argument is on the page, and the verdicts are shown, including where the account
departs from Quong.

## §5 Appearances and authority (850)

5.1 Clark's challenge: bluffers and futile attackers bear liability's upshots without posing
threats; the orthodox answer faces four problems.
5.2 Estoppel, explained in one sentence at first use. Knowingly inducing another's reliance is
one's own act, so by the conferral claim it can alter one's position: it removes the standing to
enforce the claim without removing the claim. This recovers Clark's unbundled result from one
ground.
5.3 Contrasts: the twin (no act of his) and an actor rehearsing unaware of any audience (no
knowing inducement) are not estopped. The actual attacker who meets reasonable but unnecessary
force is.
5.4 Why estoppel avoids the orthodox problems: the claim survives, so necessity and
proportionality still bind, the informed bystander should speak, and aggregation counts infringed
claims.
5.5 Legitimate authority, scoped: officers acting under fair procedure within their jurisdiction
confer entitlements on evidence. Soldiers have no such authority over foreigners, so Duped Soldiers
remain liable even under a legitimate state. This explains the institutional exception Quong notes
but leaves open.

Exit state: the appearance and authority cases are fitted to the account from the same principle.

## §6 Objections (1,150)

6.1 (A) Fair allocation, the dedicated objection, reply-layered (Bolinger; Ripstein): reasonable
mistakes are part of a fair allocation of the risk of unjust harm, so the twin's claim is only a
claim to reasonable treatment. Reply: the contractualist comparison. Response: defenders must act
on judgment. Reply to the response: the account keeps their permission; it denies only the claim
to prevail over an innocent they have wrongly attacked. (verify — awaiting sources #3, #5)
6.2 (B) Control and fairness (Burri; Ferzan): liability should rest on an agential failure.
Reply: this is not a sanction but the intact claim of the other; the resident controls whether to
take another's fate into his hands; the umbrella analogy. Concede that culpability matters for
degree and selection.
6.3 (C) The driver (McMahan; Frowe 2022; Lang 2022): he chose to set a lethal machine in motion,
or the line between him and the resident cannot be held. Reply: he acts on a valid licence and
singles no one out; McMahan himself calls the case weak-intuition; Lang's arguments target Quong's
line. (verify — awaiting sources #2, #4)

Exit state: the strongest objections met; concessions registered.

## §7 Conclusion (300)

7.1 Restate the view and its three claims: conferral; two kinds of entitlement; estoppel by one's

7.2 Scope, plus one flagged open question: how culpability fixes the degree of liability.
