## The problem the candidates answer

Every live account fixes one standpoint for liability and then meets a counterexample from the
other side. Fact-relative accounts (Thomson; McMahan's Responsibility Account) make faultless
agents liable: the Conscientious Driver, and for Thomson even the falling man. Evidence-relative or
fault accounts (Ferzan; Burri's Disrespect for Rights Account) exonerate the Resident who
reasonably but wrongly attacks the harmless twin, and so cannot explain Police Intervention or
Duped Soldiers. Quong's Moral Status Account mixes the standpoints, but Clark shows its
"dependence" test is too narrow (Warehouse, Scare the Bear) and arbitrary (Waiting on Trolley 1
vs 2). Merely apparent attackers (Jam, Bluff) force every account into a disjunction or into Clark's
"partial forfeiture". A new account must say, on principled grounds, *when* liability is
fact-sensitive and when it is evidence-sensitive, and why.

## Candidates

**C1 — The Conferral Account.** *I argue that* a person is liable to defensive harm when, as a
responsible agent, she acts on an entitlement to harm another that only the other (by consent or by
her own wrong) or the actual balance of claims could confer, and no such entitlement exists — or
when she imposes risk without due care; *against the view that* liability has a single standpoint
(fact-relative responsibility, evidence-relative disrespect, or Quong's dependence test); *on the
grounds that* entitlements to harm others cannot be conferred by the harmer's own evidence, while a
licence to impose risk is by its nature conferred on evidence. Type: new account plus a diagnosis
(the fact/evidence dispute dissolves once liability borrows its standard from the entitlement
exercised). Originality hypothesis: no account derives liability's fact- or evidence-sensitivity
from *who can confer* the entitlement the agent acts on; this differs from Quong's MSA in its
condition (the entitlement acted on, not counterfactual dependence), and so covers Warehouse and
Waiting on Trolley, treats Scare the Bear like the Conscientious Driver, and handles apparent
attackers by estoppel rather than by a second ground of liability.

**C2 — The Knowledge-Norm Account.** *I argue that* a person is liable to defensive harm when she
harms, or risks harm to, another in reliance on a proposition she does not know, where the act is
proper only if that proposition is known; *against* evidence-relative and culpability views; *on
the grounds that* knowledge norms on action independently mark acts that are excusable yet
improper, and forfeiture tracks impropriety, not blame. Type: new account imported from
epistemology. Originality hypothesis: searches found no use of knowledge norms of action in the
defensive-liability debate.

**C3 — The Guarantee Account.** *I argue that* liability is the imminent breach of an obligation of
*result* owed to the victim (or the culpable breach of an obligation of *means*), and that
obligations of result arise when an agent takes another's fate into her control; *against* uniform
objective or subjective accounts; *on the grounds that* the civil-law distinction between
obligations of result and of means explains the pattern of strictness across tort and defence.
Type: reframing. Originality hypothesis: the vocabulary is unused here, but the substance is C1's.

**C4 — The Cheapest-Avoider Account.** *I argue that* the unavoidable harm falls, as a matter of
liability, on the party who could have avoided the conflict at the lower legitimate cost; *against*
responsibility and culpability accounts; *on the grounds that* fairness assigns avoidable conflicts
to their cheaper avoider. Type: new account (a moralised Calabresian criterion).

**C5 — The Informed-Duty Account.** *I argue that* one is liable to exactly the harm one would be
duty-bound to accept, if fully informed and able, to avert the threat one poses; *against*
forfeiture accounts; *on the grounds that* rights not to be harmed track the duties one has, or
would have, to avert threats. Type: new account. Originality hypothesis: weak (Tadros 2012 and 2021
already hold this).

**C6 — The Selection Account.** *I argue that* liability arises when one selects an identified
person for harm on a justification that fails, but not when one imposes a statistical risk under an
ex ante justification; *against* the Responsibility Account and MSA; *on the grounds that* ex ante
and ex post justifications answer to different claims. Type: new account. Originality hypothesis:
moderate; its content is the harm/risk half of C1.

**C7 — The Estoppel Account.** *I argue that* an agent is liable when the practical principle her
own conduct manifests licenses the defensive response to her; *against* forfeiture accounts; *on
the grounds that* no one may complain of treatment she herself claims licence to give. Type: new
account. Originality hypothesis: moderate, but Clark 2025 already uses hypocrisy for the standing
upshot.

**C8 — Standpoint-Indexed Liability.** *I argue that* there are two liability relations,
fact-relative and evidence-relative, with distinct roles (compensation and third parties versus
in-the-moment resistance); *against* single-notion accounts; *on the grounds that* Parfit's senses
of wrongness each have a liability analogue. Type: distinction. Originality hypothesis: low
(Frowe 2010's decoupling; Chehtman 2017's "mere permissibility"; Clark 2025's unbundling).

**C9 — The Risk-Pool Account.** *I argue that* a faultless threatener is liable only when the risk
she imposed lies outside a reciprocal risk pool that her victim shares, while deliberate harms lie
outside every pool; *against* both the Responsibility Account and MSA on the Conscientious Driver;
*on the grounds that* reciprocity of risk (Fletcher's paradigm in tort) fixes who bears a
materialised risk. Type: new account of the risk half. Originality hypothesis: reciprocity is
mentioned in passing (McMahan, Ferzan, Lazar) but no search hit develops it as a liability account.

**C10 — The Address Account.** *I argue that* liability arises from addressing another, through
conduct, with an unwarranted demand that she submit; *against* Ferzan's culpability view and
Bolinger's signalling view; *on the grounds that* aggression is a second-personal act. Type: new
account. Originality hypothesis: low-moderate; overlaps Ferzan's "communicative act" and C1.

**C11 — The Present-Duty Account.** *I argue that* liability is the enforceability of a present,
dischargeable duty (to desist, divert, or absorb) that the target is now failing to discharge;
*against* backward-looking forfeiture accounts; *on the grounds that* enforcement targets present
non-compliance, which also fixes when liability begins. Type: new account. Originality hypothesis:
moderate; close to Tadros's and Clark's duty accounts.

**C12 — The Public-Authority Account.** *I argue that* mistaken force grounds liability when
private but not when exercised under legitimate public procedure; *against* MSA's unexplained
institutional exception; *on the grounds that* no private person may be judge in her own cause,
while fair public procedures can confer evidence-based authority. Type: extension. Originality
hypothesis: moderate; best as a component of C1.

**C13 — The Trying Account.** *I argue that* one is liable when trying to harm a nonliable person
(or culpably risking harm), whether or not one succeeds or is at fault; *against* causal and
threat-based views; *on the grounds that* the attempt, not the outcome, is what the defender
resists. Type: new account. Originality hypothesis: low-moderate; subsumed by C1.

**C14 — Liability Pluralism.** *I argue that* there is no single ground of liability: each upshot
(no counter-defence, third-party partiality, no compensation, no standing to complain) has its own
ground; *against* all monistic accounts; *on the grounds that* the upshots come apart in apparent-
attacker cases. Type: diagnosis. Originality hypothesis: low; Clark 2025 and Tadros 2021 go most of
the way.
