## Part A — The accounts and their stated rationales

**Thomson 1991 (rights-based / "VIOLATION").** A person who will otherwise violate your right not to
be killed lacks a right that you not kill him; neither fault nor agency is needed for violating a
right (Villainous Aggressor, Innocent Aggressor, Innocent Threat all "Yes" cases) {307}–{309}.
Bystanders (not causally involved, or only minimally) keep their rights: Substitution-, Use-, and
Riding-Roughshod-over-a-Bystander cases are "No" cases {296}–{297}, {305}–{306}. Irrelevance of
fault/intention to permissibility {301}–{302}. Fighting back and third-party defence track the same
fact {310}–{313}. Attacks the agent-relative permission view ("whatever I may do to save my own life
I may surely do to save the life of someone I love"; the boring man) {314}–{315}. Fn. 12 {309}: Kamm —
the falling man, could he alter his fall, would be required to absorb a larger cost than a bystander.

**McMahan 2005a (Responsibility Account).** Liability ≠ desert {348}. Criticises Rights-Based
(Tactical Bomber symmetry {350}–{351}) and Culpability (futile attempts; basement-window villains;
"culpability for what?" line-drawing {352}–{355}). Conscientious Driver liable: voluntarily set a
risky object in motion; costs indivisible, "fair that he should suffer them all"; should share if
divisible {355}–{356}. RA: "moral responsibility, through action that lacks objective justification,
for a threat of unjust harm" {356}; liability scalar; tort analogy, "preventive justice… ex ante"
{357}. Mother of attacker (too remote); torture-memo official (remote but liable) {358}. Cell Phone
Operator not responsible — foreseeability fails; "risk-imposing activity" condition {359}–{360}.
Ambulance driver: reasons for driving form a spectrum; no principled line {360}–{361}. Justification
defeats liability; defence permitted against justified infringement; symmetry {361}–{362}. Why not
kill falling man/Cell Phone Operator? They will not *wrong* you — responsibility is a precondition
of wronging {363}. Resident "at least knows that he poses a threat" {364}. Reciprocal drivers:
causation necessary {365}.

**McMahan 2005b (reply to Ferzan).** Culpability view's line-drawing ("arbitrary, or ad hoc")
{370}ff.; objective account of justification; Roulette challenge. (Skimmed.)

**McMahan 2011 (war).** Liability a function of (1) expected wrongful harm, (2) causal contribution,
(3) foreseeability/mens rea, (4) excuses, (5) others more responsible, (6) effectiveness {4}.
Comparative dimension: a minimally responsible threatener is liable to an indivisible harm if the
victim bears no responsibility; "Liability… arises only when harm is unavoidable and must be
distributed" {7}. Numbers and "moral remainder" (partial compensation owed to the minimally
responsible) {9}–{10}. Epistemic dimension: an unthreatening unjust combatant is "responsible for
appearing to pose a threat"; practical joker — the apparent victim does not become liable {11}–{12}.

**Ferzan 2012 (Culpability).** Liability requires culpable aggression: (1a) intention to
purposefully/knowingly/recklessly kill, no justification/excuse, and necessity; or (1b) culpable
conduct one is aware may lead the defender to believe (1a); then (2) not wronged by force intended
to repel the *perceived* attack {319}, {346}. Against Thomson: Alcove vs Fat Man (no principled
difference), fundamental right lost by misfortune, numbers (100 babies), compensation {322}–{324}.
Against McMahan: Duff (responsibility ≠ liability); foreseeability is description-relative (Fearless
Fred, Nervous Nellie); reciprocal risk and moral luck; pedestrian with a gun or mines; poor Alice
must drive; "it is not that Albert cannot control… but that it is unfair to ask Albert to so
control"; "The mere foresight that harm may occur… should not be sufficient… when you do all that
we may fairly ask of you" {325}–{331}. Act requirement: Fortune Teller (Thomson would license killing
Ivan at birth); Total Recall (on McMahan, Dena liable before the failure) {335}–{336}. Objective
necessity not required: Involuntary Roulette; Unloaded Gun; liability as a *communicative act*;
contract analogy (objective manifestation of assent) {338}–{342}. Responsiveness: Unknown Attack —
liable only to force intended to repel {344}–{345}.

**Quong 2012 (Moral Status Account).** Against culpability: variable culpability (duress; Albert
can't be killed at 70/100), threshold culpability (Delia vs Eric), Mistaken Attacker {228}–{232}.
Against MRA: distributive fairness/luck-egalitarian rationale; Conscientious Driver, Ambulance
Driver; rejects McMahan's premise (2) via an agent-relative prerogative; third parties may not
intervene when both are nonliable {233}–{238}. Evidence-relative account: Police Intervention,
Duped Soldiers {238}–{241}. MSA: liable for choosing X iff (a) minimal responsibility and (b) the
evidence-relative permissibility of X "depends either on the assumption that those who are harmed…
are liable to the harm, or else on false moral beliefs" {246}–{247}. Rationale: reciprocity;
"gamble with the moral rights of others"; "go out on a moral limb" {243}–{246}. Justified Bomber:
justification defeats liability {248}–{249}. Accidentally Permissible Killing (Frank/Greta) {250}.
Nonresponsible threats: not liable; prerogative permits killing; third parties may not intervene
{253}–{254}. Mistaken Bomber (mistake about numbers): not liable {254}–{255}.

**Quong 2009.** Agent-relative permission grounded in the agent-relative value of one's life;
Means Principle: X *uses* Y when Y's presence is causally necessary for X's goal; so you may kill
Innocent Aggressors/Threats (you keep what you'd have without them) but not Bystanders
(Sleepwalking/pinch; neighbour) {274}–{276}.

**Burri 2022 (enforcement framing).** Liability is local; distributive justice is global (85-year-old
pedestrian vs disadvantaged 30-year-old driver; my unredeemed past doesn't cancel an attacker's
liability) {44}. Enforcement view: threatening a rights violation is necessary and sufficient for
liability {46}. Accounts trade protection-as-rightholder against control-as-would-be-violator; MRA
and MSA give too little control; favours a subjective-impermissibility account; concedes Duped
Soldiers is counterintuitive for it; doubts a wholly satisfactory account is within reach {49}–{51}.
Fn. 8: foreseeably risk-imposing activities = activities with duties of care (Burri 2020).

**Burri 2025 (Disrespect for Rights Account, DRA).** Hybrid rights: patient-side protection is
objective, agent-side duties are subjective {19}–{22}. DRA: liable to what defenders *reasonably*
consider necessary and proportionate iff one disrespects another's rights by acting against the
subjective requirements {25}. Duties stretch over time (declining police advice); Duress doesn't
block liability; genuine threat irrelevant; Poisoned Cake (disrespect without threat); Practical
Joke (right to feel safe) {22}–{27}. Hard results she accepts: Shooter with Immoral Intentions and
Disrespectful Victim (Shooter liable to Victim, not vice versa) {27}; Eggshell Skull Eddie (Puncher
liable to be killed) {28}–{29}; Resident "acts disrespectfully" (should not have opened the door)
{28}; Duped Soldiers not liable {31}–{32}. Bolinger's aggression-signalling restriction {28}. Harm
Triangle and "within the risk" {29}. Claims DRA is non-disjunctive and grounds liability in
agential failure {19}, {31}.

**Tadros 2012 (Duty and Liability).** Liability = lacking a right not to be harmed for goal G
because one has an enforceable duty to bear that harm for G; no forfeiture needed (tax,
conscription) {157}–{159}. Duty to rescue grounds liability (Pond Rescue: finger yes, foot no);
liability without choice (paralysed; unconscious — "to the extent that duty would require of her if
she were able") {160}–{165}. Liability to be killed without responsibility: saving humanity; Bertha
and the tiger lottery; Double Hit Man 2 {165}–{167}. Boulder: no agent-relative prerogative; the
priority of the status quo {169}. Civilians: duties from causal contribution + benefit; Remote
Control Plane (may divert onto the Zoolon builder, but not use him as a shield) {170}–{172}.

**Tadros 2021 (Two Grounds).** Responsibility (strength of agential connection) and culpability
(vices) are independent grounds; liability scalar; individual vs *selective* liability {100}–{104}.
Justified risk-taker acting for the victim's own sake is not liable {101}. Dog owner liable though
permissibly risking {101}. Duress (Xavier/Yolanda) against Quong's stringency-only view {107};
Involuntary Intoxication; Supercars; Two Pushers; Two Killers {111}–{115}. Rights not to be harmed to
avert a threat track the duties one has (or would have) to avert it; culpability strengthens the
duty {116}–{117}.

**Clark n.d. ("As If"; *Free & Equal* per acknowledgements — verify).** Formalises MSA (Quong 2020):
liable iff (i) poses threat or apparent threat to R, and (ii) lacks evidence-relative permission,
or (iii) has it *only because* the evidence says V lacks R {180}. Too narrow: Scare the Bear (mistake
about whether anyone is harmed), Warehouse (mistake about a lesser-evil justification) {184}–{185}.
Arbitrary: Waiting on Trolley 1 vs 2 (backup justification) {186}–{188}. "As if" read objectively
collapses into VIOLATION {191}–{192}.

**Clark 2025 (Ethics 135(3); partial forfeiture).** Challenge of Merely Apparent Attackers: Jam and
Bluff bear the four upshots (no counter-defence, no third-party defence, no compensation, little
standing to complain); False Testimony and Evil Twin do not {194}–{198}. Orthodoxy (disjunctive
accounts: Ferzan, McMahan/Frowe, Jorgensen) faces Necessity (Easy Defense; Two Snipers; Redux),
Proportionality (Overkill), Perverse Incentives (Mixed Bluff), Aggregation (Tracks) {200}–{206}.
Partial Forfeiture: apparent attackers keep the right, lose its contingent "perks"; via Liability by
Fairness or Liability by Duty ("(i) there is harm that must be distributed and (ii) they have a duty
to assume (a share of) that harm") {207}–{215}. Lifeguard; complicity {213}. Vehicular Bluff: naked
rights still matter {217}–{218}. Necessity Mistake {220}.

**Frowe 2010 (practical account).** Permission to defend is subjective (reasonable belief in own
innocence and that the target will kill); liability is objective and requires culpability
(malicious intention to kill, to expose to risk, or to make V believe) {60}, {68}–{70}. Actor may
counter-defend; Incompetent Murderer may not {73}–{76}. Very Incompetent Murderer: liable, but not
permissibly killed {73} fn. 29.

**Frowe 2008a/b; McMahan 1994; Tadros 2014.** (Skimmed; notes to follow if engaged.) Frowe:
obstructors are threats; innocent threats vs bystanders. McMahan 1994: innocence in war. Tadros
2014: vicarious liability of noncombatants via fairness and benefit.
