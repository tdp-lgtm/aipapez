## 1. The landscape: five families of account

| Family | Core condition | Holders | Standpoint | Verdicts on the four test cases (Resident / Driver / falling man / bluffer) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rights-violation | Liable iff one will otherwise violate the victim's right; no agency needed | Thomson 1991 [T]; Uniacke, Leverick (via Ferzan [T]) | fact | liable / liable / liable / not addressed |
| Responsibility | Liable iff morally responsible, through objectively unjustified action, for an unjust threat; foreseeability; comparative dimension | McMahan 2005a, 2011 [T]; Otsuka 1994, 2016 [R14]; Gordon-Solmon 2018 [A, R11]; Frowe 2014 [R13] | fact (with foreseeability) | liable / liable / not / liable for appearing (McMahan 2011) |

Cross-cutting positions: Clark 2025 [T] (partial forfeiture for merely apparent attackers: rights
kept, "perks" lost); Bolinger 2021 [A, R3] (reasonable mistakes as fair distribution of the risk of
unjust harm; aggression-signalling); Ripstein 1996 [A, R5] (reasonableness = which party should

liable dichotomy too rigid); Renzo 2017 [A, R12] (forfeiture needs a further principle).

## 2. Where the field is stuck

Each family fixes one standpoint and meets a counterexample from the other:
- **Fact-relative accounts** make faultless risk-takers liable (Conscientious Driver, Ambulance
  Driver; Quong 2012 {235}–{238}; Ferzan 2012 {325}–{331}; Burri 2022 {44}).
- **Evidence-relative accounts** exonerate the reasonably mistaken attacker, and so cannot explain
  Police Intervention or Duped Soldiers (Quong 2012 {239}–{241}). Burri concedes the Duped Soldiers
  verdict is counterintuitive (2022 {51}) and later bites it (2025 {31}–{33}).
- **The mixed Moral Status Account** has the right shape but the wrong test: its "dependence"
  condition is too narrow (Warehouse, Scare the Bear) and arbitrary (Waiting on Trolley 1 vs 2)
  (Clark "As If" {184}–{188}). Read objectively, its guiding idea collapses into Thomson's view
  ({191}–{192}). Lang [A] and Frowe [A] press further.
- **Apparent attackers** force every family into a disjunction (Ferzan's 1b; McMahan's
  responsibility for appearing; Jorgensen's signalling) or into Clark's partial forfeiture, which
  still needs an account of *why* the bluffer loses the perks (Clark gives two optional "deep
  stories", fairness and duty).
- **Burri (2022 {51})**: "I doubt that a wholly satisfactory account of liability … is within our
  reach." This is the open challenge.

## 3. The open niche

No account says, from one principle, *when* liability is fact-sensitive and when it is
evidence-sensitive. The Conferral Account's answer: liability borrows its standard from the
entitlement the agent acts on. Entitlements over a particular person are conferred only by her own
acts, by the balance of claims, or by public authority, so they are fact-dependent. A licence to
impose risk is conferred by due care, so it is evidence-dependent. The same principle, "only a
person's own acts change her normative position", also explains estoppel for knowing bluffers.

## 4. Nearest neighbours and how the essay differs

1. **Quong's Moral Status Account (the named rival).** Same verdict shape on the Resident and the
   Driver. It differs in the *condition* (the entitlement acted on, not counterfactual dependence
   of evidence-relative permissibility), and so in verdicts: Warehouse, Waiting on Trolley 1,
   Mistaken Bomber, bluffers, institutional roles. It also differs in the *rationale* (self-
   authorship of normative positions and the kind of entitlement exercised, not "treating as if
   lacking moral status"). The essay must show it is not the MSA restated. Clark's objections are
   the lever.
2. **Burri's DRA.** Shares the enforcement framing and the "hybrid" idea that rights are objective
   on the patient side. It differs by making the *agent-side* duties objective for acting upon a
   person and subjective only for risk. That is exactly the division Burri's uniform subjectivism
   denies, and it is what saves Duped Soldiers and Police Intervention.
3. **Bolinger / Ripstein (fair allocation of the risk of mistake).** The rival route for the crux:
   they treat the mistaken attacker's permission as part of a fair risk allocation. The essay's
   reply is the independence argument plus the contractualist comparison (crux memo, step 5). This
   is a must-engage: at least one of them is the "strongest objection" section. Awaiting texts (R3,
   R5).
4. **Clark 2025 (partial forfeiture).** The essay *adopts* the unbundled result for bluffers and
   supplies its ground (estoppel by knowing manifestation as self-conferral), replacing Clark's two
   optional deep stories with one.
5. **Ferzan 2012/2017 (communicative aggression; the bluff).** Source of the manifestation idea and
   the contract analogy (Restatement §19 {340}). The essay limits manifestation to estoppel, not
   full liability, and so avoids Clark's necessity, proportionality, incentive and aggregation
   objections.
6. **Tadros (duty, two grounds).** The essay's account is compatible with Tadros's scalar and
   selective liability as a secondary matter. Flag in a note that culpability may bear on
   *selection*.

## 5. Originality verdict

**OPEN (moderate confidence).** Eighteen Stage 1 searches and three record checks found no account
that (a) grounds liability's fact- or evidence-sensitivity in who can confer the entitlement acted
on, (b) splits acting-upon from risk on that basis, or (c) derives the bluffer's partial forfeiture
from the same self-conferral principle.

Risk register:
- (i) Quong 2020 or 2023 [R1, R6] may frame the MSA in authority or entitlement terms. Check on
  receipt; if so, recast the contribution as the corrected condition plus the estoppel unification.
- (ii) Lang 2022 [R2] may already propose a "minimal" account in this spirit. Check on receipt.
- (iii) Bolinger 2021 [R3] may contain the independence point and reject it. Engage directly.
- (iv) Ripstein's Kantian tort theory contains the use/care structure. The essay credits it and
  claims only its application to defensive liability, plus the conferral argument.
