   classification paragraph (§3, third paragraph) and say what the account adds: the line decides

   crux arguments.

   "Finally, *Bluff*.", "The verdicts follow.", "Contrast the twin."; keep at most three.
4. **Optional engagement.** Ferzan 2012, 682 n. 49 rejects Otsuka's "gamble" rationale for liability
   (Otsuka 1994, 91, as quoted there) and argues that a reasonable defender also gambles if he does not
   defend himself. This is the sharpest form of the control objection; consider using it in §6
   (B). Thomson 1991, 285 ("who are you, private person that you are, to be dishing out punishment")
   is a possible echo for the argument from authority; use only if it earns its place.
5. **Burri 2025 §4 point 4** (following Bolinger 2021): defenders act disrespectfully if they do not
   base targeting at least partly on the target's conduct. That bears on the estoppel section (the
   twin's conduct was not attack-like). Engage once Bolinger 2021 (#3) arrives.
