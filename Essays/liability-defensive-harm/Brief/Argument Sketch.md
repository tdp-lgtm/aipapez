## Sketch A — The Conferral Account (rank 1, recommended)

**Thesis (P/Q/R).** A person is liable to defensive harm when, as a responsible agent, she acts on
an entitlement to harm another that only the other (by consent or by her own wrong) or the actual
balance of claims could confer, and no such entitlement exists; or when she imposes risk on others
without due care. This is against the view that liability has one standpoint, whether fact-relative
(McMahan), evidence-relative (Burri), or Quong's dependence test. The grounds: entitlements to harm
another cannot be conferred by the harmer's own evidence, whereas a licence to impose risk is by its
nature conferred on evidence.

**Premises.** (1) To harm a person permissibly, one needs an entitlement: her consent, her
liability, or an overriding balance of claims. (2) Consent and liability are conferred only by the
person's own will or own wrong; the balance of claims is a fact about the claims. None is conferred
by what the harmer's evidence says. (3) So a reasonable but mistaken harmer acts without
entitlement: the victim's claim stands and she may enforce it, which is what liability to her
defensive force consists in. (4) A licence to impose risk, by contrast, must be satisfiable by an
agent who does not know how things will turn out, so it is conferred by due care on the evidence.
(5) So a careful risk-imposer whose risk misfires has exceeded no entitlement and is not liable.

**Payoff.** One principle yields the contested verdicts. The Resident and the Duped Soldiers are
liable, which saves Police Intervention. The Conscientious Driver, the Ambulance Driver and the Cell
Phone Operator are not. Nonresponsible threats are not liable, because only responsible agents act
on entitlements. The justified bomber is not liable, because his entitlement exists. It also
answers Clark's three objections to Quong: Warehouse comes out liable, the two Waiting on Trolley
cases get the same verdict, and Scare the Bear is treated like the driver. Bluffers and futile
attackers are not liable; they are *estopped* by the claim they manifest, which recovers Clark's
"partial forfeiture" without a second ground of liability. Legitimate public procedures can confer
evidence-based entitlements, which explains Quong's unexplained institutional exception.

**Main objection.** Why isn't the defender's entitlement just a licence to take reasonable
defensive risks, conferred on evidence the way the driver's is? This is the route taken by
Bolinger and by Ripstein's fair-allocation view.

**Crux.** Show that claims against being *acted upon* protect a person as a particular person.
Their content cannot be made relative to others' evidence without making her standing depend on how
she appears. Claims about *risk*, by contrast, protect only a fair share of general safety, and
their content is due care. Candidate solution: an independence argument. Also answer Lang's claim
that the driver and the mistaken attacker cannot be distinguished (text to be requested).

**Venue fit.** A named new account with a clear rival (Quong's Moral Status Account) and a
diagnosis of why the field keeps splitting over the fact–evidence choice. It fits 6,000 words: one
principle, one crux, three case clusters.
