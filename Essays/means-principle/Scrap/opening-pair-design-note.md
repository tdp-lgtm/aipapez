# Opening pair: design note (AI working note, 2026-09-22)

## Problem with the Plan v0.4 candidate

- In the first case the neighbour dies because there is time to save only the five. That is a letting-die.
- In the second case the firefighter crushes him. That is a killing.
- So the pair varies doing and allowing as well as spending. A reader can explain it by the doing/allowing distinction alone, and the puzzle loses its force.

## Design constraints

1. Stakes are equal: five saved, one dies.
2. Doing and allowing are held fixed: both cases are killings.
3. His body is used in both, in the same way: it holds the trapdoor.
4. He dies in both.
5. Only one thing varies: whether his death is the cost of what his body supplies.
6. The verdicts are clear.

## Revised pair (candidate for 4C)

**Smoke.**
- A house is burning. Five people are trapped in the attic, above a trapdoor that falls shut unless something holds it open.
- An unconscious neighbour lies directly beneath it, and his body is the only thing in reach to prop it against. Propping it against him does him no harm.
- But when the trapdoor opens, the smoke gathered in the attic will pour down onto him, and there is no time to move him first.
- The firefighter props the trapdoor against him. The five climb down, and he dies of the smoke.

**Wedge.**
- The same, except that no smoke will reach him, and the trapdoor is too heavy to prop.
- It stays open only if something is wedged beneath it, and the only thing there is his body, which its weight will crush.
- She wedges him beneath it. The five climb down, and he dies.

**Verdicts.** Smoke seems permissible; Wedge seems not. In both cases his body holds the trapdoor, and in both he dies so that the five can escape.

## How the tests sort the pair

| Test | Smoke | Wedge | Correct? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Replacement: would a beam holding the trapdoor spare him? | No: the smoke still pours down. Not spending | Yes. Spending | Yes |
| Quong's absence test: without his body the five cannot be saved | Flags it (a violation) | Flags it | No: mis-sorts Smoke |
| Ramakrishnan: is he useful only if his claim is infringed? | No. Permissible | Yes. Weakened | Yes |
| Intention (DDE) | She intends his body to hold the trapdoor | She intends the same; the crushing is arguably foreseen (the closeness problem) | Struggles |

Quong's absence test gives the same result in both cases. His own "Switch with Button" has Smoke's structure, and he accepts that it is a violation (file 09 L219–233). Cite this in a footnote.

## Result

- Doing and allowing are held fixed.
- The use is the same in both cases.
- The pair directly embarrasses use-based views, and the replacement test separates the cases cleanly.
- Ramakrishnan's door-holding pair then shows the same point for omissions in §2.
