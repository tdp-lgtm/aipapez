## C1 — The Spending View (use vs. spending; the replacement test) [flagship]

- **One-sentence:** I argue that the MP should prohibit *spending* persons — counting the good a person supplies to others as a reason for imposing on her the loss that is the cost of her supplying it — rather than *using* them, against Quong's version (keyed to use of body or property plus a presence test) and in place of Ramakrishnan's primitivism about his Utility principle, on the grounds that spending is exactly what it is to treat something as a resource (the thing the MP exists to rule out), so the MP's content can be derived from its function rather than listed.
- **The principle (draft):** A good that a person supplies to others cannot count as a reason for imposing on her (by act or omission) a loss that is the cost of her supplying it — unless she owes that supply at that cost, or consents to it.
- **The replacement test for "cost of supplying":** her loss is the cost of her supplying the good iff, were that contribution supplied by something else, her loss would be avoidable. "Supplying" = making a positive difference to the good that would justify the act (an improvement over the agent's alternative), not merely preventing the act's own collateral damage.
- **Type:** new version + new distinction (use/spend) + rationale (function-first derivation) + scope result for property.
- **Originality hypothesis:** for harms to persons the test is close to Ramakrishnan's Utility (both turn on whether the victim's usefulness requires the loss), so the novelty lies in (i) the derivation from the MP's function, which Ramakrishnan doubted could exist ("I would be delighted to be proven wrong"); (ii) a principled extension to holdings, which Utility excludes and Quong over-includes; (iii) a duty-bounded disabling structure (not a discount, not an absolute) that falls out of the rationale; (iv) the explicit use/spend distinction, which explains why harmless or costless uses (Room, Six Heart Attacks, Trolley Tool, Cullity's leaning bicycle) are not the MP's business. No search hit for a use/spending distinction or a function-first derivation of this kind.
- **Verdicts (second pass, after stress tests):** spent (forbidden beyond duty): Bridge (push and trapdoor versions), Loop, Heart Attack (all three), Avalanche, Engine Fire, Rush, Icy Trolley, Warning, Life Jacket, Drug Owner, Decoy House, terror bombing, Mines, strike Bakery 3–5. Casualty (ordinary weighing): Switch, Room, Six Heart Attacks, Tracks, Trolley Tool, Three-Track (Option 1 permissible, so no perverse result), Switch with Button, Overdetermined, strategic bombing, Eliminate and Manipulate, strike Bakery 2. Spent but owed (permissible, redress owed): Heirloom Tool, Medication, Assisted Pond Rescue.
- **Stress-test log:** a first version of the test ("replace the contribution at the victim's own location") permitted Loop, which looked attractive given Choo (2025), but the same reading permits a trapdoor Bridge in which the only trapdoor holds the victim — a clear counterexample. Any consistent reading treats Loop and trapdoor Bridge alike, so the view sides with Otsuka and Ramakrishnan against the Loop intuition and must explain that intuition away (it plausibly tracks the redirection of a threat, a doing/allowing feature, not the MP).
- **Notes:** Crux: that "spending" is a morally deep category (resource-treatment; inverse protection by usefulness; persons' goods are theirs to give) rather than a relabeled counterfactual. Costly Icy Trolley and Streets come out permissible on the objective test alone; C3's point about prerogatives can enter as one section.

## C2 — The Jurisdiction View (a service conception of the MP)

- **One-sentence:** I argue that harmfully using a person is pre-empting her judgment about what her body, agency, or holdings are to serve, and that such pre-emption is legitimate only as a service to her own reasons (her duties) or by her consent, against Quong's distributive-justice rationale and Tadros's compulsion rationale, on the grounds that reasons for outcomes cannot confer authority (so the numbers never license use by themselves), which also explains the duty-bounded exceptions, consent, harmless-use wrongs, and the parallel with the state's authority to tax and conscript.
- **Type:** new rationale (cross-literature: Raz's normal justification and dependence theses) + reframing.
- **Originality hypothesis:** the Razian derivation looks unused; **but** Alexander (2016) already calls the MP "a jurisdictional limitation on the reach of strong (legitimately enforceable) moral obligations" (per the search abstract), and Tadros (2011) speaks of permissibly "taking command" of a body only within an enforceable duty. Novelty would be the service-conception derivation, not the jurisdictional framing.
- **Notes:** Crux: is physically using a body an exercise of authority (a power) rather than an infringement of a claim? The NJT is contested as a sufficient condition for authority (e.g., Hershovitz 2007). Content converges with Tadros/Quong.

## C3 — Keeping and Taking (when intentions matter to the MP)

- **One-sentence:** I argue that intentions bear on the MP only through agent-relative prerogatives — permissions to *keep* one's own resources for one's own sake do not cover refusals of aid undertaken in order to *take* the value of another's plight — against both Quong's claim that intentions are irrelevant to the MP and Tadros's and Parry's inference that the MP is intention-based, on the grounds that prerogatives are deliberative permissions (permissions to weight one's own interests) whose scope is fixed by the reasons acted on, while agent-neutral justifications are not.
- **Type:** new distinction + reconciliation.
- **Originality hypothesis:** Parry (2023) and Christensen (2026) supply the cases (Costly Icy Trolley; Streets) but explain them by intention-sensitivity of the MP itself; no search hit connects the reason-relativity of prerogatives to the MP. Legal analogue: the civil-law doctrine of abuse of rights (a right exercised for a purpose foreign to its ground is not protected).
- **Notes:** Narrow and complete; high feasibility. Crux: showing prerogatives are reason-relative without making all permissibility motive-sensitive (Quong's Murders Averted must still come out permissible). Could stand alone or be a section of C1.

## C4 — Derivative claims (the MP determines rescue rights)

- **One-sentence:** I argue that beneficiaries' claims on a person's contribution are claims against that person and are exactly as strong as her duty to contribute, so they cannot extinguish her own claim to rescue, against Quong's rights-first "rescue proviso," on the grounds that one person's contribution can be owed only by her.
- **Type:** new argument.
- **Originality hypothesis:** Parry (2023) states the problem (Icy Trolley vs Icy Trolleys) and concludes the MP must inform rights; Quong's "none of the people he rescues can reasonably demand of Betty" anticipates the derivative-claim idea in another setting.
- **Verdict:** MERGE into C1 (C1's test delivers the same result for omissions without the separate machinery).

## C5 — The Fruits Principle (no self-funding justifications)

- **One-sentence:** I argue that a justification for infringing someone's claim may not draw on the value that the infringement extracts from her, against Ramakrishnan's view that his Utility principle is primitive, on the grounds that rights are protections fixed before the fact and rights sensitive to their own yield would protect people inversely to their usefulness (the structure of a search "not made legal by what it turns up").
- **Type:** new rationale via analogy.
- **Originality hypothesis:** the Fourth Amendment analogy appears unused; the inverse-protection idea is gestured at by Ramakrishnan ("abrogates our moral protections on the basis of our usefulness").
- **Verdict:** MERGE into C1 (its inverse-protection argument becomes part of C1's function-first rationale; as stated, C5 condemns Room unless it adopts C1's replacement test).

## C6 — The Leverage Principle (harms that work through other minds)

- **One-sentence:** I argue that harms whose benefit flows through a third party's will (terror bombing, hostage-taking, deterrent punishment, coercive strikes, sanctions) fall under a stricter version of the MP, permitted only when the victim is responsible for the conduct they aim to change, against treating them as ordinary means-harms (Quong, Ramakrishnan) or as governed by case-by-case thresholds (Christensen's multiple-threshold deontology), on the grounds that such harms conscript the victim's suffering as a message addressed to another agent.
- **Type:** new distinction + application.
- **Originality hypothesis:** no search hit for a general lever principle; Christensen (strikes), Tadros (Mines; deterrence) treat instances.
- **Notes:** Strong applied payoff; weaker as "a new version of the MP" (a sub-principle). Crux: a non-arbitrary line between lever-harms and ordinary means-harms.

## C7 — The restitutionary mark of using

- **One-sentence:** I argue that the MP tracks enrichment at another's expense rather than damage, against Scanlon's view that being a means has no significance beyond cost, on the grounds of a restitution asymmetry: the beneficiaries of a (justified) use owe the used restitution, while the beneficiaries of a justified side-effect harm do not.
- **Type:** reframing + new prediction.
- **Originality hypothesis:** no search hit linking the MP to unjust enrichment; Cullity's "redress" for justified infringements is agent-directed, not beneficiary-directed.
- **Verdict:** MERGE into C1 as a corollary (spending creates a debt in the beneficiaries; casualties do not) — a testable prediction that supports C1.

## C8 — Trespass and nuisance

- **One-sentence:** I argue that using is the moral analogue of trespass (wrong per se, defeasible only by consent or a necessity privilege bounded by the owner's duty) and side-effect harming the analogue of nuisance (wrong only when unreasonable on balance), against the view that the distinction lacks independent footing, on the grounds that the law's two regimes track the same difference in normative logic.
- **Type:** reframing (cross-literature).
- **Verdict:** KILL as a thesis (illustrative only; the analogy strains on omissions, which trespass does not cover). Keep as a possible footnote illustration.

## C9 — Property: services, not holdings

- **One-sentence:** I argue that the MP protects a person's holdings only where the loss imposed is the price of the holding's service to her (the life jacket, the drug), not wherever her property is used (the wrench), against both Quong's property-inclusive MP and Parry's worry that property-inclusion lets acquisitions reshape others' protections, on the grounds that the MP guards persons against being spent, and holdings matter to it only as channels through which a person can be spent.
- **Type:** new argument (scope).
- **Verdict:** MERGE into C1 (falls out of the replacement test: Trolley Tool and Three-Track come out as casualty cases, Life Jacket and Drug Owner as spending).

## C10 — Status security (no usefulness penalty)

- **One-sentence:** I argue that the MP is the minimal principle that keeps a person's protection from varying inversely with her usefulness to others, against Kamm-style inviolability views that make the MP absolute, on the grounds that status requires that protection not track use-value while permitting ordinary tragic conflicts.
- **Verdict:** MERGE into C1's rationale.

## C11 — The veil of location (ex ante contractualism with self-knowledge)

- **One-sentence:** I argue that the MP is what ex ante contractualism delivers when parties know their own bodies and holdings but not their situations, against ex ante views that license pushing, on the grounds that policies selecting victims by bodily properties are rejectable by those who have them.
- **Verdict:** KILL. Counterexample: a Bridge variant in which any body would stop the trolley selects by location only, so the view would permit pushing there, which is clearly wrong.

## C12 — General average (maritime sacrifice-sharing)

- **One-sentence:** I argue that the MP is a sharing principle: a sacrifice extracted for common safety must be spread among the beneficiaries, and indivisible sacrifices (lives) cannot be extracted, on the model of the maritime law of general average.
- **Verdict:** KILL. General average does not track the means/side-effect line (an intentional sacrifice of cargo by steering counts as general average whether the loss is a means or a side effect), and compensability does not track permissibility (a compensated kidney extraction is still forbidden).
