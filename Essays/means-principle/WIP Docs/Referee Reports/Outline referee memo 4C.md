## Referee's summary of the essay

The means principle's target is spending, not use, cashed out by a replacement test and defended by a four-premise argument. The essay applies it against Quong and Ramakrishnan, extends it to omissions and holdings, and uses it to argue that *Loop* is impermissible. "This thesis is stated crisply (¶1.4)."

## Major concerns, as the referee ranked them

1. **[HIGH] *Loop* (¶5.1–5.3).**
   - The section does not engage Choo's robustness argument: the order-effect controls, the survey split, and the reply to the "illusion" diagnosis.
   - Under the essay's own replacement test, *Loop* is spending exactly as it is use. So the reply "they see use, not spending" does no work in this case.
   - Fix: engage Choo's actual argument, and openly own and defend "theory overrides a robust, illusion-free intuition" for this case.
2. **[MED] Display 2 (¶3.1).** P2's defence is assigned to §2's case-sorting, which is extensional. The explanatory advantage over Ramakrishnan needs a derivation, not a redescription. Fix: a non-case-based defence of P2, built out from ¶3.4's engagement with Quong's rationale.
3. **[MED] *Smoke* (¶1.1–1.2).** Quong counts the parallel case (Switch with Button) a violation. The opening puzzle presupposes the *Smoke* verdict, and the only support is a preview of the test. Fix: argue for *Smoke* independently, or frame the opening as disputing Quong's verdict.
4. **[MED] Quinn** is credited but never directly engaged. He is cited only through Liao & Barry. Fix: one direct, pinpointed engagement.
5. **[MED] ¶5.6.** Christensen's *Streets*, a spiteful duty-free omission that he judges wrongful, sits uneasily with the essay's spiteful-donor footnote, which calls such a case permissible. Fix: distinguish the cases on the essay's terms, or flag the disagreement.

## Strongest missing objection

The same point as major 1.

## Minor concerns

- **Density.** ¶2.4, ¶2.9, ¶2.10, ¶4.2, ¶5.3 and ¶5.6 pack more than their share of the budget.
- **Budget arithmetic.** The section budgets sum to 5,050, not "about 5,500" (the gap is the abstract and footnotes).
- **Wording.** ¶5.1 says "shown first"; say "unprimed".
- **Case load.** 18–20 labelled cases is a lot for a general panel.
- **Device.** Don't let the "Why not X?" sub-dialogue device become a tic.
- **¶5.2 footnote.** The partial-justification footnote does real work in one sentence: expand it or flag it as underdeveloped.
