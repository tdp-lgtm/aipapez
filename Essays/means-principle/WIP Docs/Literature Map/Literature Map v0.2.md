## 1. The landscape in one paragraph

The Means Principle (MP) says that harming someone as a means to others' good is harder to justify than harming her as a side effect. It is meant to:
- explain the puzzling pairs: Switch and Bridge; the terror and the tactical bomber; *Room* and *Heart Attack*; Driftwood and Life Jacket;
- constrain lesser-evil justifications and agent-relative prerogatives (functions F1–F6 in the provided-corpus notes).

Since 2011 the literature has run on two tracks.

**The criterion track** asks which relation between victim and good triggers the principle. The candidates:
- Quong's absence test;
- Ramakrishnan's "in virtue of usefulness" relation;
- Walen's restricting claims, whose baseline moved from the victim's absence (2014) to the agent's "toolkit" (2016) and then to property claims with a "tightness" test (2022);
- Øverland's moral obstacles;
- Kamm's causal-structure doctrines;
- Alexander's exclusion of benefits "causally derived from usings";
- Otsuka's intention-based treatment of loops.

**The rationale track** asks why any such relation should matter:
- Quinn's unowed service;
- Tadros's compulsion to serve a goal;
- Mack's subordination by others;
- Alexander's jurisdiction and his prerogative–MP corollary;
- Walen's "patient-analog" of the claim not to make large sacrifices;
- Quong's distributive justice;
- Cullity's recognitional respect.

Two sceptics hold that no rationale has been found: Ramakrishnan, for whom the principle is probably primitive, and Steinhoff.

Each track has a standing failure:
- **The criterion track** measures the victim against her absence or against entitlements. It fails on Liao & Barry's counterfactual cases, and it is charged with presupposing the MP.
- **The rationale track** has no criterion of its own. So Quong's dilemma catches it: its idea either condemns side-effect harm or is defined as harmful use.

## 3. The open niche (revised)

No one has offered a criterion for when a person is made to serve others that does all three of these:
1. **It sorts the hard cases where absence and entitlement baselines fail:**
   - Liao & Barry's counterfactual cases;
   - Walen's two counterexamples to his own 2014 baseline;
   - *Six Behind One* vs Loop, stated objectively;
   - *Room* vs *Heart Attack*;
   - the wrench vs the life jacket.
2. **It does not mention "means" or "use"**, so it escapes the charge of presupposing the MP.
3. **It gives the inherited rationale what that rationale lacks.** The rationale says the principle protects people from being made to serve others beyond what they owe (Quinn, Tadros, Mack, Alexander, Walen). With such a criterion it escapes Quong's dilemma and gets the right scope: omissions in; side-effect harm, *Room*, the wrench, the button and *Six Behind One* out.

The essay's candidate is **spending**: imposing on someone, by act or omission, the loss that supplying a good would cost her, for the sake of that good, beyond what she owes. Two features fix it:
- **Replacement.** Her loss is the cost of her supplying the good if, were her contribution provided from another source, the good could be had without that loss.
- **Contrast.** Her contribution supplies a good only if the good is a respect in which the act beats the agent's alternatives. This follows from the contrastive structure of justification.

Details: Crux Memo v0.2 §1–§3; Rethink Memo v0.1 §2.

**Why the niche is open** on the texts read:
- **The criterion track.** Ramakrishnan disclaims an explanation. Quong's criterion is tied to use and property theory. Walen's baselines are entitlement-based, and his tightness test covers holdings only and is not contrastive. Øverland's and Alexander's measure against absence.
- **The rationale track.** Tadros's "contribute" and "service of a goal" (L277, L85–101) come closest, but no independent test goes with them. Alexander's corollary is stated for "using." Mack's restriction is general.
- **Thomson 2008** over-generalizes the prerogative argument. Tadros and Cullity restrict it to using. No one states the doing-versus-serving distinction precisely, or shows that the correct scope is spending: omissions included; *Room*, the wrench, *Switch with Button* and *Overdetermined* excluded.
- **Searches 73–77** (logged) found no anticipation of the contrastive baseline or of the doing-versus-serving distinction.

## 5. Originality verdict (revised)

**Verdict: OPEN, narrowed.**

**The essay does not claim:**
- the rationale family, or the link between the prerogative and the MP (Quinn, Tadros, Mack, Alexander, Walen 2022, Cullity);
- the structure for omissions (Alexander);
- the symmetric Loop point (Costa);
- verdicts on holdings as such (Walen's tightness test).

**It claims** (Rethink Memo v0.1 §2):
- (a) the criterion: replacement plus contrast, with the contrastive grounding;
- (b) its fit to the inherited rationale: it escapes Quong's dilemma and gets the right scope;
- (c) the corrections that follow: Alexander on *Six Behind One*; Quong's bullets; the victim-centred views on Liao & Barry's cases; Kamm on Loop;
- (d) doing versus serving, stated precisely, against Thomson and Steinhoff;
- (e) the research-ethics check.

**What would still close the niche:**
- Walen 2019 (#16, not on disk) states a contrastive, replacement-based criterion. This is low risk: Walen 2022 summarizes that book, and its test is the tightness test.
- The Stage 10 re-search finds a recent paper with the same criterion.

**Significance: high.** The essay answers both tracks' standing failures at once. It settles live disputes: Quong vs Parry, Liao & Barry vs the victim-centred views, and Alexander vs Otsuka. It takes a stand on Loop, and it reaches research ethics.
