## 09 — Quong, *The Morality of Defensive Force* (OUP 2020), ch. 7 "The Means Principle"

**Status:** read in full (L1–L416).

**The principle (L47; stated at MDF p. 82 per file 06, verify):** "It is morally wrong to harm Y in the
pursuit of an objective if doing so involves using Y's body or other things over which Y has a
rightful prior claim, unless Y is duty bound to suffer this harm, or has consented to this harm."
- fn 7: "rightful prior claim" = "or at least a claim that is stronger than any claim of the agent who
  might harm Y."
- fn 8: failing to save Y counts as harming Y when Y has a right to be saved by you.
- **Counterfactual test (L49; MDF p. 178 per file 06, verify):** "whether X's act could succeed in the
  absence of Y and Y's rightful property. If the answer is no, then X uses Y as a means."
- Refined (L199): hold everything fixed except removal of Y and Y's property; "Given the world as it
  currently is, does the particular act X performs require the instrumental use of Y's body or other
  resources?"
- **Absolute, not pro tanto** (L147): "it is never permissible to violate the means principle."
- **Intention-independent** (L163): what matters is "whether the outcome that ostensibly justifies the
  agent's act ... requires using some person or her property."

**Extensional arguments (7.2):**
- vs intention views: *Assumed Consent* (L63–67): reckless false belief in consent; rape is harmful use
  though no intention to use harmfully → intentions "superfluous."
- vs Kamm's DPP: *Large Alcove Crush* (L79–81; DPP permits, Quong: clearly impermissible) and *Bridge
  with Five Victims* (L83–91; DPP forbids; should pattern with Alcove) → DPP gets pairs wrong; also
  "lacks a compelling rationale" (L93).
- vs Ramakrishnan's *Utility*: *Driftwood* vs *Life Jacket* (L101–111): Utility covers persons only, not
  property, so can't separate them; "It would be a very strange coincidence if the moral principle that
  explains the difference between Driftwood and Life Jacket is entirely different than the principle
  that explains ... Switch and Overpass" (L111) — a **unity demand**.

**Rationale (7.3):**
- Rejects "if Y is not duty bound to bear C for G, wrong to impose C for G" — fails to separate Switch
  from Overpass (L125).
- Rejects Tadros's compulsion rationale via a **dilemma** (L127): "compelling to act for an end she may
  reject" either = imposing costs not duty-bound to bear (horn 1, already failed) or is defined as
  harmful use (horn 2, tautology). Credits Sinclair & Quong, "The Means Principle: Still in Need of a
  Rationale" (unpublished MS) (L131). **This dilemma is the standing test any new rationale must pass.**
- Positive rationale: **distributive justice and independence** (L129–139). Justice allocates each
  person rights to control body + fair share of resources; within limits "no one else can permissibly
  make use of our resources without our permission. This is constitutive of a fair system of
  interaction among free or independent persons." "The fact that P would be useful to achieve some
  goal, G, simply cannot serve as a reason to use P without B's consent. The very purpose of having
  rights to control a fair share of the world's resources is to block this fact from serving as a
  reason." (L135) Mere-harm claims "are defeasible — they must be weighed"; control rights "block
  certain facts from serving as reasons" (L137). Control rights are bounded by duties of rescue (L139).
- **Why absolute (L147–159):** harm-rights are insensitive to aggregate costs, hence pro tanto; control
  rights' scope already reflects total costs to others (rescue duties, aggregated), so a violator is
  "necessarily mistaken about the balance of moral reasons" (L155). *Combined justification* objection
  (Betty duty-bound to bear 100 units; 200 needed): lesser-evil can justify harming, not using; "none of
  the people he rescues can reasonably demand of Betty" (L159). fn 18 (L165): trivial excess (101 vs
  100) — "why assume Betty is not duty bound to bear it?"; denies clear cases exist.
- **Intentions irrelevant (L169–175):** treatment is an objective standard ("treat politely," "treat as
  an employee"). Violator "acts *as if* the victim was under a duty to allow her body or property to be
  used" (L173). Revisionary but clarifying: disregards "moral independence."

**Objections handled (7.4):**
- *Why should the victim care?* (Scanlon, L185–187): independence + living on fair terms.
- *Counterfactual concerns* (L191–237): substitution (Z would have been there) → narrow removal test;
  *space claims*: claim to non-private space S1 iff no other person/object could occupy S1 without
  harming the victim (L207); **Frowe's location-dependence worry** (Betty right after the junction →
  Switch impermissible) accepted (L209–219). *Switch with Button* (L227–229): Betty's body holds down a
  failsafe button → Quong: **violation** (contrast Walen's "toolkit" view, which treats it like Switch).
  *Overdetermined* (L223–237): Carl strapped to boulder; Betty may not use Carl's ray gun → Quong bites
  the bullet.
- *Too restrictive:* Ramakrishnan's *Room* (Archibald's legs keep door ajar; L243–251): not harm, a
  failure to save; if Archibald owned the medicine, saving the five is impermissible.
- *Too permissive:* Frowe's *Sneeze* (L257–271) → *Blink* variant; kills non-responsible threat
  permissibly; bullet bitten.
- *Property different?* denies (L275–277): stringency = severity + status.
- *Intentions and puzzling cases:* *Intending to Kill One*; *Murders Averted* 4 variants (L283–313):
  bad intentions don't make permissible acts impermissible; rudeness asymmetry (L311).
- *Poisoned Pipe* (Tadros; L321–341): collective wrongness (Dietz), *Poisoned Pipe and Fumes*.

**Implications (7.5):** *Large Alcove Crush*; *Decoy House* (civilians lure killers to a farmhouse whose
owners will be killed; MP forbids — "Utility and the DDE cannot explain" it, L353–359); *Medication*
(Albert duty-bound to give medicine; Carl may harm him as means: "liable to be used," L363–365).

**Rights conception (7.7):** rights = reasonable demands; mid-level concepts in a theory of "how free
and equal persons can interact with one another on fair terms" (L411); evidence-relative; "publicly
verifiable" standards of conduct (L407).

**Pressure points for a new view (my notes, not Quong's):**
1. Everything turns on the scope of "rightful prior claim," so the MP inherits every indeterminacy of
   distributive justice (space claims; the alcove; location-dependence). Quong has to settle property
   theory to settle trolley cases.
2. Bites several bullets: Overdetermined; Switch with Button; location-dependence; Sneeze/Blink.
3. The absolutism argument depends on control-rights' scope already pricing in aggregate rescue
   duties; the "trivial excess" footnote denies clear threshold cases rather than explaining them.
4. The rationale explains why *using what is mine* is special, but "use" is still fixed by a bare
   counterfactual-necessity test (would the act succeed without Y and Y's stuff?). Why should
   counterfactual necessity be the mark of appropriation? The test is causal-structural, and the
   rationale is distributive; the bridge between them is asserted.
5. Omissions only covered where there is a prior right to rescue/resources (fn 8).
