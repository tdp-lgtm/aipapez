## 09 — Quong, *The Morality of Defensive Force* (OUP 2020), ch. 7 "The Means Principle"

**Status:** read in full (L1–L416).

**The principle (L47; stated at MDF p. 82 per file 06, verify):** "It is morally wrong to harm Y in the
pursuit of an objective if doing so involves using Y's body or other things over which Y has a
rightful prior claim, unless Y is duty bound to suffer this harm, or has consented to this harm."
- fn 7: "rightful prior claim" = "or at least a claim that is stronger than any claim of the agent who
  might harm Y."
- fn 8: failing to save Y counts as harming Y when Y has a right to be saved by you.
- **Counterfactual test (L49; MDF p. 178 per file 06, verify):** "whether X's act could succeed in the
  absence of Y and Y's rightful property. If the answer is no, then X uses Y as a means."
- Refined (L199): hold everything fixed except removal of Y and Y's property; "Given the world as it
  currently is, does the particular act X performs require the instrumental use of Y's body or other
  resources?"
- **Absolute, not pro tanto** (L147): "it is never permissible to violate the means principle."
- **Intention-independent** (L163): what matters is "whether the outcome that ostensibly justifies the
  agent's act ... requires using some person or her property."

**Extensional arguments (7.2):**
- vs intention views: *Assumed Consent* (L63–67): reckless false belief in consent; rape is harmful use
  though no intention to use harmfully → intentions "superfluous."
- vs Kamm's DPP: *Large Alcove Crush* (L79–81; DPP permits, Quong: clearly impermissible) and *Bridge
  with Five Victims* (L83–91; DPP forbids; should pattern with Alcove) → DPP gets pairs wrong; also
  "lacks a compelling rationale" (L93).
- vs Ramakrishnan's *Utility*: *Driftwood* vs *Life Jacket* (L101–111): Utility covers persons only, not
  property, so can't separate them; "It would be a very strange coincidence if the moral principle that
  explains the difference between Driftwood and Life Jacket is entirely different than the principle
  that explains ... Switch and Overpass" (L111) — a **unity demand**.

**Rationale (7.3):**
- Rejects "if Y is not duty bound to bear C for G, wrong to impose C for G" — fails to separate Switch
  from Overpass (L125).
- Rejects Tadros's compulsion rationale via a **dilemma** (L127): "compelling to act for an end she may
  reject" either = imposing costs not duty-bound to bear (horn 1, already failed) or is defined as
  harmful use (horn 2, tautology). Credits Sinclair & Quong, "The Means Principle: Still in Need of a
  Rationale" (unpublished MS) (L131). **This dilemma is the standing test any new rationale must pass.**
- Positive rationale: **distributive justice and independence** (L129–139). Justice allocates each
  person rights to control body + fair share of resources; within limits "no one else can permissibly
  make use of our resources without our permission. This is constitutive of a fair system of
  interaction among free or independent persons." "The fact that P would be useful to achieve some
  goal, G, simply cannot serve as a reason to use P without B's consent. The very purpose of having
  rights to control a fair share of the world's resources is to block this fact from serving as a
  reason." (L135) Mere-harm claims "are defeasible — they must be weighed"; control rights "block
  certain facts from serving as reasons" (L137). Control rights are bounded by duties of rescue (L139).
- **Why absolute (L147–159):** harm-rights are insensitive to aggregate costs, hence pro tanto; control
  rights' scope already reflects total costs to others (rescue duties, aggregated), so a violator is
  "necessarily mistaken about the balance of moral reasons" (L155). *Combined justification* objection
  (Betty duty-bound to bear 100 units; 200 needed): lesser-evil can justify harming, not using; "none of
  the people he rescues can reasonably demand of Betty" (L159). fn 18 (L165): trivial excess (101 vs
  100) — "why assume Betty is not duty bound to bear it?"; denies clear cases exist.
- **Intentions irrelevant (L169–175):** treatment is an objective standard ("treat politely," "treat as
  an employee"). Violator "acts *as if* the victim was under a duty to allow her body or property to be
  used" (L173). Revisionary but clarifying: disregards "moral independence."

**Objections handled (7.4):**
- *Why should the victim care?* (Scanlon, L185–187): independence + living on fair terms.
- *Counterfactual concerns* (L191–237): substitution (Z would have been there) → narrow removal test;
  *space claims*: claim to non-private space S1 iff no other person/object could occupy S1 without
  harming the victim (L207); **Frowe's location-dependence worry** (Betty right after the junction →
  Switch impermissible) accepted (L209–219). *Switch with Button* (L227–229): Betty's body holds down a
  failsafe button → Quong: **violation** (contrast Walen's "toolkit" view, which treats it like Switch).
  *Overdetermined* (L223–237): Carl strapped to boulder; Betty may not use Carl's ray gun → Quong bites
  the bullet.
- *Too restrictive:* Ramakrishnan's *Room* (Archibald's legs keep door ajar; L243–251): not harm, a
  failure to save; if Archibald owned the medicine, saving the five is impermissible.
- *Too permissive:* Frowe's *Sneeze* (L257–271) → *Blink* variant; kills non-responsible threat
  permissibly; bullet bitten.
- *Property different?* denies (L275–277): stringency = severity + status.
- *Intentions and puzzling cases:* *Intending to Kill One*; *Murders Averted* 4 variants (L283–313):
  bad intentions don't make permissible acts impermissible; rudeness asymmetry (L311).
- *Poisoned Pipe* (Tadros; L321–341): collective wrongness (Dietz), *Poisoned Pipe and Fumes*.

**Implications (7.5):** *Large Alcove Crush*; *Decoy House* (civilians lure killers to a farmhouse whose
owners will be killed; MP forbids — "Utility and the DDE cannot explain" it, L353–359); *Medication*
(Albert duty-bound to give medicine; Carl may harm him as means: "liable to be used," L363–365).

**Rights conception (7.7):** rights = reasonable demands; mid-level concepts in a theory of "how free
and equal persons can interact with one another on fair terms" (L411); evidence-relative; "publicly
verifiable" standards of conduct (L407).

**Pressure points for a new view (my notes, not Quong's):**
1. Everything turns on the scope of "rightful prior claim," so the MP inherits every indeterminacy of
   distributive justice (space claims; the alcove; location-dependence). Quong has to settle property
   theory to settle trolley cases.
2. Bites several bullets: Overdetermined; Switch with Button; location-dependence; Sneeze/Blink.
3. The absolutism argument depends on control-rights' scope already pricing in aggregate rescue
   duties; the "trivial excess" footnote denies clear threshold cases rather than explaining them.
4. The rationale explains why *using what is mine* is special, but "use" is still fixed by a bare
   counterfactual-necessity test (would the act succeed without Y and Y's stuff?). Why should
   counterfactual necessity be the mark of appropriation? The test is causal-structural, and the
   rationale is distributive; the bridge between them is asserted.
5. Omissions only covered where there is a prior right to rescue/resources (fn 8).

## 06 — [author to verify], "The Scope of the Means Principle," *J. Moral Philosophy* symposium on Quong's MDF (uncorrected draft)

**Status:** read in full (L1–L300). Author not named in the conversion (acknowledges an Oslo workshop,
Dec 2019; thanks Bowen, Burri, Fabre, Healey, Hecht). **Verify author before citing.** Gives MDF page
numbers: MP formulation p. 82; counterfactual test p. 178; Room reply p. 194; rights to easy rescue
p. 185; rationale quote p. 184; "fair share" p. 207; property pp. 181–182, 191–193, 195–196 (all verify).

**Target:** Quong's "rights-first" approach to the MP's *scope* (MP applies only to usings that violate
an independently grounded right).

**Argument 1 — omissions (L47–105):**
- *Icy Trolley* (L67; from Tadros 2011 p. 122, verify): Billy slides toward the trolley; Sarah could
  easily stop him; she refrains so his body stops the trolley and saves five (paralysis). Verdict:
  wrong, "on-a-par with pushing Billy." Quong's counterfactual test says: use.
- But Quong also needs an independent right to rescue, and Billy has none: compare *Icy Trolleys*
  (L73; two independent trolleys; Sarah saves five not Timmy) where Timmy lacks a right to rescue,
  because (i) no compensation is owed, and (ii) saving the greater number is permissible even by a
  margin (5 over 4; 100 over 99), unlike harming, so rescue rights are **extinguished** by numbers
  whereas harm rights **persist** (L85–89).
- So either the MP applies to Billy directly (no right needed) or the MP *explains* why Billy has the
  right. Either way the MP **informs** rights; it doesn't merely track them (L103).
- *Room* then collapses into *Icy Trolley* on Quong's test; the fix must revise the test: "we need to
  identify some quasi-causal difference ... more fine-grained than counterfactual dependence" (L105).

**Argument 2 — omissions and intentions (L107–125):**
- *Costly Icy Trolley* (L121): rescuing Billy would cost Sarah her arm (supererogatory); she was
  willing, then refrains *in order that* Billy's body stop the trolley → wrong. Variant: she refrains
  *for the sake of her arm* → not wrong. Same conduct; only the reason differs → favours a
  mental-state component, at least for omissions. (Notes McMahan 1994 anticipates the anti-"independent
  right" point, L135.)

**Argument 3 — property and distributive justice (L137–289):**
- *Trolley Track/Tool* (Barry's wrench needed to divert onto Barry; L171–173): Quong → impermissible;
  even property-inclusive defenders balk (Walen 2014 p. 455, verify).
- *Three-Track Trolley* (L191–195): divert toward Barry (needs Barry's wrench) or toward three (needs an
  unowned branch) to save fifteen → Quong's view makes killing **three** rather than one the only
  permissible option. "far more counter-intuitive."
- **Bidirectional thesis:** property-inclusive MP (World B) vs body-only MP (World A); some property
  distribution is acceptable in A but not B, because in B acquisitions create **normative
  externalities**: (i) *unilateralism* — acquiring changes others' moral protections at will; Quong's
  view says acquisition "*extinguishes* the reason-giving force" of saving others' lives (L247); (ii)
  *inequality* — the more-propertied get stronger comparative protection; others' protections are "at
  the mercy of the wills of the more-proprieted" → a sui generis relational-egalitarian complaint
  (L257–259). Interest in *what it is permissible to do to us* (Kamm/Nagel/Quinn status; L233).
- Body-size thought experiment (L287): if bodies could be enlarged with wealth, a body-inclusive MP
  would draw the same complaint → even bodily rights are partly shaped by the MP's implications.

**Pressure points it creates (for me):**
1. A "rights-first" MP can't handle harmful *omissions* (Icy Trolley) or supererogatory-rescue cases.
2. A counterfactual-dependence test is too coarse (Room vs Icy Trolley).
3. Property-inclusion yields Three-Track; the natural diagnosis: what makes using *my wrench* special
   cannot simply be "it's mine."
4. The author's positive suggestion — the MP partly *informs* distributive entitlements — opens a
   question: what *is* the MP for, such that its scope feeds back into rights? (Candidate hook.)

## 03 — Tadros, *The Ends of Harm* (OUP 2011), ch. 6 "Defending the Means Principle"

**Status:** read in full (L1–L300). No page markers (Quong cites Ends of Harm 127 for the compulsion
line; file 06 cites p. 122 for the Icy-Trolley ancestor — verify both).

**Context:** general deterrence harms offenders to benefit others (L5–11) → does it violate the MP?
MP's general idea (L13): permissible to pursue the good with lesser harm as a side effect, not where
"others will be used as a means to achieve that good." Preferred interpretation (intention-sensitive)
deferred to ch. 7 (L45). Candid: "I will not be able to provide a knockdown argument for the means
principle" (L17).

**§II vs doing/allowing (L61–113):**
- *Trolley Driver* vs *Bridge* (L33–37). *Bystander*; Thomson's *Bystander's Three Options* (L75–101):
  Tadros replies that Thomson assimilates **bearing a cost in the service of a goal** to **having a cost
  imposed as a side effect**; stealing to give to charity is using. Suggests cost-sharing by coin flip
  in Three Options (L93).
- Driver may not push a passenger under the wheels; I may not throw someone before a trolley I
  wrongfully started (L107–109) → MP has significance beyond doing/allowing.
- **Allowing as a means** (L111–113): letting a patient die to use his organs; *child and bus* — "I must
  save the one child. Failing to do this would really be no different from throwing a child in front of
  the bus ... that would be to regard the one child as a useful tool" (L113). (Ancestor of *Icy
  Trolley*.)

**§III Means and status (L115–173):**
- Against act-quality/"minimize using" views: they'd license using one to prevent five usings,
  even punishing an innocent to prevent rapes (L129–137) — the **paradox of deontology** handled by
  status.
- Against agent-relative purity (L139).
- **Status view** (Quinn/Kamm/Nagel; L141–157): if it were permissible to use one to prevent five
  usings, "each of us would be available to be treated as a means"; what matters is *having* the status
  (not being available for use), not whether others respect it (L153). Agent-neutral.
- Flowers/redwoods/deer (L163–169): no constraint on using objects/animals as means → the status is
  grounded in a capacity: **end-setting** (L171). "When a person is used as a means, she is used in
  pursuit of a certain goal. But as an independent person, she ought to be able to set goals for
  herself. It is wrong to compel her to act for the sake of some end that she is permitted to reject
  for herself." (L173) ← the line Quong's dilemma targets.

**§IV Limits (L175–285):**
- Not absolute: nuclear catastrophe (L181); *bruising to save a life* (L183) → "multiplier" idea
  (L185), itself needing explanation.
- Harmless use can still be wrong: intercourse with a comatose person (L187, L275).
- Culpable threat-creator may be thrown in front of the trolley to save even one (L191).
- **Duty-based limit (L193):** "it is permissible to harm a person as a means to an end if that person
  would have had an enforceable duty to avert the threat were she able to do so, even if, in exercising
  her duty she would be harmed to the same degree." Using = imposing a cost for the pursuit of a goal;
  wrong "if that person would not be required to pursue that goal at the same cost were she able to."
- "The duty to rescue and the means principle have a common source ... corollaries of each other"
  (L197–201): duties are limited so each can shape her own life around what she values (Bratman on
  valuing; L197–199).
- *Pond Rescue* → coercion to fulfil an **enforceable** duty (L215–231); *Assisted Pond Rescue* (L235–243):
  you may push the paralysed me into the rivulet (finger lost); authorization should be sought but my
  refusal isn't decisive. Unconscious variant (L245–265): use "to the extent that duty would require of
  her were she able to consent and no more"; supererogatory use needs actual authorization (L257).
- Side effects (L277–283): side-effect victims are "not compelled to adopt" the goal; "She does not
  contribute to the pursuit of the goal and so pursuit of the goal is not imposed on her."
- Coercion analogy (L285): coercing someone to do their duty is often permissible.

**Conclusions (L289–297):** best account rests on "the moral status that we have ... not to be available
for use by each other in a way that harms us"; status "not pure" → not absolute.

**Pressure points (mine):**
1. Quong's dilemma bites: "compelled to act for an end" is either cost-bearing (no Switch/Bridge
   split) or defined as use.
2. The "contribution" idea (L277: side-effect victims do "not contribute to the pursuit of the goal")
   is suggestive but undeveloped: what makes the victim's being a *contributor* the thing that matters?
3. The duty-based limit makes permissible use track the victim's own enforceable duty; but then the
   MP reduces to "don't make people do what they needn't" — and why does that bite on *using* but not
   on *side-effect harming*? Tadros's answer ("not compelled to adopt the goal") re-invokes compulsion.
4. Status/inviolability explains agent-neutral constraint structure but not *which* acts count as using.

## 02 — Tadros, ch. 6 "The Significance of Intentions" (war book; likely *To Do, To Die, To Reason Why*, OUP 2020 — verify)

**Status:** read in full (L1–L336). Context: ethics of war (non-combatant immunity). Cites *Wrongs and
Crimes* (2016) and his 2018 Oxford Handbook chapter, so post-2018.

**§6.1–6.2 (L15–137):** consequentialists should accept intentions matter (worlds with bad-intention
acts are worse; *Trolley* Dorabella/Fiordiligi, L41–47). Non-consequentialist case: spiteful killing that
happens to produce sufficient good isn't justified — "Justified wrongdoing is wrongdoing that is done
for sufficient reason" (L71). Replies to Thomson/Scanlon: deliberation-oddness (L77–91), choosing
intentions (L93–119; racist judge, patronizing boss), discouragement/doctor case (L121–137).

**§6.3 against "intending harm" (L139–181):** Nagel's "intensifying beam" (L157) rejected: in *Bridge*
Dorabella doesn't aim at harm as such (L159–161). *Padded Bridge* variant (L169–171: lowers Fiordiligi
with a rope to give a tiny chance) — "makes little difference" → it's using, not intending harm. Harm as
side effect of use (engine-room fire) no less wrong (L175). **Failing to save the useful** (L177):
Ramakrishnan's heart attack/harmless tests case → "I ought to drive the person to the hospital, even
though I do not use her, her being used is not harmful."

**§6.4 Prerogatives and heteronomy (L183–243):**
- Core intuition (L185): harm (or non-prevention) decided "in order to achieve some good," person used
  to achieve it, no consent → harder to justify than side-effect harm.
- **Prerogatives as partial ground** (L187–211): if cost low enough that she'd be required to bear it,
  using is fine; the answer "you'd be required to bear that cost anyway" is "a central part" of
  justification. Asymmetric grounding: prerogative → wrongness of using, not vice versa (L199–201).
  Admits exceptions both ways (author-of-own-downfall permission; duty to pursue autonomously) (L205–207;
  fn 20 on exploiting vs bypassing agency).
- **Heteronomy** (L213–243): autonomy = life shaped by one's own character/values/aims; heteronomy =
  lack of autonomy *plus* one person "overriding or bypassing the other's autonomous decisions" (L219);
  cooperation and valid consent don't produce heteronomy (L223); heteronomy is scalar (L229–231;
  deceiving a vegetarian). **Key claim (L235–243):** a used person is "more deeply integrated into the
  plans of the person who uses her" than a side-effect victim; "Heteronomy is to do with extent to which
  a person's life is shaped by the character, aims, and values of others" (L237). Not an independent
  ground: using an unwilling person "is a distinctive and deep kind of heteronomous interference"
  (L239).

**§6.5 Using and usefulness (L245–291):**
- Two models: using as a **weight** (bad-making) vs using as a **disabler** of the good's justifying
  force — enabled by the person's duty or consent (L247–251); scalar version (side-effect harm reduces the
  justifying force a bit; using reduces it more).
- *Rush* (L261): to get home for football, Dorabella topples Fiordiligi, whose body stops a trolley
  (five saved). Wrong like *Bridge*, but she doesn't *use* Fiordiligi. Two responses: (a) **Usefulness
  View** (L267): "The fact that a person harms, or otherwise negatively affects a person, is especially
  difficult to justify if doing so relies on some good that will occur only because the person, or her
  body, is useful." — "still recognizably an intentions-based view ... usefulness relies on intentions"
  (L269); credits Ramakrishnan (fn 23). (b) right to be harmed *with the right intention* (*Trolley and
  Telly*, L279–283).

**§6.6 Using combatants (L293–323):** *Mines* (L301–305): burning captured-combatant Kim to terrorize
Kerry into revealing mines — wrong even though Kim is liable to eliminative killing; *Eliminate and
Manipulate* 1 & 2 (L313–323): killing already justified on eliminative grounds may also serve
manipulative ends; in *Mines* capture suffices to eliminate, "The *only* rationale for killing her
rather than capturing her is that this will be useful as a means" (L323). → **Liability to be harmed ≠
liability to be used.**

**Pressure points (mine):**
1. The heteronomy idea ("more deeply integrated into the plans") is the closest existing thing to an
   *agency-structural* rationale, but it is stated in degree terms (scalar), grounded in the *user's*
   plans, and so drifts back to intentions (Usefulness View is "recognizably intentions-based").
2. The disabler model (usefulness disables the justifying force of a good) is shared with Ramakrishnan's
   *Utility*; the question is what does the disabling and why.
3. *Mines*: a person liable to be harmed may still not be *used* → any new view must separate
   liability-to-harm from liability-to-use; the MP is not just an extension of harm-liability.

## 01 — Ramakrishnan, "Treating People as Tools," *Philosophy & Public Affairs* 44 (2016): 133–165

**Status:** read in full (L1–L300). Author/pages confirmed by Quong's citations (file 09 L57, L107:
"Ramakrishnan, 'Treating People as Tools', 133–65"; Utility at 134). (File 06 miscalls it "Using People
as Tools" in two notes.) Page pinpoints below come from other authors' cites only (verify).

**Opening pairs (L1–13):** *Scarcity* (withhold medicine from one to medicate five: permissible) vs
*Guinea Pig* (withhold medicine to observe the disease and learn to save five: impermissible; from
Quinn); *Explosion* (bomb kills one as side effect, saving ten) vs *Block* (trip one into the trolley's
path); *Terror Bombing*. Two strategies: intending harm vs using/exploiting (L11).

**The principle (L15; p. 134 per Quong):** "*Utility*: It is especially difficult to justify infringing a
person's rights on the basis of her usefulness to others." **Technical statement (L153):** "Suppose some
fact F provides a *pro tanto* justification for infringing a person P's claim C. And suppose that F
holds in virtue of the different fact that P will be useful to others (that is, she will serve as a
means for their benefit) only if C is infringed. Then F provides a substantially weaker *pro tanto*
justification for infringing C than it otherwise would." A **disabler/discounter**, not absolute
(million-person variant permissible, L157). Persons only; not property (fn 36, L243).

**Section I — new cases and the failure of rivals (L31–145):**
- *Heart Attack* (L35): Waldo will run harmless tests on unconscious Elsa to cure five; she has a
  heart attack; treating it makes her useless in time; he leaves her untreated and tests her → wrong,
  like Guinea Pig.
- **Harmful Intention** fails (no intention of harm or anything close; L51). **Harm as Means** fails
  (L63). **Causal Intention** (Quinn: intending a person be used/affected in a foreseeably harmful way)
  fails: the tests are harmless; "makes it certain" ≠ harms (Silvia and the drowning child, L75);
  causation-by-omission reply rebutted, plus *Heart Attack\** (L87: Waldo goes to the cinema; ambulance
  arrives too late; doctors then test futile-to-save Elsa) → wrong though Waldo intends no use.
  Revised *Causal Intention (R)* (L103).
- **Compulsion** (Tadros: may not force someone to serve an end she may decline) fails for omissions:
  not aiding doesn't *compel*; stretching "compel" voids its appeal (*Drowning*, L109–117: Marzia's body
  will fall on the button saving your child; she demands redirection to save five; you may let her
  fall).
- **Opportunism** (Quinn; goods that couldn't be secured in the person's absence don't justify harming
  or letting her be harmed): fails on *Heart Attack\*\** (L127: Elsa lies on the only pill; the five
  could be saved in her absence; still wrong to test instead of save) and on *Room* (L133: Archibald's
  legs keep the door ajar; saving five permissible). Covers Moral Obstacles (Øverland, Walen) too
  (fn 21).
- **Person as Means / Intend as Means** (new): condemn *Room* → wrong (L139–145).

**Section II — Utility's mechanics (L147–233):**
- The **"in virtue of" structure** (L151–165): in Heart Attack the five die if Elsa is saved *because*
  she will serve as a means only if not saved; in Room the five die if Archibald is saved *because*
  there isn't enough medicine — the explanation runs the other way (fn 23, L169). "extremely subtle"
  (L167; fn 24 confesses worry).
- *Six Heart Attacks* (L167–175): six heart attacks; save five; then test Elsa → permissible; Utility
  agrees (the five die if Elsa is saved because of time, not usefulness). Counterexample to
  Opportunism, Person-as-Means, Intend-as-Means (fn 25).
- Harming: *Block* vs *Explosion* handled in parallel (L181).
- *Avalanche* (L185–197): Wanda drives to save her pinky; triggers an avalanche that kills Billy and
  also clears a roadblock so scientists can harmlessly test him to save ten → wrong, but she doesn't
  *use* him ("using appears to be an intentional one," L187). Utility: the ten's rescue holds in virtue
  of Billy's usefulness only if his right is infringed → discount. (Disagrees with Alexander, who
  thinks using needn't be intentional, fn 26.)
- *Tracks* (L201–209): turn trolley toward Billy, whose body stops it from reaching twenty behind →
  permissible. Utility: the *pro tanto* justification (ten will die unless diverted) doesn't hold in
  virtue of usefulness; Billy's usefulness to the twenty only **prevents a would-be defeater**. (Also
  fn 30: diverting from one stranger to another is still less wrongful than from an empty track.)
- Inherent plausibility (L215): "people are not resources for others' benefit." Better expression of
  Quinn's "then and there *for* his purposes" (L217–219) than Causal Intention.
- *Cave Exits* (L227–233): fifty trapped; dislodge boulder (crushes Gillian below) or dislodge Trudy
  (who blocks the other exit; she falls and dies) → coin flip; Causal Intention wrongly favors killing
  Gillian. **Removing an obstacle is not using**: "Refusing to let an object hinder my purpose, by
  eliminating the obstacle ... does not make the object *serve* my purpose" (L233).
- fn 33 (L231): Utility "only pronounces ... when supplemented by an independent account of the claims
  we have"; *Drowning* shows treating someone as "then and there for your purposes" is only objectionable
  "*in a particular sense*: namely, infringing her rights on the basis that she will only serve others'
  purposes if her rights are infringed."

**Section III — no deeper explanation (L235–299):**
- Utility is probably **primitive** (L237); rejects neo-Kantian constitutivism, paradox-of-deontology
  worries, and the insect asymmetry (proposal: Utility governs entities in proportion to their
  personhood, L255).
- **The troubling objection (L267–277):** like the distance principle, Utility may lack inherent
  plausibility: "Utility is ultimately a principle about causal structure" (L269); "a person's location
  in causal space sometimes strikes me as a terribly arbitrary feature of his situation — not much less
  arbitrary than his location in physical space" (L273); and it is fine-grained (defeater-prevention
  exempt; Room and Six Heart Attacks exempt) (L275). Suspects all rivals share this (fn 45: except
  Harmful Intention).
- Methodological stance: privilege robust case verdicts over inherent plausibility (L281).
- **The standing challenge (L295):** "we must be careful that the deeper explanations we propose do not
  simply replicate the very features that make principles like Utility call out for explanation in the
  first place (such as their attribution of great moral significance to mere causal structure)."

**Pressure points (mine) — this is the central foil:**
1. Ramakrishnan sets the exact bar: a rationale that explains why *this* fine-grained causal structure
   matters **without being mere causal structure**. Any new MP should show the structure is a proxy
   for something with independent moral significance.
2. His "in virtue of" relation is doing all the work, but its normative relevance is unexplained. What
   kind of fact is "the five die if Elsa is saved *because* she will be useful only if unsaved"? Perhaps
   it is a fact about what the *justification* for the act asks of the victim, or about whose claims the
   act's case depends on.
3. Avalanche and Heart Attack\* push toward an objective (non-intention) version; Costly Icy Trolley (file
   06) pushes the other way. A new view might explain both.
4. Property is his acknowledged gap (Life Jacket, Decoy House).
5. Loop: he'd overturn the permissibility intuition (fn 36 → Otsuka).

## 07 — Walen, "Transcending the Means Principle," *Law and Philosophy* 33 (2014): 427–464

**Status:** read in full (L1–L396). Pages 427–64 per Quong's citation (verify). Walen revised the view
in "The Restricting Claims Principle Revisited" (L&P 35, 2016: 211–247) — **not in corpus; request**
(Quong and file 06 cite it; the "toolkit" version and "agent-patient divide" are there).

**The RCP (abstract L7; first pass L45; revised L203):** patient claims are **restricting** if, respected
as rights, they "would restrict an agent from doing what she could otherwise permissibly do for herself
or others ... if the claimants were absent" (revised: "or would require her to take an action that would
make others worse off than if the claimants were absent"); otherwise **non-restricting**. "restricting
claims are substantially weaker than non-restricting ones" (L45). Means-victims have non-restricting
claims; side-effect victims restricting ones (L67–69). Absence baseline: "the closest possible world in
which the person is unaffected by what the agent can choose to do" (L53).
- **Holistic, not dyadic** (L79): permissibility from "a global balance of claims on an agent."
- **Baseline shift** (L81): not "harder to justify using" but "*easier* to justify" harming those with
  restricting claims.
- Claims assessed **per justification** (L95–103; bomber: morale justification vs munitions-factory
  justification).
- Objective interpretation; intentions secondary (L19–21; replies to Tadros in L&P 2012).

**Grounding (L105–139):** the liberal **autonomy principle** extended from agents ("life to *lead*") to
patients ("life to *enjoy in peace*") (L117). **Moral "negative externalities"** (L125): a patient's mere
presence with a claim that makes others worse off than his absence would is like an externality; so its
strength must be discounted. Side-track man's claim changes the five's status from "must be saved" to
"may be saved" (symmetric, L127); if it had a qualitative edge it would swing them to "must not be saved"
(L135). Negative claims get only a *weak* priority over positive ones (L137).

**Conceptual objections (L141–179):** numbers (overdetermination handled by how a claim "pushes," L153);
niece/nephew inheritance (L157–163); **"The Baseline Is Wrong"** (L167, Otsuka): if the baseline were
"the person lacks the claim," the massive man's claim would be restricting → RCP "trades on the MP."
Walen's reply (L173–179): accept that **what an agent can use is morally significant** — the world is
divided by property into what she may presumptively use and what "belong[s] to others"; the RCP then
explains why *side-effect* claims are weaker. (So the RCP presupposes a property/use baseline.)

**Hard cases (L181–359):**
- *Loop Trolley* (L185–215): all claims non-restricting → symmetric **interdependence** → permissible,
  and not a tragic dilemma (vs MP).
- Asymmetric loops (Figs. 2–3, L219–267): Fig. 3 implication (may kill lower-track man to spare
  non-restricting upper-track man, one-for-one) — counterintuitive; bites bullet via claim-strength
  algebra: P ≫ RP; RN > RP; hence P > RN (L239–259).
- **Property** (L269–307): *Drug Owner* (B needs all his drug to live; A may not take it to save five);
  Thomson's First/Second Property (L281). Three exceptions: small harm/large good (hiker breaks into
  cabin, Feinberg); property conventions yield in extremes; harm not "directly connected to the interest
  protected by the property" — *Trolley Switch Tool* (tool belongs to side-track man; still like Switch,
  L301–303).
- *Rescue Ahead* (L311–323): train to rescue five; man trapped ahead; many think must stop → RCP needs a
  "property-like claim" to the track he occupies (tentative).
- *Massive Man on the Side Track* (L327–329): his weight depresses the track so the switch works → RCP
  indeterminate; two readings. (= Quong's *Switch with Button*.)
- *Heirloom Tool* (L339–359): bystander may smash an heirloom vase to hit a stuck switch and save five,
  though the owner needn't sacrifice it himself → agent–patient **asymmetry**: agents have a "right to do
  wrong"/right of self-indulgence, patients don't. (A counterexample to strict Tadros-style
  duty-matching.)

**Methodology (L361–377):** matching intuitions isn't accounting for them; RCP is "actually an intuitive
doctrine": people say the massive man is not "involved" (L377).

**Pressure points (mine):**
1. The RCP's own grounding rests on a baseline of what the agent may presumptively *use* — so Otsuka's
   objection stands: the RCP presupposes the use/non-use line it was meant to explain.
2. Absence counterfactuals are indeterminate (which absence?), and the view is highly complex
   (claim-algebra; Fig. 3 bullet).
3. Valuable idea to keep in view: **interdependence** (Loop) and the negative-externality analogy — the
   side-track man's claim *competes* with the five's; the massive man's claim *doesn't*. That is a claim
   about the **structure of competing claims**, not about causation per se.

## 11 — Cullity, *Concern, Respect, and Cooperation* (OUP 2018), ch. 10 "Using Others as a Means," pp. 195–216

**Status:** read in full (L1–L362). **Has page markers** ("p. 196" etc.) — pinpoints below are from the
conversion's markers (still verify).

**Opening (pp. 195–96, L33–35):** Zhukov marching weak infantry and penal battalions across minefields;
Nero's human torches; *harmless* uses (wiping hands on my arm; using my back as a desk; leaning a bike on
me) — "might literally be harmless ... However, it could still be wrong." Photographer/surveyor uses
aren't wrong (p. 196, L43) even with a callous disposition.

**Four approaches (L49–55):** treating as thing; property-like violation; subordination of agency
(Quinn); Nagel's "guided by evil." Cullity combines the first three; rejects the fourth (L207–209).

**Right of self-ownership (pp. 197–99, L59–89):** authority over use of one's **body, agency, and
judgement** (L69; judgement: concealing a product flaw so an employee tells customers untruths = "wrongful
use by omission"). Distinct from self-determination (restricts others' *ends*); self-ownership restricts
the *means* others may use (L71). **Responsive derivation** (L79–83): recognizing each other's freedom
from being used communicates recognition of "equal respectworthiness" of self-expressing agents: "If you
force me to serve your ends rather than my own, different ends, you thereby compel me to serve as an
extension of your own agency ... you make me subservient to you" (L81). Admits reasons of concern
against the right (L89).

**Context-underminer (pp. 200–204, L91–121):** "the fact that *using X in this way will serve end E* is
disqualified from being a reason" when X is yours and E is a personal end of mine that you lack decisive
reasons to serve (L99). Balance-tipping argument vs a "Persistence View" (library footstool / bomb-switch /
pain-relief intermediate cases; personal convenience can't tip a tie, L111–117).

**Boundaries (pp. 204–206, L123–151):** right-shaping question (1) (L129). No right against:
traffic-diversion uses (concern), photographer uses (respect), fairly pooled burdens for worthwhile
collective action, e.g., national service (cooperation) (L137–141). **Content-undermining**: attacker used
as a shield (L147–149) — not forfeiture but circumscription. Consent/employment as conditional waiver (L151).

**Justified infringements (pp. 206–208, L153–173):** towel-as-tourniquet (owe a new one); knocking you
against a button / paralysing you to shade a panel → apology + care owed. Right-infringement question (2)
(L167). Agent-neutral ends *you yourself have decisive reasons to serve* are not undermined; "a
justification for me to treat your resources, including your body, as a **necessary stand-in for my
own**, and then make good your loss" (L171). fn 32 (L326): "it must be impossible for me to perform the
action myself. If I treat you as a convenient way of doing what I am not prepared to do myself, then I
*am* subordinating you."

**Other sources (pp. 208–209, L175–197):** privacy; exploitation of intimacy (Lessing's Anna); expressive
disrespect (Nazi use of corpses); preciousness of human life.

**Implications (pp. 209–216, L199–273):**
- Intention: using is intentional (fn 38: side-effect advancement isn't use), but wrongness comes from the
  right, not the intention (L211–213); Scanlon's "costly involvement without consent" rejected — "your use
  of me need not cost *me* anything" (fn 42, L337).
- Zhukov (L219–231): not squandering but a failure "to recognize their standing as bearers of a right that
  rendered them unavailable to be used in this way" (p. 212).
- **Trolley** (pp. 212–213, L235–249): Push: fat man not required to jump → usefulness undermined.
  Diversion: a *justified infringement* of the sidetrack man's right not to be killed, with redress owed
  (care for dependants). Killing as a means justified only if (i) end so important the victim is
  required to sacrifice his life for it and (ii) you cannot give your own life instead (L249).
- **Spectrum** (pp. 214–215, L253–265): Push > **Swap** (fat man behind the five; move him in front) >
  **Loop** (moving the trolley, not the man) > **Warning** (don't warn the fat man so his body shields
  five behind him: use by omission, but *no* right violated: "When U is not helping you to avoid a
  threat, in order to protect a greater number of others from an equally serious threat," answer to (1)
  plausibly No). "A sharp description of the dividing line ... is not to be had" (L265).
  fn 51 (L357): if the five moved behind the fat man to shield themselves from a guided missile, *they*
  violate his right.
- Consent (p. 216, L267–273): waivable; Basiji child mine-clearers; a commander who ignores my consent
  still wrongs me expressively.

**Pressure points (mine):**
1. Cullity locates the wrong in a **subordination** of agency (an agent extends his agency through you),
   grounded expressively; but concedes vagueness and a spectrum, and needs a separate "deprivation" limb
   for side-effect killing.
2. The **stand-in** idea (you may use me only as a necessary stand-in for yourself when I have decisive
   reason to serve the end and you cannot do it yourself) is a sharp, underused idea: **using someone =
   making them stand in for an agent's own contribution.**
3. Warning case (a use by omission that is permissible) sits uneasily with Icy Trolley (file 06: wrong)
   and Tadros's child-and-bus (wrong). Note the difference: in Warning the one is *already* positioned
   as the shield and the threat is a common one; in Icy Trolley Billy is sliding *into* a threat that
   otherwise wouldn't touch him. → a candidate data point for a new view.

## 04 — Parfit, *On What Matters* vol. 1 (OUP 2011), ch. 9 "Merely as a Means," §§30–32, pp. 212–232

**Status:** read in full (L1–L302). **Has page markers** ("p. 213" etc.).

**§30 Mere Means Principle (pp. 212–218):** using isn't wrong per se (ladder, dictionary, witness).
Definitions (p. 213): treat as a means = "make any use of this person's abilities, activities, or body to
help us to achieve some aim"; *merely* as a means = use + regard as mere tool, ignoring well-being and
claims. Kamm's slave-owner objection → **Second Mere Means Principle** (wrong to treat merely, "or to come
close to doing that") (p. 214). (B): not merely a means if governed by relevant moral belief/concern or
would bear great burden for their sake (pp. 214–215). Bandits taking half; attitudes/policies are
counterfactual (p. 215). **Gangster buying coffee** (p. 216): treats seller merely as a means but acts
permissibly → wrongness of *regarding* ≠ of *acting*. Egoist keeps promise / saves child for reward.
*Mutual Benefit* (Green/Gold, p. 217). → **Third Mere Means Principle** (p. 217): wrong to treat merely as
a means or come close "if our act will also be likely to harm this person."
- Consent Principle link; *Lifeboat*, *Tunnel*, *Bridge* (pp. 218–219). Parfit argues the **Consent
  Principle doesn't condemn Bridge** (I could rationally consent to being killed as a means to save five;
  pp. 219–221).

**§31 As a means vs merely as a means (pp. 221–228):** **Standard View** (harming without consent as a
means = merely using) makes three mistakes (p. 221). *Self-Defence* (breaking Brown's leg): harming Brown
as a means but not using Brown — "since I am merely protecting myself from Brown's attack, my aims would
be more easily achieved if Brown wasn't even there. If I was using Brown, I *would* want him to be there"
(p. 222). *Third Earthquake* (Black's toe to shield your child; you'd not take a second toe to save
yourself, pp. 222–223) → not merely as a means. (C) revision refuted (pp. 223–224; soldier offering
surrender). Bridge: even if you'd have sacrificed yourself, "your act may be wrong ... what makes it wrong
may be the fact that you would be *killing me as a means*" (p. 224). Against O'Neill/Korsgaard
(coercion/deception = mere means) (pp. 224–226): *Desperate Plight*. *Bad Samaritan* treats as a "mere
thing," not a mere means (pp. 226–228).

**§32 Harming as a means (pp. 228–232):** rejects Third MMP; **Harmful Means Principle** (p. 229): "It is
wrong to impose harm on someone as a means of achieving some aim, unless (1) there is no better way to
achieve this aim, and (2) given the goodness of this aim, the harm we impose is not disproportionate, or
too great." Upper limits and indeterminacy (p. 229). Q1 (does harming *as a means* matter?) maybe Yes;
Q2 (does *merely* matter?) "always or nearly always No" (p. 230). *Fourth Earthquake* (gangster bruises
Black to save his child — permissible though he treats her merely as a means, pp. 231–232). Conclusion:
"It is wrong to *regard* anyone merely as a means. But the wrongness of our *acts* never or hardly ever
depends on whether we are treating people merely as a means." (p. 232)

**Pressure points (mine):**
1. Parfit cleanly separates the *Mere Means* question (attitudes; rarely matters for permissibility) from
   the *Harmful Means* question (causal role of harm; may matter). So the MP debate in this corpus is about
   the latter; any "Kantian" rationale must avoid collapsing into the attitudinal "merely" question.
2. His test for *using a person* (vs harming as a means): "If I was using Brown, I *would* want him to be
   there" — the **presence test** (p. 222). Opportunism-style.
3. The Harmful Means Principle is a weight/proportionality principle with no rationale — Parfit doesn't
   say why means-harm is special (he leaves Q1 open).

## Cross-corpus synthesis (first pass, 2026-09-22)

**What the MP is supposed to do (functions named across the corpus):**
(F1) explain the *puzzling pairs* (Switch/Overpass; Strategic/Terror Bomber; Scarcity/Guinea Pig;
Explosion/Block) — Quong, Ramakrishnan, Tadros, Walen, Parfit; (F2) unify doing *and allowing* cases
(Heart Attack, Guinea Pig, Icy Trolley, Streets) — Ramakrishnan, Tadros, file 06, Christensen; (F3) constrain
agent-relative prerogatives and lesser-evil justifications in defensive force (Quong; file 06 L17); (F4)
separate liability-to-harm from liability-to-be-used (Tadros *Mines*; Quong *Medication*); (F5) underwrite
limits on punishment/deterrence (Tadros 2011), war (non-combatant immunity; Tadros ch. 6), strikes
(Christensen), police quotas (Guerrero); (F6) express persons' status as not available for others' use
(Kamm/Nagel/Quinn via Tadros; Cullity's subordination; Ramakrishnan's "not resources for others' benefit").

**The unmet demands (where every existing version strains):**
(D1) **Rationale gap** — Ramakrishnan: probably primitive; "mere causal structure"; any rationale must
not replicate it. Quong's distributive rationale explains *property* in use but the use-test is bare
counterfactual necessity. Tadros's compulsion rationale faces Quong's dilemma. Walen's RCP presupposes
a use baseline (Otsuka). Cullity: vague spectrum, expressive grounding.
(D2) **Omissions without rights** — Icy Trolley, Costly Icy Trolley, Streets, Heart Attack; yet Room, Six
Heart Attacks, Warning, Drowning are permissible.
(D3) **Intention vs objectivity** — Avalanche and Heart Attack\* and Rush (objective usefulness matters
without intention) vs Costly Icy Trolley and Streets (the agent's reason seems to matter) vs Assumed
Consent and Unfortunate Hug.
(D4) **Property** — Life Jacket, Decoy House, Drug Owner (property use seems covered) vs Trolley Tool,
Three-Track, Heirloom Tool (not, or not stringently) — and the normative-externality/inequality worry.
(D5) **Structure** — absolute (Quong) vs pro tanto/discount (Ramakrishnan, Walen, Parfit) vs duty-matched
limits (Tadros; Cullity's decisive-reason ends) vs thresholds (Christensen).
(D6) **Hard boundary cases** — Loop; Switch with Button / Massive Man on the Side Track; Tracks; Cave
Exits (removing obstacles isn't using); Sneeze/Blink; Overdetermined; Rescue Ahead; Warning vs Icy Trolley.

**Recurring unexploited ideas worth developing (possible seeds — to be tested, not assumed):**
- *Justification-relative* framing: Walen (claims assessed under a justification), Ramakrishnan ("in
  virtue of" usefulness grounds the justification), Tadros (disabler of the good's justifying force),
  Cullity (context-undermining of reasons), Quong ("usefulness cannot serve as a reason"). **All of these
  are claims about which facts may figure in the case for the act**, i.e., about the structure of
  justification owed to the victim. None develops this as the principle's core: what does the
  justification *ask of the victim*?
- *Stand-in / substitution* (Cullity fn 32; Thomson's "cost of one's good deed"): using makes the victim
  do (or bear) what the agent's project needed from someone.
- *Contribution vs incidence* (Tadros 2011 L277: side-effect victims "do not contribute to the pursuit of
  the goal"): the used person is made a **contributor** to a project; the side-effect victim is only
  **affected** by it.
- *Levers on other wills* (Christensen strikes; Terror Bombing; Mines; general deterrence): the victim's
  suffering is the medium through which a third party's will is moved.
