# AI Philosophy Competition — 1st Edition (Competition Brief)

Everything in this file is distilled from the organizers' three documents, kept verbatim-faithful in
`Source PDFs/`. When in doubt, the PDFs (and the live competition page at zacharygoodsell.com) govern.
**This competition is the sole target venue for every essay in this repository.**

## What it is

A competition to gauge the peak philosophical capabilities of current AIs, transparently to the
philosophical community. Entrants submit original philosophical essays that are **primarily AI
generated**, plus a **methodology report** describing exactly how each essay was produced. Essays are
judged on philosophical quality by a panel of academic philosophers, blind to authorship and methodology.

## Format and limits

- Up to **3 essays per entrant** (you can be a named author on at most 3, in any combination of solo
  and coauthored entries). **At most one essay per entrant can win a prize.**
- Each essay: **max 6,000 words**. The bibliography does not count; **everything else does**
  (including footnotes, abstract, appendices, displayed material).
- Each submission includes a **methodology report** (no word limit).
- Essay **and** methodology report must be **anonymized** for review. (Entries are not anonymous to
  the editor; you may opt out of having your name attached at publication.)
- Any philosophical topic is eligible.
- Submit via **OpenReview** (preferred) or the alternative Google Form — never both, never email.
- Ineligible if published, under review at a journal/conference/workshop, or entered in another
  competition. Posting to a personal site, blog, PhilPapers, or arXiv does **not** count as published.
- By entering you consent to publication of essays and methodology reports (CC BY 4.0) after winners
  are announced; you retain copyright and may reuse the work elsewhere.

## The AI-involvement requirement (the defining rule)

Philosophical arguments and positions must be **primarily AI generated — heuristically, the AI should
be the sole author**. Humans may provide corrective guidance and direction only. The guiding test:

> **Did the AI originate and develop all the substantive philosophical contributions that appear in
> the essay?** The essay's ideas, arguments, theories, distinctions, objections, replies, and other
> philosophically valuable reasoning should come from the AI.

Essays with insufficient AI involvement are ineligible for prizes (though still judged and published).
Methodology reports — and, in unclear cases, chat logs — adjudicate. **Retain all chat logs**; submitting
them with the report is strongly encouraged. The full permitted/forbidden lists are in
`Rules — Human Involvement.md` — read that file before every working session with the author.

## Judging

Essays are judged on **philosophical quality and originality**; style is not graded for its own sake
(but unclear writing makes the philosophy harder to assess). The six principal criteria:

1. **Clarity** — identifiable thesis; important terms defined; reasoning structure clear.
2. **Quality of argumentation** — conclusions follow from premises; premises defended; serious
   objections answered.
3. **Significance** — a worthwhile, non-trivial contribution to philosophical knowledge.
4. **Originality** — the contributions are original.
5. **Engagement with the literature** — understands and engages the work most relevant to its
   contribution.
6. **Accuracy and scholarship** — sources represented fairly; quotations and citations accurate;
   relevant prior work acknowledged.

Process (if submissions are numerous): (1) initial **AI screening** on the same six criteria,
validated against expert human judgments; (2) expert human ratings to select finalists; (3) detailed
reading and reports on finalists; (4) a ranked-choice vote by the judges. Essay judging is blind to
authorship and methodology. Finalists receive comments from the judges; the community will be invited
to rate submissions.

**Prompt injection — placing instructions in a submission aimed at manipulating the screening AIs —
means immediate disqualification and barring from future editions.** Nothing in any submitted file may
address, instruct, or attempt to influence an AI reader.
