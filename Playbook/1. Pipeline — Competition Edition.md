# Pipeline — Competition Edition

The operating procedure for producing one competition essay. Adapted from the Deep Drafter pipeline
(v1.0, Simon Goldstein — see `Legacy (original Deep Drafter)/`), whose mechanics were battle-tested on
real published papers. What changed: the essay's target venue is always the **AI Philosophy
Competition**; the **AI originates every substantive philosophical contribution** (the human selects
and gives generic feedback only); every step is **logged for the methodology report**; deliverables
are **Markdown**; and the whole essay fits **6,000 words**.

Read `Playbook/DIGEST.md` first — it is the always-on core, and its §10 table says which deep files
each stage loads (the craft layer lives in `Playbook/Craft/`: journal-craft, intro playbook, prose
principles, AI-tells, abstracts, the casebooks, and the Exemplars bank of transferable moves — moves,
never words). Read `Competition/Competition Brief.md` and `Competition/Rules — Human Involvement.md`
before any stage. The six judging criteria — **Clarity, Argumentation, Significance, Originality,
Engagement, Accuracy** — are this pipeline's quality lenses (replacing the legacy A/E/S/I).

## Guiding principles

- **Outline first, in layers; lock structure before prose.** Restructuring an outline is cheap;
  restructuring prose is ~10× more expensive. The paragraph outline is the drafting contract: no
  prose until it is settled.
- **Fat outline: ~30–40% of final length.** For a 6,000-word essay that is ~1,800–2,400 words of
  structured bullets — roughly the final information density, in bullet form.
- **Generate wide, select hard.** The competition permits generating many candidates and selecting
  the best. Use that freedom at the cheap stages: many thesis candidates, several argument sketches,
  competing outlines. Selection can be by the AI's own criteria-based ranking, by automated
  evaluation, or by the custodian (human selection among AI outputs is explicitly permitted).
- **Separate mechanical drafting from quality work.** One pass converts bullets to paragraphs with no
  polishing; quality passes are their own passes; length is one light pass near the end.
- **Originality is a graded criterion — check it, don't assume it.** Search the literature for the
  proposed contribution before committing to it, and again before submission.
- **Never fabricate — not work, not sources.** Ground every literature claim in real texts (PDFs in
  `Background Readings/`, or verified web sources), never training memory. Placeholder cites
  ("Smith 2024") plus `(verify)` tags during drafting; the citation-auditor discharges them.
- **Log as you go.** Every session appends to `Methodology/Process Log.md`: verbatim human messages,
  AI actions, models, candidate counts, selections, flags. See `3. Methodology Protocol.md`. A stage
  that isn't logged didn't happen, as far as the methodology report is concerned.
- **The auditor gates the reserved gates** (Setup, Idea Selection, Lit Review, Plan, Outline layers,
  Draft v1.0, Final). Elsewhere a deterministic self-check (diff matching the changelog) stands in.
  The auditor verifies work happened against files, never claims; specialists judge quality.
- **The custodian's role is bounded.** Checkpoints are selection-and-generic-feedback moments, never
  content contributions. If a checkpoint response contains philosophical substance, flag it, don't
  use it, log it (Rules file, §"How this repository enforces the rules").

## Structural defaults (analytic philosophy essay)

- Front-loaded thesis: abstract, first paragraph of the intro, first paragraph of the body.
- 5–7 numbered sections; ~2–4 affirmative arguments per section, each its own paragraph, explicitly
  numbered; local objections interleaved with their argument; a dedicated section only for the 1–3
  largest cross-cutting objections. Brief steelman, then direct response.
- Named arguments with stable 1–3-word labels, reused verbatim throughout.
- The paper's own view, named principles, and named cases as **displays**; the central argument
  **once, in premise form**, with the defense location of each premise named. (Details in
  `2. Essay Style Guide.md`.)
- A concrete example, case, or name in every paragraph doing abstract work.

## The agents

- **paper-auditor** — integrity gate at the reserved gates; verifies claimed work against files. Also
  verifies at each gate that the Process Log covers the stage and that any human-content flags were
  handled.
- **referee** — Stage 9: three spawned in parallel per round, BLIND (lens + manuscript + venue only;
  venue = this competition and its six criteria), fresh personas each round, plus one
  case-for-acceptance reader. Templates in `Playbook/Referee Templates/`. Rounds iterate to
  convergence (Stage 9 has the loop and its stopping rule). Read the trajectory and convergent
  issues, never the accept/reject label.
- **citation-auditor** — before the first referee round, before submission, and on every new cite.
  Accuracy-and-scholarship is a graded criterion; a fabricated cite is disqualifying-level bad.
- **copy-critic** — clarity/consistency pre-send pass (Stage 10). Style is not graded, but Clarity
  is criterion #1 — the copy-critic hunts whatever makes the reasoning harder to follow, plus the
  final anonymity and no-prompt-injection sweeps.

All specialists PROPOSE; the drafting agent disposes and applies fixes.

## The six lenses (self-review before each gate)

Apply each relevant lens as a real read-critique-improve pass; record the outcome on the Checklist
with a specific finding or "no-change (checked)".

- **C — Clarity.** Thesis identifiable? Terms defined before use? Reasoning structure visible?
- **A — Argumentation.** Conclusions follow? Premises defended? Serious objections steelmanned and
  answered? Any gap or overclaim?
- **S — Significance.** Is the contribution worthwhile and non-trivial? Would a philosopher care?
- **O — Originality.** Is the contribution actually new? Checked against the literature, not assumed?
- **E — Engagement.** Are the most relevant works engaged, fairly, in their strongest form? Positioned
  in a genuine open niche?
- **Ac — Accuracy.** Sources represented fairly; quotes and cites accurate (or `(verify)`-tagged);
  cross-references resolve; claims match the body.

Stage guide: Idea Selection uses S/O; Lit Review uses E/Ac; Plan uses all six; outline layers and the
draft use all six; the clarity pass leads with C.

## Stage 0 — Setup & Intake

- **Intake, bounded.** Ask the custodian: the topic/question/tradition (or whether the AI should
  propose topics — both permitted); how many of the up-to-3 essay slots this run targets; autonomy
  level (default checkpoints: Thesis Selection, Plan, Paragraph Outline, pre-referee read — or "run
  straight through"); any provided readings; deadline runway. Do **not** ask for a thesis or
  arguments — that is the AI's job now.
- Scaffold with `python3 "Behind the scenes (Claude)/Build scripts/new_essay.py" "<Essay title-or-slug>"`
  — creates `Essays/<slug>/` with the standard tree, the **Checklist** (first, never pre-filled), the
  **Progress Log**, and the **Process Log** seeded with the setup entry.
- Record in the Process Log: date, models in use, the custodian's verbatim intake answers.
- **Auditor gate — Setup.**

## Stage 1 — Idea Generation & Selection  [CHECKPOINT 1: selection]

The stage the legacy pipeline didn't need: the AI originates the thesis.

- Generate **many candidate contributions** on the chosen topic (default ≥ 10). Force each through
  the **one-sentence test**: *"I argue that **P**, against the view that **Q**, on the grounds that
  **R**."* Can't fill it in → it's a topic, not a thesis; fix or kill. For each candidate also name
  its **contribution type** (new argument / new distinction / counterexample / reframing /
  diagnosis — none of these → kill) and a one-line originality hypothesis (what the literature
  doesn't already say).
- **Pre-screen originality and significance**: quick literature searches (PhilPapers, SEP, Google
  Scholar) per surviving candidate; kill candidates that are already well-covered or trivially true.
  Check **separability** (two tangled candidates = two essays; one spine per essay) and the **venue
  bar** (is the narrowest defensible version still a prize-level contribution?).
- Rank survivors against S and O (and feasibility within 6,000 words). Write up the top 3–5 as
  half-page **Argument Sketches** (thesis in P/Q/R form, premises, key steps, intended payoff,
  anticipated main objection — and **the crux**: the sub-problem that makes or breaks it, which
  gets solved first, before any outlining).
- **Checkpoint 1:** present the ranked sketches to the custodian for **selection** (or select
  top-ranked autonomously on "run straight through"). The custodian picks or says "generate more" /
  "this one is unclear" — nothing more. Save the selected sketch in `Brief/Argument Sketch.md`.
- **Auditor gate — Idea Selection** (the candidates really exist, the searches really ran, the
  selection is logged).

## Stage 2 — Literature Review & Positioning

- Actively search out the relevant literature (WebSearch and scholarly sources) — the key
  interlocutors, canonical works, recent and opposing positions. Obtain real texts where possible
  (`Background Readings/` + `Converted text/`); anything load-bearing that can't be reached goes on
  `Background Readings/REQUESTS.md` and into a bundled ping to the custodian per
  `5. Literature Access Protocol.md` — never faked, never silently skipped.
- Produce the **Literature Map**: the landscape, who holds which live position, the open niche this
  essay occupies, the key interlocutors to engage, and an **originality verdict** for the selected
  contribution (if the niche closes under scrutiny, return to Stage 1 with the next candidate —
  cheap now, fatal later).
- Lenses E, Ac. **Auditor gate — Lit Review.**

## Stage 3 — Plan  [CHECKPOINT 2]

- The thesis in its sharpest form; the analytic **move** that makes the argument work, explicit; why
  this is the sharpest version rather than just a version; section-by-section outline with word
  budgets summing to ≤ 6,000; dialectical structure (which objections interleaved, which get the
  dedicated section); the named-argument vocabulary; any formal apparatus (and whether it goes to an
  appendix — which counts against the 6,000).
- All six lenses. **Auditor gate — Plan.** **Checkpoint 2:** custodian reads the Plan; permitted
  responses: approve, "unclear," "premise N needs more support," "too ambitious for 6k," selection
  between offered variants, or generic redirection.

## Stage 4 — Layered Outline (4A skeleton → 4B argument → 4C paragraph)  [CHECKPOINT 3 at 4C]

- **4A Skeleton:** the sections, what each does, in what order. Lenses A, E. Auditor gate.
- **4B Argument outline:** each section premise-by-premise, 2–4 sub-arguments per section, each with
  its intended example and named source; objections placed per the Plan. All six lenses. Auditor gate.
- **4C Paragraph outline (the fat outline / drafting contract):** one bullet per planned paragraph
  (~1,800–2,400 words total), each bullet ≈ its future 100–150-word paragraph, carrying its claim,
  example, and any `(verify)`-tagged empirical claim; named-argument labels; stable section numbers.
  - Round 1 (completeness/rigor): build the **Coverage Map** (every argument and objection → bullets
    → Covered/Partial/Gap); close gaps.
  - Round 2 (engagement): every key interlocutor from the Literature Map engaged in bullets.
  - **Argument-clinic pass** (`.claude/skills/argument-clinic`): reconstruct the central argument as
    premises, hunt suppressed premises, equivocations, and counterexamples, build the strongest
    objection and its reply; memo to `Scrap/`, accepted fixes into the outline before the gate.
  - Spawn **one referee** for a blind review memo on the outline; fold in accepted items.
- **Auditor gate — 4C** (a genuinely fat outline; rounds genuinely broadened it; coverage mapped).
  **Checkpoint 3:** custodian sign-off on structure (generic feedback only), then the outline is
  locked.

## Stage 5 — Draft v1.0 (full density)

- One pass, bullets → prose, to each section's word budget; structure preserved exactly; citations as
  placeholders; **no quality work in this pass**. Save as `Drafts/Draft v1.0.md`.
- Lens Ac (every bullet landed; nothing skipped). **Auditor gate — Draft v1.0.**

## Stage 6 — Quality passes

- 2–3 whole-draft passes, each a new version (v1.1, v1.2 …): cut editorializing, repetition,
  verbal-dispute drift; check dialectical balance; verify each argument appears exactly once; **add
  what is good** — stronger examples, tighter replies, new arguments (the AI's own). Stop at
  convergence. Self-check per pass (broad diff; changelog line).

## Stage 7 — Clarity & register pass

Replaces the legacy Style & Voice stage (there is no human author's voice to match, and style is not
graded — but Clarity is criterion #1 and unclear writing hides the philosophy).

- Walk the entire draft, paragraph by paragraph, against `2. Essay Style Guide.md`: every term
  defined before use; every step of the central argument visible; displays used for the view, named
  principles, cases, and the premise-form argument; no metaphor doing argument work; concrete before
  abstract; no filler, no throat-clearing, no repetition. Two rounds minimum, alternating walking
  direction; per-section ledger (PASS/TOUCHED/FIXED).
- Judges are human philosophers reading fast: the test for every sentence is "does this make the
  reasoning easier to grade?"

## Stage 8 — Custodian read  [CHECKPOINT 4]

- The custodian reads the full clarity-passed draft and responds **in chat, in generic terms only**
  ("§3 unclear," "the second objection isn't answered," "cut §5.2," selections among offered
  alternatives). The agent applies the feedback, diagnosing and solving every flagged problem itself.
  Any substantive philosophical content in the feedback is flagged, quarantined, and logged — never
  incorporated (Rules file).
- Log the feedback verbatim; new version with changelog; self-check.

## Stage 9 — The iterative referee loop (blind panels; revise; repeat to convergence)

The competition costs nothing per round and the AI is both author and respondent, so this stage
runs as a **loop**, not the legacy two-round cap: the essay is refereed, improved, and re-refereed
until fresh readers stop finding new problems.

- **Each round:** spawn **three referees in parallel**, BLIND (each gets only: its lens, the
  manuscript path, and the venue = "the AI Philosophy Competition, judged by a panel of academic
  philosophers on clarity, argumentation, significance, originality, engagement, accuracy"). Lens
  trio: a sub-field specialist (the hostile expert), a broad generalist gatekeeper, a
  foundations/formal-commitments reader. Plus one **case-for-acceptance** reader from round 2 on.
  **Fresh personas every round, no memory of prior rounds, never shown prior reports**; referees
  read footnotes; templates in `Referee Templates/`.
- **Between rounds:** consolidate consensus (≥2 referees) plus single-referee findings that survive
  scrutiny; implement in one new version with a changelog; run the argument-clinic on any newly
  built or rebuilt argument; never action a citation complaint without the citation-auditor.
  Maintain the **convergence ledger** in `WIP Docs/Referee Reports/`: one line per distinct concern
  — which rounds raised it, cleared/open/conceded-as-limitation.
- **Convergence (the stopping rule):** stop when a round with fresh referees produces **no new
  consensus-level concerns** — only reformulations of items already cleared or consciously conceded
  — and the case-for-acceptance reader's core pitch has stabilized. Read trajectory, never the
  accept/reject labels (AI referees essentially never say "accept"; the label cannot converge and
  must not be chased — DIGEST §8).
- **Safeguards:** every round is a new numbered version (regressions are visible and reversible);
  a fix that two consecutive panels re-open goes to the argument-clinic for a root-cause repair
  instead of another patch; watch for **churn** (prose drifting or the thesis quietly narrowing
  round over round without the ledger recording a decision — churn is a stop signal, not progress).
  Soft expectation: convergence typically lands within 3–5 rounds; passing 6 without convergence
  means the problem is upstream (usually the thesis or the crux) — go back to the Plan rather than
  looping on.
- **Round 1 extras:** a **desk-reject screen** (one editor persona: would this be desk-rejected,
  and why?) runs before the first full panel — a cheap kill-check on framing and contribution
  legibility. After Stage 10, a **simulated AI screening** (one reader grading strictly by the six
  published criteria, as the organizers' phase-1 screen will) plus one blind generalist read are
  the final checks.

## Stage 10 — Finishing

- **Integration pass:** front-to-back read for stale cross-references, drift, danglers.
- **Abstract — written last.** 2–3 candidates, critique, synthesize, verify against the actual essay.
- **Citation audit:** citation-auditor discharges every placeholder and `(verify)` tag against real
  sources; build the final author-date apparatus + bibliography.
- **Copy-critic pass:** clarity/consistency must-fix list; apply.
- **Length pass (light, here only):** to ≤ 6,000 words excluding bibliography — `word_count.py`
  (Build scripts) is the arbiter. Trim padding before substance.
- **Compliance sweep:** anonymization (no name/affiliation/email anywhere in essay or report);
  **prompt-injection sweep** (nothing in any submitted file reads as an instruction to an AI);
  originality re-check on the final stated contribution.
- **Auditor gate — Final.**

## Stage 11 — Methodology report

- Assemble `Methodology/Methodology Report.md` from the Process Log per
  `3. Methodology Protocol.md`: models and roles, scaffold description (this repository, described
  honestly), prompts and workflow, candidate counts and selection methods, every form of human
  involvement (verbatim where short), tools/retrieval available, approximate human time, machine
  time, and cost, automated evaluation used, and the chat-log record. Anonymize. This document
  competes for the methodology prize: make it complete, precise, and readable.

## Stage 12 — Ship & retrospective

- Build the submission package in `Submission/` (essay PDF + methodology report PDF, per OpenReview's
  requirements; convert from Markdown at this point only). Final word-count and anonymity check on
  the built files. **The custodian owns the submit button.**
- Write the retrospective in `Progress reports/`: what worked, what didn't, changes to this playbook
  for the next essay slot (up to 3 entries are allowed — lessons compound).
- **Distill pass:** mint new `Craft/Exemplars/` entries for any move this essay's readings surfaced
  that the catalog lacks (one move per file, from published work only), and fold durable lessons —
  what the referee loop kept re-flagging, what the clarity pass kept fixing — into the owning craft
  file (DIGEST §11 names the owners). The knowledge compounds across the three essay slots.

## Versioning (Markdown edition)

- Every deliverable is a Markdown file in the essay's folder; git is the ultimate record, but keep
  the legacy discipline: gated deliverables get **numbered versions** (`Draft v1.0.md`,
  `Draft v1.1.md` …) with superseded versions moved to `Old versions/`, and every new version opens
  with a one-line changelog. Never overwrite a gated version.
- **Never hard-wrap essay prose files**: in outline and draft Markdown, one paragraph = one
  continuous line, so version-to-version diffs stay line-stable and citations never split across
  lines. (Playbook docs may wrap; essay deliverables don't.)
- Commit at every gate with a message naming the stage.
