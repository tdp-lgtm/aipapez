# Annotated exemplar — Frowe (clinching analogy; wry dialectical close)

## Source
- **Author / Works:** Helen Frowe, *If You'll Be My Bodyguard: Agreements to
  Save and the Duty to Minimize Harm* (froweBodyguard) and *The Comparative
  Claims of Victims* working draft (froweVictimsDraft — unpublished; private
  repo only).
- **Why this passage:** two closing devices the original profile missed — the
  one-sentence analogy that ends an argument, and wry wit that diagnoses *why*
  the rival view seemed plausible.

## The passages
> It's akin to taking a life preserver that Alice has brought to protect herself
> and giving it to Betty.

> Little wonder, then, that it appears invariant: this implausible picture does
> imply a binary choice between having a full right and having no right at all.

> So much for outsourcing as a ground of preferential saving, then.

## What makes it work (the lesson)
- **The clinching analogy**: after the argument is made, one concrete
  redescription makes the verdict feel *forced* rather than asserted — the
  reader supplies the conclusion themselves.
- **Diagnose the rival's appeal, don't just reject it**: "Little wonder, then,
  that it appears invariant" explains the intuition away, which defuses it far
  better than denial.
- **The wry dismissal close** ("So much for X, then.") ends a rival's section
  with finality and a touch of dryness — used once or twice, never as a tic.

## What to borrow
End major arguments with one concrete analogy that re-describes the verdict; and
when a rival view is intuitive, spend a sentence explaining *why* it seems
right before showing it isn't.
