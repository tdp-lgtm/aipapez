# Annotated exemplar — Muñoz, "The Many, the Few, and the Nature of Value" (musical metaphor + homely parenthetical)

## Source
- **Author / Work:** Daniel Muñoz, "The Many, the Few, and the Nature of Value," *Ergo* 9, no. 4 (2022): 70–[end] · suggested cite key: `munoz2022manyfew` (verify against the source before citing)
- **Why this passage:** A musical metaphor collapses several rival formulations of one objection into a single claim ("the same argument … in different keys" / "the song is the same"), saving the reader from a literature dump; a parenthetical aside restates an axiom in plain words so it needs no defense.

## The passage
> The same argument is given in different keys. Kavka (1979 …) says Taurek must give up either Pareto or Transitivity. For Hirose (2001) … Transitivity is assumed sotto voce in the case against Tradeoffs. … Either way, the song is the same. Given Transitivity, Taurek is in trouble.

## What makes it work (the lesson)
- **One metaphor absorbs a whole cluster of citations.** Rather than treat Kavka and Hirose as separate objections to summarize in turn, Muñoz declares them one argument "in different keys," lists each briefly, and closes with "the song is the same." The reader gets the unity *and* the variants without a survey paragraph.
- **The metaphor is sustained, not decorative.** "Keys," "sotto voce," "the song is the same" all belong to the same musical frame, so it reads as a controlled device rather than a stray flourish. Pick one image and stay inside it.
- **Bookend the list with the point.** "The same argument is given in different keys" opens the move; "Either way, the song is the same. Given Transitivity, Taurek is in trouble" closes it. The variants sit *inside* a thesis sandwich, so no reader loses the thread.
- **A parenthetical can make an axiom obvious in passing.** Elsewhere Muñoz restates Pareto in homely terms — "(Again: A2 is better for Alex, worse for nobody.)" — so the formal principle needs no separate defense; the plain-language gloss in parentheses *is* the proof that it is undeniable.

## What to borrow
When several authors press the same objection in different vocabularies, name the shared core with one sustained metaphor, list the variants tersely inside it, and bookend with the upshot — then, when you invoke a formal axiom, drop a parenthetical that restates it in plain words ("(better for one, worse for none)") so the reader grants it without argument.
