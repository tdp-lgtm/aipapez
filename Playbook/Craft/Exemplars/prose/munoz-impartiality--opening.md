# Annotated exemplar — Muñoz, opening of "Impartiality, Anonymity, and Caring Who"

## Source

- **Author:** Daniel Muñoz
- **Work:** "Impartiality, Anonymity, and Caring Who," *Free & Equal: A Journal of
  Ethics and Public Affairs* (2025).
- **Why this passage:** a model "open plain, then turn" introduction that motivates
  a sharp puzzle without any throat-clearing, names the target principle, and
  states the thesis flat — all in the first page.

## The passage

> Most of us, most of the time, are partial: we care more about some people just
> because of who they are. But most ethicists think we should sometimes be
> impartial. […] "Each counts for one," as the slogan goes.
>
> I have no objection to impartiality in itself. But I believe it has become a
> Trojan horse for classical utilitarians.
>
> […] But is Outcome Anonymity truly a requirement of impartial concern?
> I argue that it is not.

## What makes it work (the lesson)

- **Plain opening claim, then a "but" turn.** The first sentence is almost
  conversational and commands assent; the "But…" immediately creates the tension
  the paper will resolve. No "much has been written about impartiality."
- **One vivid metaphor, used once.** "A Trojan horse for classical utilitarians"
  fixes the stakes in a single image and is not over-extended.
- **The target is named and displayed** (*Outcome Anonymity*) so it can be a
  recurring referent — clarity + citability (see `journal-craft.md` §2, §5).
- **Rhetorical question as a pivot, answered on the next line.** "But is [it]
  truly…? I argue that it is not." The thesis is stated flat, unhedged.
- **Stakes shown, not asserted.** The bite comes from the surprising claim that a
  "minimal" impartiality principle smuggles in utilitarianism — not from "this is
  important."

## What I want to borrow

When opening a paper, write a plain claim the reader already accepts, turn on a
"but" into the tension, name the view I'm targeting, and state my thesis in one
flat declarative — within the first page, before any apparatus.
