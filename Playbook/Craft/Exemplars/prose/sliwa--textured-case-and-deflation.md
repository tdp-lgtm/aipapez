# Annotated exemplar — Sliwa, "Changing Minds and Hearts" (textured case; homely deflation)

## Source
- **Author / Work:** Paulina Sliwa, *Changing Minds and Hearts*, Oxford Studies
  in Metaethics vol. 20, 2025 · sliwa2025changing (verify against the source before citing)
- **Why this passage:** her sharpest divergence from the minimal-pair norm —
  socially textured cases whose dialogue IS the philosophy — plus the
  homely-analogy deflation of a rival.

## The passages
> "I'll tell you what just happened", her friend says, "your colleague threw a
> tantrum. How mortifying for him—no wonder things got awkward. At least he
> didn't throw himself on the floor!"

> After all, if you have both a tooth- and a backache and a medicine effectively
> relieves you of the toothache, it's still a pretty good medicine!

## What makes it work (the lesson)
- **The case's punchline is the thesis.** The friend's reframe ("threw a
  tantrum") *is* the perspective-shift the paper theorizes — the reader
  experiences hermeneutical advice before it's defined.
- **Dialogue carries philosophy** without dropping precision; humour targets the
  situation, never the person.
- **Homely deflation**: a rival's inference (partial success = failure) dies in
  one domestic analogy — no apparatus, one stroke.
- **The discipline underneath:** every vivid detail is normatively load-bearing.
  Imitators who add colour without function produce padding — texture must earn
  its keep.

## What to borrow
When a case involves social perception, let a character's own words deliver the
philosophical move; and keep one homely analogy in reserve for deflating an
opponent's over-reaching inference.
