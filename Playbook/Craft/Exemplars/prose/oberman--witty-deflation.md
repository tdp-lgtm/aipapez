# Annotated exemplar — Oberman, "Killing and Rescuing: Why Necessity Must Be Rethought" (witty deflation)

## Source
- **Author / Work:** Kieran Oberman, "Killing and Rescuing: Why Necessity Must Be Rethought," *Philosophical Review* 129, no. 3 (2020): 433–[end] · suggested cite key: `oberman2020killing` (verify against the source before citing)
- **Why this passage:** Oberman dispatches an over-clever defense (that one could prevent serious wrongdoing by pre-empting the attacker) with one deflating line rather than a paragraph of refutation.

## The passage
> To prevent serious wrongdoing, one would have to identify and kill Attacker before she has even decided to kill. That is an idea for dystopian science fiction, not the real world.

## What makes it work (the lesson)
- **Carry the move to its absurd terminus, then name it.** Oberman first spells out what the opponent's view *requires* (killing before the decision to kill) — the wit lands only because the literal consequence is already on the table.
- **A vivid label does the refuting.** "An idea for dystopian science fiction" classifies the position in four words; the reader laughs and agrees in the same beat. (A footnote even pins it: "Steven Spielberg's *Minority Report* to be precise.")
- **The "X, not Y" frame snaps it shut.** "…dystopian science fiction, not the real world" is a clean antithesis — the deflation and the verdict arrive together, no follow-up needed.
- **Deploy sparingly, after the serious work.** Oberman has already granted the opponent's premise "for the sake of argument"; the joke isn't a dodge, it's the exit door once the argument is exhausted. Earned wit, not glib wit.

## What to borrow
When an objection only survives by demanding something preposterous, state the preposterous thing plainly, then deflate it with one vivid label in an "X, not Y" frame — and only after you've taken the view seriously, so the line reads as a verdict, not a sneer.
