# Annotated exemplar — Muñoz, "Each Counts for One" (visualize formal/technical material)

## Source
- **Author / Work:** Daniel Muñoz, "Each Counts for One," *Philosophical Studies* 2024
- **Why this passage:** Introducing the social-choice axiom of Anonymity, Muñoz promises the reader a picture before he gives the abstraction — making a formal definition readable.

## The passage
> There is a nice way to visualize this axiom. Suppose you list out the voters alongside their preference orderings, as we did above. If our voting rule is anonymous, then swapping the names of two voters—while leaving everything else the same—never affects the output of the rule.

## What makes it work (the lesson)
- **Promise a picture, then deliver one.** "There is a nice way to visualize this axiom" tells the reader help is coming, so they relax into the formalism instead of bracing against it. The promise is a small kindness that buys patience.
- **Concrete operation, not restated formula.** He doesn't re-paraphrase "Anonymity"; he gives a *thing you can do* — swap two names on a list — and names the result. The axiom becomes a gesture you can perform in your head.
- **Reuse the running example.** "as we did above" anchors the new idea to the Al/Bob/Cait list already in working memory; no new apparatus to load.
- **The warm register ("nice") lowers the stakes.** A single evaluative word signals the technical bit is friendly, not a wall — and Muñoz repeats the move ("Let me mention a nice way to visualize Neutrality"), training the reader to expect a payoff each time.

## What to borrow
Before any axiom, lemma, or formal definition, write one sentence promising a concrete picture ("Here's a way to see this…"), then give an operation the reader can perform on an example already on the page — don't just restate the symbols in words.
