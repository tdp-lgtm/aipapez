# Annotated exemplar — naming views & the explicit roadmap (Clark; "As If")

## Source

- **Works:** David J. Clark, "Refusing Protection," *Philosophy & Public Affairs*
  (2023); and "'As If': Reasonable Mistakes and Defensive Liability" (the prose
  corpus's defensive-ethics piece).
- **Why this passage:** shows the field-wide conventions of *naming* views in
  small caps and ending the intro with an explicit section roadmap — not unique to
  any one author, so safely imitable.

## The passages

> Refusal sometimes has the power to render protection impermissible. Consider, for
> example: **Siblings:** […] I call this the LIABILITY CONTRACTION Account.
>
> The paper proceeds as follows. Section II sketches two general strategies […]
> Section III considers the most developed attempt […] Jonathan Parry's EXCLUSION
> Account. I argue that we should reject this account, and in Section IV I develop
> […] (Clark)

> Call this the VIOLATION Account. […] This theory has come to be known as the
> CULPABILITY Account […]. ("As If")

## What makes it work (the lesson)

- **One-line hook, then a named case.** Clark opens with a single declarative
  ("Refusal sometimes has the power…") and immediately gives the *Siblings* case —
  no preamble.
- **Views are named in small caps** (LIABILITY CONTRACTION, EXCLUSION, VIOLATION,
  CULPABILITY) so each is a stable, citable referent the dialectic can track.
- **Explicit roadmap.** "The paper proceeds as follows," then one sentence per
  section saying what it *does* ("I argue that we should reject…"). Heavy
  signposting is the norm, not a flaw (see `journal-craft.md` §7).
- **Engagement is asymmetric:** one rival (Parry's EXCLUSION Account) gets a whole
  section; everyone else is a footnote.

## What I want to borrow

Name my view and my main rival in small caps and reuse those names throughout.
Open with one declarative + a named case. End the intro with a "proceeds as
follows" paragraph that says what each section *does*, in verbs.
