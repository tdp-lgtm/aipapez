# Annotated exemplar — Lichter; "As If" (retreat to the safer claim)

## Source
- **Author / Works:** Andrew Lichter, *Wrongdoer-Centered Reasons for Blame*,
  Ethics 135(3), 2025 · lichter2025; and D. J. Clark (attr.), *"As If":
  Reasonable Mistakes and Defensive Liability* · clarkAsIf.
- **Why this passage:** scope discipline as a *move* — pre-emptively downgrading
  to the weaker claim the argument actually needs, so the strong version can't
  sink the paper.

## The passages
> this claim is, ultimately, stronger than necessary for this purpose. For even
> if we can never properly complain about being denied blame… (Lichter)

> My claim is just that in as much as one finds Quong's Ur-Idea intuitively
> compelling, one arguably has more reason to embrace Thomson's VIOLATION
> account on that basis than Quong's MORAL STATUS account. ("As If")

## What makes it work (the lesson)
- **The fallback is built in before the objection arrives.** Lichter flags that
  his claim is "stronger than necessary," then shows the conclusion survives on
  the weaker version — a referee who rejects the strong claim has been handed
  nothing.
- **"My claim is just that…"** re-anchors an ambitious-sounding argument to its
  precise, modest, defensible content — conditional and comparative rather than
  absolute.
- Both are the honest sibling of over-claiming: state the interesting version,
  *and* show which weaker version carries the paper.

## What to borrow
For every load-bearing claim, know (and say) which weaker version would still
deliver your conclusion: "this is stronger than I need — even if only [weaker
claim], it follows that…" and mark modest theses explicitly: "My claim is just
that…".
