# Annotated exemplar — Sliwa, "Reverse-Engineering Blame" (vignette opening; theory epigram)

## Source
- **Author / Work:** Paulina Sliwa, *Reverse-Engineering Blame*, Philosophical
  Perspectives 33 (Ethics), 2019 · sliwa2019blame (verify against the source before citing)
- **Why this passage:** the two poles of her register — an opening that drops the
  reader into ordinary life with comic timing, and a whole theory compressed into
  a parallel epigram.

## The passages
> Imagine your friend was supposed to meet you for a drink at 6. You arrive at
> the pub a few minutes early, settle down with a beer, and wait . . . and wait
> . . . and wait.

> Wrongdoing creates a normative footprint. Blame communicates it. Forgiveness
> erases it.

## What makes it work (the lesson)
- **Scene before literature.** The reader is inside the phenomenon (being stood
  up) before any theory appears; the ellipses *perform* the waiting — typography
  doing phenomenology.
- **The theory epigram**: three subject–verb–object beats, parallel and flat,
  carrying the entire account. Earned compression — it arrives *after* the
  machinery is built, as the payoff.
- The governing metaphor ("normative footprint") is coined once, then worked for
  the whole paper — one image, total consistency.

## What to borrow
Open a paper (or section) inside the experience the argument is about; and when
an account is fully built, compress it into a two-or-three-beat parallel epigram
the reader can carry away.
