# Annotated exemplar — Frowe, "The Limited Use View of the Duty to Save" (concede-the-conditional + negative specification)

## Source
- **Author / Work:** Helen Frowe, "The Limited Use View of the Duty to Save," in *Oxford Studies in Political Philosophy*, vol. 7, ed. David Sobel, Peter Vallentyne, and Steven Wall (Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2021), 66–94 · suggested cite key: `frowe2021limited` (verify against the source before citing)
- **Why this passage:** Two of Frowe's signature scope-control moves — granting an opponent's *conditional* (while denying its antecedent) and stating a claim by saying what it is *not*.

## The passage
> I grant the conditional claim of the Broad Scope View. If agent-relative prerogatives bear on our duties to rescue, then they should also bear on our duties not to harm. … To be clear: my claim is not that, provided that one is not making use of a person … one is permitted to cause harm in order to save oneself.

## What makes it work (the lesson)
- **Concede the conditional, contest the antecedent.** "I grant the conditional claim" hands the opponent the inference (if-prerogatives-then-harming) so Frowe can later attack the antecedent instead. She turns the opponent's own logic into a lever: the converse "should also hold."
- **The concession narrows the battlefield.** By granting what is true, she signals exactly where the real disagreement lives — no strawman, and a referee sees she has isolated the load-bearing premise.
- **"To be clear: my claim is not that…" fences off the misreading.** Negative specification pre-empts the over-strong reading a critic would otherwise pin on her, and does it in the author's own voice rather than waiting for the objection.
- **Negative then positive.** Saying what the claim is *not* sharpens what it *is* (the exception "arises only because useful harming is both reciprocal and unavoidable"). The denial does precision work a flat assertion can't.

## What to borrow
When an opponent's argument has the form "if P then Q," grant the conditional out loud and fight over P; and when your thesis is easily over-read, add a one-line "To be clear: my claim is not that…" to fence off the strong misreading before a referee supplies it.
