# Annotated exemplar — Tadros, "Permissibility in a World of Wrongdoing" (reply-layering)

## Source
- **Author / Work:** Victor Tadros, *Permissibility in a World of Wrongdoing*,
  Philosophy & Public Affairs, 2016 · tadros2016 (verify against the source before citing)
- **Why this passage:** the dialogue engine for deep objection sections — replies
  stacked on responses, each layer explicitly announced, so the reader never loses
  their place in the exchange.

## The passage
> Here is a reply to the response. Suppose that X is not required to rescue V and
> does not do so. X would have saved V if Y had done what he was required to do.

*(The full sequence runs: "Here is a reply…" → "Here is a response…" → "Here is a
reply to the response…", each layer opened by that formula.)*

## What makes it work (the lesson)
- **The exchange is labelled, not implied.** Each dialectical layer gets a flat
  announcement of its role, so three levels of back-and-forth stay legible —
  no "but one might counter that the counter-counter…" contortions.
- **Each layer is short and case-anchored**: a fresh stipulated scenario carries
  the reply, rather than abstract restatement.
- The device lets a paper go *deep* on one objection instead of *wide* over many
  shallow ones — usually the stronger choice at top venues (cf. journal-craft §3).

## What to borrow
When an objection deserves multiple rounds, run the exchange explicitly: "Here is
a reply. … Here is a response. … Here is a reply to the response." — one case per
layer, and stop one layer after your strongest ground.
