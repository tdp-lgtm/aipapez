# Annotated exemplar — Estlund, "What's Unjust about Structural Injustice?" (named dilemma as the paper's structural engine)

## Source
- **Author / Work:** David Estlund, "What's Unjust about Structural Injustice?," *Ethics* 134, no. 3 (April 2024): 333–ff. · suggested cite key `estlund2024whats` (verify against the source before citing)
- **Why this passage:** A single coined dilemma — the *reach/grievance dilemma* — is announced as the spine the whole argument "revolves around," with each horn named so it can be reused as a fixed referent.

## The passage
> My argument will revolve around a core dilemma about structural injustice, what I'll call the reach/grievance dilemma: If for there to be injustice there must necessarily be individual culprits, then the idea of injustice can't reach cases we might think it must.

## What makes it work (the lesson)
- **One dilemma is declared the engine, up front.** "My argument will revolve around" tells the referee the paper has a single spine; everything that follows is the working-out of two horns, not a list of loosely related observations.
- **Both horns get names you can reuse.** The two sides become "the reach challenge" and "the grievance challenge" (and the rival views "deontic" vs. "telic") — fixed handles that let later sections refer back without re-explaining.
- **The aim is to sharpen, not to decide.** The abstract says he will "press the dilemma" and "critically consider possible ways out of it." Refusing to resolve is licensed by showing the dilemma bites a "wider swath of views" than the target idea — the contribution is the *pressure*, not a verdict.
- **The horns are stated as matched conditionals (If X… / If not-X…).** Symmetric phrasing makes the trap visible: both escape routes cost something.

## What to borrow
If your paper's real contribution is a problem rather than a fix, coin and name the dilemma early, name each horn so it recurs as a referent, and earn the right not to resolve it by showing the trap is deeper or wider than anyone noticed (cf. `Craft/structure-casebook.md` "Name the view, the rival, and the key principle"; `Craft/journal-craft.md` on naming as a handle for the dialectic).
