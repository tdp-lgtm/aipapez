# Annotated exemplar — Parry, "Why Paternalism Is Wrong When It Is Wrong" (argue-by-elimination + explicit four-stage roadmap)

## Source
- **Author / Work:** Jonathan Parry, "Why Paternalism Is Wrong When It Is Wrong" (forthcoming 2026) · suggested cite key `parry2026paternalism` (verify against the source before citing)
- **Why this passage:** A flat four-stage roadmap that derives the positive view by elimination — defeat the rival families first, then build the residual view and ground it.

## The passage
> The paper proceeds in four stages. I begin by motivating the idea that the distinctive wrong of paternalism inheres in the paternalist's beneficent end… First, I explain the limitations of two rival accounts, which locate the wrong in violations of autonomy rights or in imputations of incompetence, respectively… I argue that these views struggle to explain certain core judgments for precisely this reason.

## What makes it work (the lesson)
- **"Proceeds in four stages," then four matching paragraphs.** The roadmap is a literal contract: one stage = one paragraph = one section, each opened with a positional cue (begin / I then set out / Third / I conclude). A referee can hold the paper to it.
- **The positive view is the residue of elimination.** The rivals are sorted into named *families* ("autonomy rights" vs. "imputations of incompetence"), each defeated, so the author's end-based view arrives as what is left standing rather than as a bare proposal.
- **The defeat has a single, repeated diagnosis.** Both rivals fail "for precisely this reason" — they locate the wrong somewhere other than the beneficent end — which both motivates the survivor and unifies the demolition.
- **Matched minimal-pair cases do the eliminating.** Later sections pit cases that vary one feature (*Public Health 1 / Public Health 2*) to isolate exactly what each rival cannot explain — the elimination is run on cases, not assertions.

## What to borrow
Open with a numbered roadmap whose stages map one-to-one onto sections; then reach your view by sorting rivals into a few named families, defeating each with matched minimal-pair cases that share one diagnosis, and presenting your account as the residue (cf. `Craft/structure-casebook.md` "Build by serial defeat of named candidates" and "Minimal-pair cases, named and reused").
