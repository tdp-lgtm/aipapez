# Annotated exemplar — Frowe (stipulation blocks; reader-modularity)

## Source
- **Author / Work:** Helen Frowe, *The Comparative Claims of Victims* — working
  draft (froweVictimsDraft — **unpublished; private repo only, never cite**).
- **Why this passage:** two argument-hygiene devices that immunize a paper
  against referee quibbles about background machinery.

## The passages
> Assume, for now, that since Rescuer can prevent serious harm to others whilst
> incurring only a small risk of a mild harm, Rescuer has at least a pro tanto
> duty to rescue. Assume further that, ordinarily, rescuers confronted by
> multiple victims facing equal threats ought to offer each victim an equal
> [chance of rescue]

> Readers may thus substitute their preferred account of liability in the
> discussion. This might produce different verdicts in particular cases, but
> will not undermine the general claim.

## What makes it work (the lesson)
- **Stipulation blocks**: assumptions declared explicitly, in sequence ("Assume,
  for now… Assume further…"), right after the case — then discharged. Nothing is
  smuggled; the referee can't complain about hidden premises.
- **Reader-modularity**: where the argument touches contested machinery (which
  account of liability?), the paper *detaches* its thesis from the choice — the
  reader plugs in their own view and the general claim survives. This converts a
  potential objection-front into a non-issue.
- Both moves shrink the paper's attack surface without weakening the thesis.

## What to borrow
After each load-bearing case, declare your assumptions in an explicit "Assume…
Assume further…" block; and wherever your argument brushes contested background
theory, add a modularity sentence: readers may substitute their preferred
account — verdicts may shift at the margins, the thesis stands.
