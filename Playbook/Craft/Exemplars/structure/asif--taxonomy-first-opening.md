# Annotated exemplar — "As If" (the taxonomy-first opening)

## Source
- **Author / Work:** D. J. Clark (attr.), *"As If": Reasonable Mistakes and
  Defensive Liability* · clarkAsIf (verify against the source before citing)
- **Why this passage:** locating a new view by first mapping the option space —
  the reader learns the terrain before being shown the unoccupied position.

## The passage
> The theory of liability to defensive harm has been a subject of great
> philosophical interest over the past few decades, and three accounts have
> emerged as most prominent. First, there is Judith Jarvis Thomson's claim that
> a person is liable to (necessary and proportionate) defensive harm just in
> case they pose a threat of violating someone else's rights.

*(Each account is then named in small caps — VIOLATION, CULPABILITY,
RESPONSIBILITY — and the paper's target, MORAL STATUS, enters as the fourth
position on the map.)*

## What makes it work (the lesson)
- **The map creates the vacancy.** Once the three prominent accounts are laid
  out and named, the new view's *location* — what it must differ from, what
  pressure it answers — is visible before any argument for it starts.
- **Naming the map's positions** (small caps) makes the whole dialectic
  navigable: every later move can say which account it targets in one word.
- **Contrast cases carve the taxonomy** (the accounts are separated by how they
  treat the same paired cases), so the map is argued, not merely asserted.
- Best for literatures with a small number of settled positions; in a sprawling
  literature, map only the two or three the contribution must displace
  (journal-craft §4).

## What to borrow
When your contribution is a new *position* (rather than an objection), open by
mapping and naming the existing options in a paragraph each — then introduce
your view as the determinate spot on the map that the others leave open.
