# Annotated exemplar — Frowe & Parry, "Wrongful Observation" (pre-emptive objection handling; thesis displayed and scoped with "can …" / sufficient-not-necessary)

## Source
- **Author / Work:** Helen Frowe and Jonathan Parry, "Wrongful Observation," *Philosophy & Public Affairs* 47, no. 1 (2019): 104–. · suggested cite key: `froweparry2019wrongful` (verify against the source before citing)
- **Why this passage:** Before building out the three positive grounds of liability, the authors pause to clear "three skeptical responses" — and the displayed thesis is deliberately modest in scope ("can render," not "always renders"; one ground among several).

## The passage
> The three grounds of observer-liability that we spell out … apply generally to cases involving observation of degrading wrongs … But before moving on, it is worth considering three skeptical responses to our claim that Observer incurs liability …
> The first holds that Observer's connection to the rape … is grounded in his failure to rescue Victim, and not his observation. … We grant that Observer wrongfully fails to rescue. However, this does not exhaust his wrongdoing …

## What makes it work (the lesson)
- **Rebut the deflationary reading first.** The most dangerous objection isn't "you're wrong" but "you've redescribed a familiar wrong" (here: it's just failure-to-rescue). Killing that off *before* the positive case means the reader can't dismiss the whole project as relabeling.
- **Objections are answered with cases, not assertions.** *Helpless Rape* and *Rescue Rape* are engineered so the rescue-duty is either impossible or fully discharged — yet the wrong remains. The minimal variants surgically detach observation-wrong from rescue-failure.
- **Scope is built into the thesis.** "Voluntary and unjustified observation … *can* render an agent morally liable" — the modal claims sufficiency under conditions, not necessity, pre-empting easy counterexamples and lowering the burden of proof.
- **The skeptical block is staged.** First objection (it's really failure-to-rescue), then the next (only proximate observers), each with its own pair of cases — a visible queue of worries dispatched in order.

## What to borrow
Before you build your positive case, name and dispatch the two or three deflationary readings a referee will reach for — answer each with a purpose-built minimal-variant case — and phrase your thesis with an explicit "can"/sufficient-not-necessary scope so you are not defending more than you need.
