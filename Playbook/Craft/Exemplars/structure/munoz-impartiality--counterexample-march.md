# Annotated exemplar — Muñoz, "Impartiality, Anonymity, and Caring Who" (the escalating counterexample march)

## Source
- **Author / Work:** Daniel Muñoz, *Impartiality, Anonymity, and Caring Who*,
  Free & Equal 1(2), 2025 · munoz2025impartiality (verify against the source before citing)
- **Why this passage:** the via-negativa engine for a whole paper: produce a
  counterexample, let the target weaken itself, break the weakened version,
  repeat — until only the author's minimal principle survives.

## The passage
> Could there be an impartial rule that violates Outcome Anonymity? As it
> happens, there is one right under our noses: Majority Rule, the principle for
> which Anonymity was invented.

*(The march is announced in advance: "let's now turn to some potential
counterexamples, starting with the first kind of case…")*

## What makes it work (the lesson)
- **The march is typed and announced.** Counterexamples come in *kinds* (cases
  where permutations make things better; cases where they create
  incomparability), each kind previewed before its section — so a long negative
  campaign reads as a plan, not a pile.
- **Each counterexample targets the *current* version** of the rival principle;
  after each hit, the strongest available weakening is constructed *for* the
  opponent, then broken in turn. The rival dies by exhaustion of its best forms,
  not by one contested intuition.
- **The opening counterexample is chosen for irony** — Majority Rule, "the
  principle for which Anonymity was invented" — which makes the first hit
  memorable and fair at once.

## What to borrow
For a section (or paper) whose job is destroying a general principle: classify
your counterexamples into kinds, announce the plan, and after each blow rebuild
the opponent's best weakened version yourself before breaking it — the
conclusion then arrives as the only survivor.
