# Annotated exemplar — Tomlin, "Innocence Lost: A Problem for Punishment as Duty" (the dialectic itself as the contribution)

## Source
- **Author / Work:** Patrick Tomlin, "Innocence Lost: A Problem for Punishment as Duty" (2017) · suggested cite key `tomlin2017innocence` (verify against the source before citing)
- **Why this passage:** Tomlin runs the paper as a chain of "what could the defender say?" moves — proposing rescues on the opponent's behalf, testing each, and conceding which survives — so the dialectical canvass *is* the contribution.

## The passage
> What could the adherent of the Duty View say in order to differentiate between Sarah and Ruth's punishments? Here is one thing they could say: Sarah chose to commit her crime… However, we should observe at the outset that without further argument this is simply a descriptive difference… it is not yet an evaluative moral difference…

## What makes it work (the lesson)
- **The author argues the opponent's side first.** He poses the rescue *for* the Duty View ("Here is one thing they could say"), which steel-mans the position and makes the eventual defeat feel earned rather than rigged.
- **Each move escalates.** Defeating one reply prompts the next "could they instead say…?", so the section reads as a live dialectic that closes off escape routes one at a time, not a static list of objections.
- **The defeat is diagnosed, not just asserted.** The chosen failure mode — a "descriptive difference" masquerading as an "evaluative moral difference" — is a reusable distinction the reader can carry to the next move.
- **He concedes a winner.** The abstract promises to "canvass several ways to respond… arguing that a rights… forfeiture theory… is the best solution": the paper grades the rescues and grants the strongest, so the contribution is the *map of replies plus a ranking*, not a single knockdown.

## What to borrow
When your real contribution is mapping a problem's dialectical space, build the section as escalating "could the defender say X?" rescues you raise on the opponent's behalf, defeat each with a named distinction, and end by conceding which survives best (cf. `Craft/structure-casebook.md` §3 dialectic; "steel-man then defeat").
