# Annotated exemplar — Oberman, "The Myth of the Optional War" (gap-framing opening: "Much has been written… Much less…" + a named, displayed governing principle)

## Source
- **Author / Work:** Kieran Oberman, "The Myth of the Optional War: Why States Are Required to Wage the Wars They Are Permitted to Wage," *Philosophy & Public Affairs* 43, no. 4 (2015): 255–. · suggested cite key: `oberman2015myth` (verify against the source before citing)
- **Why this passage:** The first two sentences locate a precise gap in an established literature, and later the governing principle is named and stated in display form (the "Cost Principle").

## The passage
> Within just war theory, much has been written on the question of when states are morally permitted to wage war. Much less is written on the question of when states are morally required to wage war.
> … The principle I propose is this: those in power should not force people to render humanitarian assistance to those in dire need if the costs exceed that which people have a humanitarian obligation to bear. Let us call this the "Cost Principle."

## What makes it work (the lesson)
- **The gap is defined by contrast, in parallel syntax.** "Much has been written on … permitted. Much less … on … required." The two clauses are near-identical but for one word (*permitted* / *required*), so the unfilled space is unmistakable and the paper's target is the difference. The opening *is* the motivation.
- **The gap is real, not strawed.** Oberman immediately credits "the predominant view" (the middle category of optional wars) with a footnote of names, so the gap-claim reads as a genuine omission in a live debate, not a convenient absence.
- **The governing principle is named and displayed.** "Let us call this the 'Cost Principle'" gives the engine of the paper a proper noun the reader can carry through every later section ("Recall that …", "the implications of the Cost Principle for …").
- **Name after stating.** The principle is fully stated, *then* christened — so the label attaches to content the reader already grasps, not a black box.

## What to borrow
Open by naming what the field has done and setting it against the precise question it has skipped, using parallel "Much has been written … Much less …" syntax that varies one term; then give your central principle a proper name in display form so it can be invoked by that name for the rest of the paper.
