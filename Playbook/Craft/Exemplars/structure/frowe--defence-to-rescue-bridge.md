# Annotated exemplar — Frowe (the defence-to-rescue bridge)

## Source
- **Author / Work:** Helen Frowe, *The Comparative Claims of Victims* — working
  draft (froweVictimsDraft — **unpublished; private repo only, never cite**).
  The same engine runs through her published defensive-ethics work.
- **Why this passage:** her signature inferential engine — deriving rescue/aid
  verdicts from defensive-harm permissions, where intuitions are firmer.

## The passage
> we can support this view by thinking about what Rescuer would be permitted to
> do in defence against someone culpably threatening to impose those costs upon
> her

## What makes it work (the lesson)
- **Argue from the firmer domain to the softer one.** Intuitions about defensive
  permissions are comparatively stable and well-theorized; rescue/aid intuitions
  are murkier. The bridge transfers credibility: if X may be *harmed* to prevent
  a cost, then a fortiori X may be *allowed to bear* it.
- **The bridge is stated as a method**, not smuggled — the reader is told exactly
  which better-understood question is standing proxy for the disputed one.
- Generalizes beyond her subfield: whenever two normative domains connect, argue
  from the one where your reader's judgments are already firm (punishment ↔
  defensive liability is the obvious analogue for this repo's author).

## What to borrow
When a claim in a soft domain (rescue, aid, punishment) is contested, look for
the corresponding claim in a firm domain (defensive harm) and build the explicit
a-fortiori bridge — announcing the method as you use it.
