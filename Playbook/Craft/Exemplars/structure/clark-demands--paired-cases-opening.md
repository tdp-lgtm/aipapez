# Annotated exemplar — Clark, "The Demands of Necessity" (paired minimal cases as a "Compare:" diptych that frames before any thesis)

## Source
- **Author / Work:** David James Clark, "The Demands of Necessity," *Ethics* 133, no. 4 (July 2023): 473–. · suggested cite key: `clark2023demands` (verify against the source before citing)
- **Why this passage:** The opening word after the topic sentence is "Compare:", and a matched pair of one-paragraph cases does the conceptual work — isolating the relevant variable — before the article's thesis is argued.

## The passage
> Moral philosophers have long recognized a proportionality constraint on defensive harm. Compare:
> *Fast and Furious.* … Pedestrian can let the car hit her, in which case she will be permanently paralyzed … Or she can redirect the car away from her, in which case Reckless will be permanently paralyzed.
> *Fast and Less Furious.* As before, except the threat to Pedestrian is much smaller. … she will only suffer a small bruise.

## What makes it work (the lesson)
- **The diptych isolates one variable.** "As before, except …" holds everything fixed but the severity of the threat, so the reader's diverging intuitions (permitted vs. not) can only be tracking that one difference. The cases *do the argument's framing* — no thesis needed yet.
- **"Compare:" is a one-word invitation to co-reason.** It signals a controlled experiment and recruits the reader as a collaborator rather than an audience for a claim.
- **Concrete before abstract.** The named constraint ("proportionality") is *demonstrated* by the pair, then named, not asserted and then illustrated. Intuition is elicited before the label is applied.
- **Minimal furniture.** Names ("Reckless," "Pedestrian"), one mechanism (redirect the car), two outcomes. Nothing in the vignette is decorative.

## What to borrow
Open a paper by eliciting the target intuition with a tightly matched pair of cases — identical but for the one feature you claim is doing the moral work — and let "Compare:" plus "As before, except …" carry the framing before you state your thesis.
