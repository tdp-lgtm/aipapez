# Annotated exemplar — Sliwa, "Reverse-Engineering Blame" (the reverse-engineering arc)

## Source
- **Author / Work:** Paulina Sliwa, *Reverse-Engineering Blame*, Philosophical
  Perspectives 33 (Ethics), 2019, 200–219 · sliwa2019blame (verify against the source before citing)
- **Why this passage:** a genuinely different way to BUILD a positive view —
  constructive hypothesis-testing rather than counterexample duelling or
  elimination of rivals.

## The passages
> Clarifying the hypothesis — Before we test the hypothesis, let me clarify it.

> Putting the hypothesis to work — To test the hypothesis, we need to see whether
> it allows us to reverse-engineer something very much like our blaming practice

*(Section-level architecture: the arc runs platitudes-of-the-practice → state the
hypothesis → clarify it → put it to work, feature by feature → rivals last.)*

## What makes it work (the lesson)
- **Constructive, not gladiatorial.** The view isn't the residue of defeated
  rivals; it's a hypothesis whose success is *shown* — if the hypothesis can
  regenerate the practice's known features (the platitudes), it earns belief.
- **The platitudes are the data.** Cataloguing what everyone already accepts
  about the practice up front gives the paper an agreed test-set — the referee
  signed off on the standards before seeing the theory.
- **"Clarify" gets its own stage**, separating what the hypothesis *says* from
  whether it's *true* — which pre-empts half the misreading-objections a
  positive view usually attracts.
- Rivals arrive *after* the constructive work, judged against the same
  platitudes — a fair fight the home theory has already trained for.

## What to borrow
For account-building papers: open by cataloguing the platitudes of the practice,
state your hypothesis, give it a clarification section of its own, then "put the
hypothesis to work" against each platitude in turn — and only then compare
rivals against the same test-set.
