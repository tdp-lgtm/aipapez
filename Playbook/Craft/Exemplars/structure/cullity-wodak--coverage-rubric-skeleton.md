# Annotated exemplar — Cullity & Wodak, "But Thinking Makes It So" (coined coverage-rubric as skeleton + objection checklist)

## Source
- **Author / Work:** Garrett Cullity & Daniel Wodak, "But Thinking Makes It So: How Bad Attitudes Wrong" (2025) · suggested cite key `cullityWodak2025thinking` (verify against the source before citing)
- **Why this passage:** A fixed set of four coined "Challenges" is set up as constraints on *any* solution, then reused both as the section skeleton and as the pass/fail checklist applied to every rival and to the authors' own view.

## The passage
> The constraints are given by the Action, Seriousness, Opacity, and Evidential Challenges. Meeting all of these requirements looks like a tall order, since they seem to pull in different directions. However, we will argue that there is a solution—the Constitutive View—that satisfies all four constraints.

## What makes it work (the lesson)
- **The rubric is coined once, then never re-derived.** Four capitalized "Challenges" (Action, Seriousness, Opacity, Evidential) become fixed referents; later sections just cite the name ("it faces the Action Challenge") instead of re-explaining the worry.
- **The same list does triple duty.** It is (i) the desiderata any answer must meet, (ii) the skeleton of the assessment sections, and (iii) the demolition tool turned on rivals — each competing view is scored against the four, and each fails one or more.
- **"They seem to pull in different directions" pre-sells the contribution.** Framing the desiderata as jointly hard to satisfy makes the positive view's "satisfies all four" a visible achievement rather than a bare claim.
- **The verdict is announced before the work.** Naming the winner (the Constitutive View) and its scorecard up front gives the referee a map and a promise to hold the paper to.

## What to borrow
When several worries cluster around one puzzle, coin a closed, named checklist of desiderata early; then structure rivals and your own view as line-by-line passes over it, so objections are discharged on a visible scorecard rather than ad hoc (cf. `Craft/structure-casebook.md` "A named coverage-rubric doubles as the skeleton" — Cullity & Wodak's four *Challenges*).
