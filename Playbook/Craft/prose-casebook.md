# Prose Casebook — moves by type, with verbatim examples

A deep example bank to sit beside `prose-principles.md`. Where that file gives the
rules, this one collects **actual moves from the corpus**, sorted by the job they
do, each with a short verbatim quote and a note on when to reuse it. Quotes are
kept short (fair-use).

> Mined from 36 published papers (heavy on Daniel Muñoz); adapted from the entrant's `acdemwrit`
> writing system. Add rows at Stage 12 as new readings surface moves. Standing rule: reuse the
> move, never the words.

---

## 1. Openings (earn attention in the first lines)

- **Deadpan transgression → reveal.** Muñoz & Baron-Schmitt: *"I took a bike
  belonging to someone in my neighborhood and rode it to work."* → *"this
  'neighbor' of mine was me."* Narrate something outrageous, then pull the rug.
- **Date the deadlock.** Muñoz, *Each Counts*: *"After 50 years of debate, the
  ethics of aggregation has reached a curious stalemate."* Dramatizes stakes for a
  stale literature.
- **Bland truism, set up to be overturned.** Muñoz, *Many/Few*: *"When it comes to
  the good things in life, more is usually better."* Deliberately flat, so the
  reversal lands.
- **Concede the ideal, indict its smuggled use.** Muñoz, *Impartiality*: *"I have
  no objection to impartiality in itself. But I believe it has become a Trojan
  horse."* Attack a principle without attacking what it claims to stand for.
- **Disarm the reader you might have alarmed.** Muñoz, *Exploitation*: *"Effective
  altruists: relax. You are not accused of exploiting anyone."* Defuse the obvious
  misreading up front.
- **Open cold on the case, theory after.** Parry: *"Consider the following case:
  Medicine:"*; Clark, *Mistaken Defense*: *"Suppose you need a liver transplant.
  You'll die without one."* Let the intuition land before any apparatus.
- **One short declarative to end an opening cascade.** Lazar: *"Most importantly,
  they kill."* Isolate the one fact the paper turns on.
- **Given/new gap.** Oberman: *"Much has been written… Much less is written on the
  question of when states are morally required."* Manufacture the gap you'll fill.
- **Open from common ground, then drill.** Graham: *"I start with the following
  platitude."* Then problematize it.
- **Fragment-questions for a surprising claim.** Estlund: *"Sin? In systems,
  structures, and situations themselves?"*
- **A vivid property, stated with relish.** Isserow: *"Pride is impressively
  promiscuous; we can extract it from just about anything."*
- **Tricolon of plain adjectives on shared experience.** Sliwa: *"…moral situations
  that are unsettling, confusing, disorienting."*
- **Big vivid claim to give a dry topic stakes.** Muñoz, *Grounding Nonexistence*:
  *"We are the only creatures who think and talk with 'not'… as distinctively
  human as walking upright."*
- **A charged epigraph that compresses the tension.** Srinivasan: *"Be angry, but
  sin not."*

## 2. Stating the thesis (the turn)

- **Question, then flat answer.** Muñoz, *Impartiality*: *"But is Outcome Anonymity
  truly a requirement of impartial concern? I argue that it is not."*
- **Short pivot question after setup.** Muñoz, *Each Counts*: *"Clearly, at least
  one of these arguments must fail. But which?"*
- **Concede appeal, then deny.** Frowe, *Assisting*: *"Both the popular view and
  Assister Obligations are intuitive. And yet… both are false."*
- **Antithesis that inverts the standard objection.** Lazar: *"it is not too
  restrictive; it is too permissive."*
- **State the radical claim bluntly mid-paper.** Tadros: *"intervening agency is
  morally irrelevant."*
- **The puzzle as a one-line paradox.** Parry: *"How can the moral valence of
  beneficence somehow flip?"*

## 3. Transitions & signposting

- **Workhorse case-chain transition.** Oberman: *"But now consider another case:"*
- **Terse scope-fencing.** Oberman: *"Before embarking, two clarifications."*
- **Self-grading signpost that ranks objections.** Muñoz, *FRT*: *"So much for the
  easy objection. Now the real challenges."*
- **Four-word hinge from over-reach to the real case.** Fritz: *"On the other hand,
  perhaps not."*
- **Grant the pull, then defuse (workhorse).** Fritz: *"But this reasoning is too
  quick"*; Frowe: *"But this is too fast."*
- **Mark the constructive turn explicitly.** Parry: *"More positively, what does
  distinguish these pairs of cases? The answer is straightforward enough:"*
- **Inline roadmap with section numbers.** Muñoz, *Supererogation*: *"We begin with
  the classic Paradox (§§2–3), then Horton's (§§4–5)…"*
- **Disarm the originality worry.** Cullity & Wodak: *"nothing we have said so far
  is novel. Far from it."*

## 4. Objection & reply

- **Em-dash flag that turns a granted move against the opponent.** Quong: *"But
  notice what this means—"* (it "stipulates the very point which is disputed").
- **Concede the bridge, run it backwards.** Frowe, *Limited Use*: *"I grant the
  conditional claim of the Broad Scope View."* Look fair while setting a trap.
- **Isolate the load-bearing assumption as a question.** *As If*: *"But why should
  the dependence relationship… determine liability, rather than the mistake
  itself?"*
- **Concede fully, then redirect.** Lichter: *"All of this is true, but it is also
  beside the point."*; Sliwa: *"The objection picks up on something that's right."*
- **Build the opponent's best reply yourself.** Muñoz, *Exploitation*: *"Now,
  Horton has a reply available."*
- **Flag a quick kill before a decisive counterexample.** Tadros: *"We can dismiss
  this view without further ado by considering:"*
- **Rhetorical question answered by one word.** Tomlin: *"What does this tell us
  about the limits to punishment? Nothing."*
- **Block a tempting fix by chaining its consequences.** Isserow: *"But this way
  lies trouble, for what is to stop us from saying…?"*

## 5. Concessions (steel-man, then hold the line)

- **Upgrade the opponent before answering.** Muñoz, *Duties to Oneself*: *"This is
  the best objection yet… There is a genuine tension."*
- **Credit, then turn.** *As If*: *"The MORAL STATUS account is fresh and ingenious.
  Nonetheless, I doubt it…"*
- **Concede totally, narrow precisely.** Muñoz, *Impartiality*: *"I concede that
  Majority Rule for ethics isn't plausible… But I'm not saying it's plausible, only
  that it's impartial."*
- **Concede the rival explanation, deny it exhausts the wrong.** Frowe & Parry:
  *"We grant that Observer wrongfully fails to rescue. However, this does not
  exhaust his wrongdoing."*
- **Concede before a bold claim.** Muñoz, *Each Counts*: *"Like many, I find his
  view extreme, bordering on incredible."*
- **One-word concession, then pivot to steelman.** *Price of Duty*: *"Perhaps. In
  this section, however, I want to take seriously…"*
- **Early concession that buys credibility.** Tadros: *"Orwell's objection to
  Brittain is not well defended."* (before the rescue).
- **Disclaim overreach to bank a modest result.** *As If*: *"I'm not claiming that
  we should endorse VIOLATION. My claim is just that…"*

## 6. Sentence cadence, fragments & wit

- **Three clipped clauses carry the move.** Muñoz: *"It is senseless. I should not
  do it. But it does not violate a right."*
- **Fragment pair as a rhythmic cap.** Muñoz, *Supererogation*: *"Lovely stuff;
  hardly required."*
- **Four-word flat reversal as a pivot.** Clark: *"It gets things backward."*;
  Frowe: *"But this assumption is mistaken."*
- **A single witty image for an impracticality.** Lazar: *"…laser-guided weaponry
  with guilt-seeking missiles."*; Oberman: *"That is an idea for dystopian science
  fiction, not the real world."*
- **A pun as a section pivot (once).** *Price of Duty*: *"The Promissory account
  isn't promising."*
- **Parenthetical wit that marks a limit.** Isserow: *"(Pride may be promiscuous,
  but it is not that promiscuous.)"*
- **A jarring one-word fragment for tonal contrast (sparingly).** Tadros, quoting
  Powers: *"Bullshit."*
- **A vivid figure to make a strange thesis stick.** Muñoz: *"something almost
  Lovecraftian about the idea that so many moral rights lurk just below the
  surface."*
- **A four-word cadence break after a long quotation.** Muñoz, *Each Counts*: *"We
  should have listened."*

## 7. Definitions, coinage & making formal material readable

- **Picture before formalism.** Muñoz: *"There is a nice way to visualize this
  axiom."* / *"an easy way to visualize…"* — always offer the matrix/diagram.
- **Name the formula ugly, then translate it.** Muñoz, *Impartiality*: *"While this
  is a mathematical mouthful, the idea behind 'Independence' is simple:"*
- **Compress a formal constraint into one plain word.** Muñoz: *"Each person in an
  outcome is just a 'somebody.'"*
- **Fence a term to a stipulated sense.** Quong: *"X uses Y, in the special sense I
  am interested in here, when…"*
- **Make the test portable as one memorable question.** Quong: *"if X and all the
  things currently belonging to X were suddenly to disappear, would your life be
  saved?"*
- **Verdict tag right after a derivation keeps formal results readable.** Hedden &
  Muñoz: *"—a paradoxical result."* / *"an intolerable betterness cycle!"*
- **Distill a paragraph into a slogan.** Muñoz: *"In a slogan: 'can get free'
  doesn't mean 'free already.'"*

## 8. Closings

- **Diagnosis → blunt prescription.** Howard: *"The most important way for
  democracies to respond to structural entrapment is, then, to stop perpetrating
  it."*
- **End on the value at stake, not a summary.** Lazar: *"each of us has only one
  life to live… we cannot be required by morality to allow ourselves to be
  killed."*
- **Collapse the thesis into a stakes statement.** Oberman: *"A war is either
  impermissible or required, and we had better get it right."*
- **Name the precise dialectical sin.** Muñoz, *Impartiality*: *"The trouble only
  starts when a utilitarian concoction is passed off as unadulterated common
  sense."*
- **Recontextualize the whole dispute.** Srinivasan: *"the debate about anger was
  never about them."*
- **Upbeat metaphor that gestures past the paper.** Muñoz, *Exploitation*:
  *"harmonies and counterpoints emerge from a cacophony of paradoxes."*
- **Self-exemplifying sign-off.** Muñoz, *Grounding Nonexistence*: *"No story is
  complete without 'The End'—but is 'The End' itself really part of the story?"*
  *(These two flourish moves are corpus catalog, not license: for this author,
  one earned final beat max — DIGEST §8 Closings.)*
- **Calibrated verdict, no overclaim.** Tomlin: *"I, for one, will find it
  difficult to endorse."*
- **One flagged slogan, only at the close.** Fritz: *"In a slogan: Drew falls short
  because people are entitled to more than just his help and his good will."*
