# Craft — the field-craft layer

How top analytic philosophy papers are built, distilled into rules this workspace's essays follow.
Files: `journal-craft.md` (structure conventions + red flags), `intro-playbook.md`,
`prose-principles.md`, `ai-tells.md`, `abstracts-and-titles.md`, and `moves-catalog.md` (the bank of
argument architectures and prose moves). The DIGEST's §10 table says when to load which.

## THE ANONYMITY RULE (canonical here)

**The playbook names no real authors and no real papers.** Craft knowledge in this repository is
kept as abstract, transferable rules — "state the thesis flat by page two," never "do it the way
[Author]'s [Paper] does." When a new lesson is worth keeping, write the rule down, anonymously; if
a lesson cannot be stated without its source's name or words, it is not transferable enough to
keep. No verbatim quotations from the craft corpus live in the playbook either.

Why: this repository (and each essay's methodology report) may be published with the entry; a
playbook built around named scholars would read as instructions to imitate particular people, would
put their names in a published artifact without their involvement, and adds nothing a well-stated
rule doesn't already carry.

Boundaries of the rule:

- It governs the **playbook and craft layer** (this folder, the DIGEST, the pipeline, the style
  guide, the agents and skills).
- **Essays are the opposite case:** they engage the real literature by name, with verified
  citations — that is a graded criterion. Literature Maps, `Background Readings/`, REQUESTS tables,
  referee reports, and citation audits inside `Essays/<slug>/` therefore name authors and papers
  freely; that is their job.
- Public competition facts (organizers, judge committee, rules) stay in `Competition/` as they are.
- At Stage 12, new moves go into `moves-catalog.md` in anonymous form, same standard.
