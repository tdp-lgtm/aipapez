# Structure Casebook — worked examples, paper by paper

A companion to `journal-craft.md`. Where that file abstracts the conventions, this
one shows **how each paper in the corpus instantiates them** — a worked example
you can imitate directly. Each entry maps to the journal-craft headings (§1
opening, §2 thesis/naming, §3 dialectic, §4 literature, §5 packaging, §7
signposting, §8 cases, §9 conclusion, §11 length). Prose-level moves live in
`prose-casebook.md`.

> Built by mining 36 published papers in top moral/political philosophy venues; adapted from the
> entrant's `acdemwrit` writing system. Add entries at Stage 12 when an essay's readings show a
> structure the casebook lacks. Standing rule: these are architectures to adapt, never text or
> content to reuse — and any paper mentioned here is cited in an essay only via a real, verified
> source.

## Cross-cutting patterns (what nearly every paper does)

- **Name the view, the rival, and the key principle** — usually in small caps or
  italics — and reuse the name as a fixed referent (LIABILITY CONTRACTION, the
  *Limited Use View*, *Observer Liability*, *Self-Other Symmetry*, the *Cost
  Principle*). Naming buys clarity, citability, and a handle for the dialectic.
- **Minimal-pair cases, named and reused.** The engine of almost every paper is a
  family of short named cases that vary one feature at a time (*Fast and Furious*
  / *Fast and Less Furious*; *Medicine 1/2/3*; *Driver/Phone*) and recur as
  referents throughout.
- **Concede the conditional / steel-man then defeat.** State the rival in its best
  form, grant what you can (often the opponent's own bridging premise), then turn
  it. Concession buys authority.
- **Build by serial defeat of named candidates.** Many positive views are reached
  by erecting and killing candidate principles in turn (Graham; Howard 2016;
  Fritz; "Price of Duty"), so the view arrives earned rather than asserted.
- **A named coverage-rubric doubles as the skeleton.** A coined checklist becomes
  the paper's spine and its objection-discharge list (Cullity & Wodak's four
  *Challenges*; Isserow's four *P's*; Sliwa's six perspective-components).
- **Concrete anchor first, theory second.** Open on a case, a sharp question, or a
  real event/testimony; defer the named positive view until rivals are cleared.

## I. Defensive ethics & self-defense

### Clark, "Refusing Protection" (*Philosophy & Public Affairs* 2023)
- **Opening (§1):** constructed case (*Siblings*) dropped in cold after one
  sentence; the puzzle ("refusal seems to render otherwise-permissible protection
  impermissible") is named within the first paragraph.
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** view christened the LIABILITY CONTRACTION Account
  (small caps), positioned explicitly between two named extremes.
- **Dialectic (§3):** rivals dispatched *before* the positive view — REFUSAL-AS-
  WAIVER then Parry's EXCLUSION get a dedicated demolition section with a
  three-part objection announced in advance.
- **Literature (§4):** asymmetric — Parry is the one close interlocutor; a dozen
  others are corralled into a single intro footnote.
- **Packaging (§5):** displayed bulleted "protections" (Deontic / Liability /
  Compensatory) make a fine-grained distinction visible.
- **Cases (§8):** heavily named and reused (*Siblings, Easy Defense, Miners,
  Slasher, Mistaken Attacker*); a *Hands 1/Hands 2* pair isolates group-size.

### Clark, "The Demands of Necessity" (2023)
- **Opening (§1):** paired minimal cases (*Fast and Furious* / *Fast and Less
  Furious*) presented as a "Compare:" diptych to extract the operative variable
  before any thesis.
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** in the abstract and restated §1 — proportionality and
  necessity "are two faces of a common moral demand"; later named.
- **Dialectic (§3):** two rival accounts (Discount, Rescue) each get a named
  subsection and are refuted before the positive theory; objections to his own
  view in dedicated late sections.
- **Packaging (§5):** displayed named principles in small caps; a schema
  formalizes the key move; a numbered triad table.
- **Cases (§8):** minimal, named, varied by one feature (*Easy/Easyish Defense*;
  *Defensive Boulder 1/2*).

### Clark, "Mistaken Defense and the Unbundling of Rights" (2025)
- **Opening (§1):** two vivid stakes-setting vignettes (steal a liver / human
  shield) before the paradigm case; reaches the contrast fast.
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** names the problem ("the Challenge of Merely Apparent
  Attackers"), the rival ("Orthodoxy"), and his view ("Partial Forfeiture");
  scope carefully hedged ("some, but only some").
- **Dialectic (§3):** a full section of four independently-labelled problems
  against the rival; then a section that explicitly "anticipates two worries"
  against his own view.
- **Packaging (§5):** numbered upshots (U1–U4) reused as a checklist; an
  explanatory-arrow diagram (Responsibility → Fair distribution → Liability).
- **Conclusion (§9):** extends the "unbundling" idea outward to promissory
  morality as a teaser for future payoff.

### "'As If': Reasonable Mistakes and Defensive Liability"
- **Opening (§1):** no case first — a taxonomy of three reigning accounts
  (VIOLATION, CULPABILITY, RESPONSIBILITY) built via a paired-case contrast
  (*Driver/Phone*) to locate the new view as a fourth option.
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** target view named MORAL STATUS, its motivating
  intuition labelled "Quong's Ur-Idea" (a coinage that becomes the pivot); thesis
  is deflationary.
- **Dialectic (§3):** three numbered worries, each driven by a fresh named case;
  constructive disambiguation rather than mere refutation.
- **Packaging (§5):** account stated as a labelled biconditional with conditions
  (i)–(iii); a long methodological footnote pre-empts an escape route.
- **Cases (§8):** comparative pairs differing in exactly one feature (*Waiting on
  Trolley 1/2*), engineered to isolate the disputed variable.

### Quong, "Killing in Self-Defense" (*Ethics* 2009)
- **Opening:** leads with the governing question and a taxonomy of target-types
  (Villainous/Innocent Aggressor, Innocent Threat, Bystander) stipulated up front
  for reuse.
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** two movements — a "purely negative" section
  dismantling the "Moral Responsibility Argument," then a positive "Principle of
  Defensive Killing (PDK)" with lettered clauses.
- **Dialectic (§3):** the rival argument laid out as a numbered 6-step deduction,
  attacked at a single premise; his own principle iteratively repaired
  (*Infringing in Defense 1→4*) in full view.
- **Packaging (§5):** the signature device is the openly revised principle — each
  version exposed, broken by a case, amended; a counterfactual "test" stated as a
  quotable rule.
- **Cases (§8):** minimal, named, paired (*Man on the Track / Man on the
  Overpass*); an appendix fields two further objections.

### Lazar, "Responsibility, Risk, and Killing in Self-Defense" (*Ethics* 2009)
- **Opening (§1):** real-stakes framing (combatants "kill"), then a clean two-fork
  taxonomy of justification strategies; reaches the puzzle within a page.
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** lands late after a long, fair reconstruction of three
  named rival views; the move announced sharply — "it is not too restrictive; it
  is too permissive."
- **Dialectic (§3):** a declared three-stage critique with an explicit "internal
  objections" methodology — beat the opponent by their own standards.
- **Literature (§4):** deeply asymmetric — McMahan gets sustained close reading;
  others relegated to a footnote roster.
- **Packaging (§5):** heavy coinage of named arguments (Justice / Responsibility /
  Risk Argument), with an explicit note that he is renaming McMahan's terms.

## II. War, necessity, and rescue

### Oberman, "The Myth of the Optional War" (2015)
- **Opening:** asymmetry framing ("Much has been written… Much less is written")
  → coins "optional wars" and the flat counter-thesis "there are no optional
  wars."
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** thesis in paragraph two; the central tool is the named,
  displayed **Cost Principle**; the recurring foil is Walzer's "domestic analogy."
- **Dialectic (§3):** roadmap maps every section to a sub-argument; objections
  handled as a numbered march (choice / consent / political obligation).
- **Packaging (§5):** displayed numbered ideal-type cases (Perfectionism,
  Preference-Dependent Benefit, Superfluous Transfer) fence off an over-broad
  reading.
- **Conclusion (§9):** returns to the domestic analogy, then a memorable closer.

### Oberman, "Killing and Rescuing: Why Necessity Must Be Rethought" (2020)
- **Opening (§1):** a bare constructed case (*How to Rescue*) before any thesis;
  the puzzle ("the description problem") named within two pages.
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** three separable claims up front; coins **Comparative
  Approach**, **Standard/Revisionary View**, "goal-necessity"/"attacker-
  necessity," "semi-internalism."
- **Dialectic (§3):** each section = one named pro-Standard-View argument
  (Fairness, Wrongful Killing, Liability, Cost), steel-manned then defeated;
  objections decomposed into labelled premises.
- **Packaging (§5):** a displayed seven-step procedure plus a "useful shortcut";
  many minimal paired cases (*Medicine/Medicine 2*).
- **Conclusion (§9):** a four-item typology of his counterexamples, then a
  methodological self-justification.

### Frowe, "The Limited Use View of the Duty to Save" (2021)
- **Opening (§1):** no hook — states the view in sentence one, then a full
  section-by-section map (the chapter format licenses this).
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** the named **Limited Use View** (duty to treat oneself
  as a means); foil is the **Broad Scope View**.
- **Dialectic (§3):** grants the opponent's conditional first ("I grant the
  conditional claim"), turning concession into leverage; a declared two-pronged
  dilemma.
- **Literature (§4):** close, named combat with Quong and Lazar (their cases
  reconstructed and answered one by one).
- **Cases (§8):** a controlled family of minimal-variant named cases (*Self-Help /
  Rescue / Equal Canoe / Unequal Canoe*), each isolating one variable; toy
  numbers drive the dilemma.

### Frowe, "Assisting the Assisters" (2023)
- **Opening (§1):** real event (the 2021 Afghan withdrawal) documented through
  press/policy citations, then distills the "popular view" into a displayed
  principle, **Assister Obligations**.
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** contrarian ("both are intuitive. And yet… both are
  false"); tracks a revised **Assister Obligations\*** through the paper.
- **Dialectic (§3):** methodically tests each candidate ground (gratitude →
  promises → associative duties → causing harm), conceding what each *can* do
  before showing it fails.
- **Literature (§4):** unusually heavy on real-world/journalistic sourcing
  alongside the philosophy.
- **Cases (§8):** dense minimal-pair grid (*Busy Grenade / Grenade*; *Clever
  Trolley / Trolley*), each toggling one feature.

## III. Observation, liability, punishment, official duty

### Frowe & Parry, "Wrongful Observation" (2019)
- **Opening (§1):** real events (Steubenville, upskirting, revenge porn) tied to
  technology, then a single legal hook showing the principle is "instituted in
  law"; puzzle reached fast.
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** displayed and named (**Observer Liability**);
  explicitly scoped — conditions "sufficient, rather than necessary"; pluralist by
  design.
- **Dialectic (§3):** heavy preemptive defense — pre-rebuts three "skeptical
  responses" before building the positive case; a whole later section on two
  rejected candidates.
- **Cases (§8):** named and minimal (*Rape, Beheading, Revenge*), systematically
  varied by one feature (*Helpless/Rescue/Online/Historical*) to isolate distance,
  time, intention.

### Tadros, "Orwell's Battle with Brittain" (2014)
- **Opening:** real historical dispute staged as a quarrel; block-quotes Orwell,
  then concedes Orwell's argument is bad before salvaging its "spirit."
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** defends "vicarious liability," carefully scoped down
  ("liable to be harmed, not killed"), disclaiming terrorism up front to pre-empt
  the obvious horror.
- **Dialectic (§3):** relentless preemption; a section runs four lettered
  subsections each absorbing a distinct objection; concessions explicit and
  frequent.
- **Packaging (§5):** tort-law literature imported as analogy; named, minimal,
  paired cases.

### Tadros, "Permissibility in a World of Wrongdoing" (2016)
- **Opening:** a sharp abstract question, then a deliberate stacking of five
  real-world cases (UK aid to India, Gaza human shields, Gandhi) to show breadth
  before any theory.
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** counterintuitive reversal — wrongdoing often makes
  duties *more* stringent; views named as principles (Take Up the Slack, Fair
  Share I/II, Intervening Agency).
- **Dialectic (§3):** dialogue-style — "Here is a reply," "Here is a response,"
  "Here is a reply to the response," layering objection and counter.
- **Cases (§8):** named, minimal, varied across whole families (*Pond I/II/III*;
  *Trolley 2/3/4/5*; *Gandhi I–V*).

### Tomlin, "Innocence Lost: A Problem for Punishment as Duty" (2017)
- **Opening (§1):** a sharp taxonomy framing — the field's two traditional theories
  and their costs — reaching the puzzle within a page.
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** a critique-paper; targets Tadros's named "Duty View,"
  reconstructed as numbered DV1–DV5; coins its own machinery (Innocence Intuition,
  Strong/Moderate View).
- **Dialectic (§3):** escalating "could the defender say X?" moves; tests three
  rescues and rejects two before endorsing the third — the dialectic *is* the
  contribution.
- **Literature (§4):** near-monographic on one target (Tadros), with a footnote
  marshalling the target's own quotes to prove an inconsistency.

### "The Price of Duty" (official immunity; working draft)
- **Opening (§1):** two constructed paired cases (*Fire*, *Mistaken Imprisonment*)
  producing a shared intuition, then names the target phenomenon ("Official
  Immunity").
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** positive view named late ("the Price of Duty account")
  after a long via negativa; rivals all named (Promissory, Responsibility,
  Beneficial State, Agency on Loan).
- **Dialectic (§3):** a sustained elimination of competitors before the author's
  own view; each rival gets a clean statement, a virtue acknowledged, then
  targeted counterexamples.
- **Packaging (§5):** principles set off in display blocks; four named limits
  (incidental, careless, renegade, unreasonable harm).

## IV. Blame, responsibility, structural injustice, speech

### Graham, "A Sketch of a Theory of Moral Blameworthiness" (2014)
- **Opening (§1):** begins from a stated platitude, then immediately problematizes
  it ("This is all fairly uncontroversial. But what exactly is it…").
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** the view is built and named incrementally as
  principles; full thesis compressed into the abstract.
- **Dialectic (§3):** builds by *successive defeat of candidate principles* (Wrong
  Behavior → Subjective Wrong → Wrongdoing → Respect), each killed by a named
  counterexample.
- **Cases (§8):** all-caps named, minimal, paired to isolate one feature (*SUGAR*
  vs *UNWITTING DEFENDER* on the direction of ignorance).
- **Conclusion (§9):** numbered recap of three elements; frank cost-accounting ("a
  small price to pay").

### Lichter, "Wrongdoer-Centered Reasons for Blame" (*Ethics* 2025)
- **Opening (§1):** a sitcom case (*Modern Family*) and a constructed case
  (*Infidelity*) reaching the puzzle ("Why aren't you angry?") fast.
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** coins "wrongdoer-centered reasons" and "contribution
  recognition"; scoped with hedges and an explicit fallback to a safer claim.
- **Dialectic (§3):** a dedicated "Addressing Some Worries" subsection plus a
  preemptive self-weakening ("this claim is, ultimately, stronger than
  necessary").
- **Literature (§4):** sustained close engagement with Strawson (named "First/
  Second Insight"); most others in footnotes.

### Howard, "Moral Subversion and Structural Entrapment" (2016)
- **Opening (§1):** a real legal case (*Jacobson*) with a Supreme Court quotation;
  reaches the puzzle by paragraph two ("did not identify violators… it created
  them").
- **Thesis:** previews three points of difference from rivals up front; coins
  "subversion," "structural entrapment," "morally protected activity."
- **Literature (§4):** an explicit labelled menu of eight rival accounts (a)–(h),
  each dispatched briskly before the positive view.
- **Cases (§8):** named, minimal, contrastive (*Barry/Carl/Leila*); a deliberately
  conditionalized empirical "story" for the structural case.
- **Conclusion (§9):** a "both sides are right" reconciliation of a public dispute.

### Estlund, "What's Unjust about Structural Injustice?" (2024)
- **Opening (§1):** a real journalistic quotation ("racism without racists"), then
  the lurking puzzle as a one-line question ("how is there a wrong if no one
  commits it?").
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** the engine is a *named dilemma* — the "reach/grievance
  dilemma" — plus coined apparatus; scoped modestly ("not to decide… but to
  identify the dilemma").
- **Dialectic (§3):** a tour of escape routes (group agents, hybrid views, Rawls),
  each tested and found wanting.
- **Literature (§4):** close, fair reconstruction of opponents (Young, Haslanger,
  Scanlon, Anderson, Rawls), often quoting them to show where they "stop short."

### Howard, "Dangerous Speech" (2019)
- **Opening (§1):** two real atrocities (San Bernardino, Christchurch) with dates,
  then the policy question and a sharp US-vs-UK framing ("Who is right?").
- **Thesis:** signposts the whole two-stage architecture (duty/right, then
  enforcement) in the intro; flags conclusions as "ambitious and modest."
- **Dialectic (§3):** audits four free-speech justifications in turn; imports the
  just-war "narrow vs. wide proportionality" and "necessity" distinctions as
  organizing machinery.
- **Cases (§8):** named, minimal, paired by one feature (*Atheist provocation*/
  Sonya vs *Atheist incitement*/Tonya); reused throughout.
- **Length (§11):** long, heavily footnoted; an "Interlude" section re-evaluates
  *Brandenburg*.

## V. Attitudes, emotions, perspective, inquiry

### Cullity & Wodak, "But Thinking Makes It So" (*PPR* 2025)
- **Opening:** two-claim setup quoting Scanlon, then a battery of paired minimal
  cases reaching the puzzle within a page.
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** positive "Constitutive View" named late, after a long
  diagnostic build; scoped hard ("can be," "modest claim").
- **Dialectic (§3):** objections converted into four named "Challenges" (Action,
  Seriousness, Opacity, Evidential), erected early then discharged one-by-one — a
  checklist architecture.
- **Cases (§8):** named, minimal, paired by one varied feature (*Race Bias /
  Gender Bias*; *Transparent / Opaque / Misleading*).
- **Conclusion:** a "Coda" flips to the mirror-image puzzle left open.

### Srinivasan, "The Aptness of Anger" (2018)
- **Opening (§1):** a real historical event (the 1965 Baldwin–Buckley debate)
  narrated at length, staging the "counterproductivity critique" before any
  thesis.
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** coins "affective injustice"; scoped via a strategic
  concession ("I want to grant the critic's empirical supposition") that sharpens
  the question.
- **Dialectic (§3):** a dedicated objection section, voiced in the critic's own
  first person, then answered via a coined taxonomy.
- **Signposting/Conclusion (§7/§9):** explicit "I proceed as follows"; the
  conclusion braids back to the opening for a political close.

### Fritz, "The Ethics of Putting Things into Perspective" (2025)
- **Opening:** mundane first-person anecdote (the dentist's office), generalized,
  then a *reductio*-style escalation before the turn ("On the other hand, perhaps
  not") into the *Town Hall* case.
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** coins "positive perspective-taking"; the positive
  proposal deferred; scoped as pluralist.
- **Dialectic (§3):** §2 is a sequence of candidate explanations entertained and
  found "incomplete" — an elimination structure that earns the positive view;
  a dedicated objections-and-replies block.
- **Cases (§8):** named and minimal (*Town Hall / Richard / Simone*),
  systematically modified to isolate one variable.

### Isserow, "Pride and Investment" (2025)
- **Opening (§1):** a playful self-referential anecdote that *enacts* the
  phenomenon ("I'm proud of myself for finally getting to… the opening
  paragraph").
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** four capitalized desiderata (Promiscuity,
  Positionality, Positivity, Pleasure) as a rubric; positive "Investment Account"
  displayed after rivals fall.
- **Dialectic (§3):** a methodological inversion (start from other-directed pride)
  plus rival-by-rival refutation; objections clustered in a dedicated section.
- **Packaging (§5):** alliterative section titles; coined parameters that yield a
  testable prediction; pop-culture cases as quick intuition pumps.

### Sliwa, "Making Sense of Things" (*PPR* 2024)
- **Opening (§1):** a real, extended testimony reproduced at length as the
  anchoring case, before any apparatus.
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** contrastive — "hermeneutical inquiry" defined against
  "verdictive inquiry" (her coinage).
- **Dialectic (§3):** a sustained foil in which the reader's objection ("it's
  really just concept-application") is voiced and answered with a contrast case.
- **Packaging (§5):** a displayed six-component definition of a "perspective,"
  worked through two perspectives in parallel.
- **Conclusion (§9):** a "promissory note" extension flagged as future work; closes
  on earned difficulty.

## VI. Muñoz — rights, prerogatives, supererogation, duties to self

### Muñoz & Baron-Schmitt, "Wronging Oneself" (*Journal of Philosophy*, forthcoming)
- **Opening (§1):** a constructed first-person "crime spree" narrated deadpan, then
  the twist reveal — reaches the puzzle in one paragraph.
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** view named **Self-Other Symmetry** ("reflexive
  rights"); landed early; scoped with "(normally)".
- **Dialectic (§3):** organized *as* the objections — silent consent, incompetence,
  ignorance, unwaivability — each a section taking a critic's best case and
  dissolving it.
- **Packaging (§5):** displayed named principles (Decision Principle, Unity);
  "minimal pair," "reflexive right" coined.
- **Cases (§8):** named/reused (the coffee, the arm-pinch, lavender allergy recur
  as a through-line).

### Muñoz, "From Rights to Prerogatives" (*PPR*, forthcoming)
- **Opening (§1):** sharp doctrinal framing (consequentialist trolley); the
  dilemma (unify restrictions + prerogatives, but no shared source) stated within a
  page.
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** a negative-then-positive arc — prerogatives need
  "waivable rights against oneself"; coins "finkish rights."
- **Dialectic (§3):** dedicated objections section, self-graded ("the easy
  objection… Now the real challenges"); concedes openly.
- **Packaging (§5):** a numbered 4-premise argument displayed and audited
  line-by-line; principle "Stability" displayed.
- **Conclusion (§9):** deliberately ends on an unresolved dilemma "with a glimmer
  of hope."

### Muñoz, "Three Paradoxes of Supererogation" (*Noûs*, forthcoming)
- **Opening (§1):** sharp question framing ("if it's so good, why isn't it just
  obligatory?"); three paradoxes posed in quick succession, then a promise to
  *unify*.
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** names competing principles; thesis = rightness is
  "multi-dimensional and comparative."
- **Dialectic (§3):** handles rivals by showing each fails to *generalize* to a
  combined case; explicit numbered objections.
- **Packaging (§5):** a recurring displayed four-option lattice; small numeric
  weight tables; "Transmission Over Ties" coined.

### Muñoz, "The Paradox of Duties to Oneself" (*AJP*, forthcoming)
- **Opening (§1):** real-phenomenon framing (servile suck-up; tragic suicide) →
  the puzzle, then Singer's three-premise paradox quoted and displayed.
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** frames the paradox as "a crossroads" with enumerated
  escape routes; targets premise 3.
- **Dialectic (§3):** survey-and-rank — four rival responses each scored by
  "margin of victory"; explicitly upgrades the best objection before answering.
- **Packaging (§5):** deontic-logic countermodels shown as world-diagrams; slogans
  set off.

## VII. Muñoz — aggregation & value theory

### Muñoz, "Each Counts for One" (*Philosophical Studies*, forthcoming)
- **Opening (§1):** sharp question, no warm-up; the puzzle (both sides claim
  equality) lands by the second paragraph.
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** thesis stated abstract + p.2; the diagnostic move is
  naming the conflated pair — **Anonymity** vs **Responsiveness** (from social
  choice theory).
- **Dialectic (§3):** a recurring template — reconstruct the opponent's argument as
  a numbered valid argument, then locate the *one* non-equality premise; objections
  to his own premises pushed into footnotes.
- **Packaging (§5):** displayed numbered arguments reused verbatim across sections
  (only premise 1 changes); formal axioms kept readable via worked Al/Bob/Cait
  tables.
- **Conclusion (§9):** recaps four arguments in one move, ends on a labelled
  "conjecture."

### Muñoz, "The Many, the Few, and the Nature of Value" (*Ergo*)
- **Opening (§1):** a rhetorical-question barrage about ordinary goods, then yanks
  to "something radically baffling" in Taurek; puzzle by paragraph two.
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** explicit "middle ground" thesis; coins **Transmission
  Over Ties**, **Taurek Lite**.
- **Dialectic (§3):** a literal "three objections" enumeration, strongest saved for
  last; explicit concessions granted to opponents.
- **Packaging (§5):** formal scores introduced only after prose intuition; the
  "nonsense argument" stated informally then made "precise."
- **Cases (§8):** minimal, named (Aaron/Alex/Betty), escalated (3-option →
  5-option) to defeat a loophole.

### Muñoz, "Impartiality, Anonymity, and Caring Who" (*Free & Equal* 2025)
- **Opening (§1):** real-pattern claim ("Most of us, most of the time, are
  partial"), then a diagnosis-as-thesis: Outcome Anonymity is a "Trojan horse for
  classical utilitarians."
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** thesis stated abstract + intro; the whole paper hangs
  on keeping two same-named principles apart (**Anonymity** vs **Outcome
  Anonymity**).
- **Dialectic (§3):** an escalating counterexample march — each section weakens the
  target principle, then breaks the weakened version; reviewer objections quoted
  and answered in-body.
- **Packaging (§5):** definitions given twice — formal statement plus an "easy way
  to visualize" via matrices; theorem in body, proof in an appendix.

### Hedden & Muñoz, "Dimensions of Value" (*Noûs*)
- **Opening (§1):** no case — a clean monist/pluralist contrast, then the "two
  fundamental questions" that organize the paper.
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** defends **Strong Pareto** and coins **Dimensionalism**;
  scope hedged ("first steps," "working hypothesis").
- **Dialectic (§3):** a "trio of counterexamples" (organic unities, golden means,
  tiny improvements), each given the same defusing template; concessions routine.
- **Packaging (§5):** value scores make balance-as-dimension vivid; a betterness
  cycle in a small option×dimension table; heavy material parked in footnotes.

## VIII. Muñoz (effective altruism, metaphysics-for-style) & Parry

### Muñoz, "Exploitation and Effective Altruism" (*PPE*, forthcoming)
- **Opening (§1):** direct address as hook ("Effective altruists: relax"), then
  twin paired puzzles via abstract-then-concrete contrast.
- **Thesis & naming (§2):** aims stated as a numbered two-part agenda; inherits the
